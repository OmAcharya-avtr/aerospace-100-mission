3. What training on transients buys the learned detector
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The same architecture, retrained with transient-spike streams added as
  negatives, recalibrated to the same ARL0, and re-measured. This is the
  one advantage a learned detector genuinely has over an analytic one:
  it can be told what not to fire on. The question is how much it helps.

  training set: 114152 windows x 8 features, 2800 positive (2.5 %), window=50, horizon=50, transients_in_training=True, 8 seeds
  Learned RF     threshold=0.68375  calibration ARL0=   508.1  held-out ARL0 = 519.5 +/- 16.5 samples (3.2 % rel. SEM, n=764 runs, 400000 samples, censored tail 0.77 %)  target error +3.9 %

  variant                      transient rate   excess   ARL1 on +1 sigma step
  stationary negatives only             94.4%    +81.2 pp                  15.5
  transients in training                90.8%    +77.6 pp                  10.8

  change in transient firing rate: -3.6 pp
  change in delay on a real +1 sigma step: -4.7 samples
  FINDING  training the learned detector on transient negatives changed its transient firing rate by -3.6 pp and its delay on a real +1 sigma mean step by -4.7 samples, both at the same recalibrated ARL0. This is the only capability in this package that an analytic detector does not have, and it is measured rather than claimed.
  PASS  the transient-trained variant was measured at the same ARL0  [target error +3.9 %]

  There is no detector in this package that ignores a transient. The
  windowed KS test fires least often, and that is not discrimination:
  it is un-armed for 65.9 % of a stationary stream (see
  validate_arl_calibration.py section 4), so it is often not watching.
  FINDING  ALL SIX DETECTORS FIRE ON THE +4 SIGMA TRANSIENT.

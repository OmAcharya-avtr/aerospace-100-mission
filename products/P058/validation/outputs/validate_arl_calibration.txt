3. Run-length distribution: is ARL0 enough to describe it
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  If a detector were memoryless its run lengths would be geometric and
  the coefficient of variation would be 1. None of these is memoryless.

  detector             ARL0   median      p90   CV   runs
  CUSUM               581.1    391.0   1335.8  1.02    679
  Page-Hinkley        485.7    427.0    843.0  0.54    815
  EWMA                527.1    374.0   1222.0  1.06    753
  Windowed KS         467.9    400.0    720.0  0.45    850
  ADWIN               520.3    385.0   1120.0  0.87    762

  A CV near 1 with a median well below the mean is a heavy right tail:
  most runs are short and a few are very long. Quoting ARL0 alone
  therefore says little about the worst case a reviewer will ask about.

5. The KS per-test level is not a stream-level false-alarm rate
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The asymptotic KS p-value controls one test on independent data. The
  detector runs a test every stride = 5 samples on windows overlapping by
  95 %, so the tests are strongly dependent. The ratio between the ARL0 an
  independence assumption predicts and the ARL0 measured is an effective
  independent-test factor.

  threshold c   per-test P(D>c)   predicted ARL0   measured ARL0   factor
  default     0.21198         0.005000           1000.0          7878.1      7.9x
  PASS  the independence assumption understates ARL0 at the default threshold  [7.9x]
  calibrated  0.13500         0.176079             28.4           470.7     16.6x
  PASS  the independence assumption understates ARL0 at the calibrated threshold  [16.6x]
  FINDING  treating the asymptotic KS p-value as a stream-level false-alarm rate understates the measured ARL0 by about an order of magnitude at both the default and the calibrated threshold, because overlapping windows make successive tests dependent.

6. ARL1 conventions: how much the choice changes the answer
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Three conventions for 'detection delay', all on the same seeded
  mean-step streams at the same calibrated thresholds:

  (a) steady-state  - detector runs through the pre-change segment and is
      reset on each false alarm. Nothing is excluded. USED EVERYWHERE
      ELSE IN THIS REPOSITORY.
  (b) conditioned   - replicates that false-alarm before the change are
      DISCARDED. This is what the first version of this package did.
  (c) zero-state    - the detector is started at the change index with no
      pre-change history. Matches the SPC tables.

  detector          (a) steady   (b) conditioned   kept   (c) zero-state
  CUSUM                   9.4               9.1    12%            10.1
  Page-Hinkley           30.4              21.4     5%           442.2
  EWMA                    9.2               9.6    16%             7.7
  Windowed KS           102.1              22.2     4%           445.3
  ADWIN                  30.3              17.7    13%           446.7

  The 'kept' column is why convention (b) was rejected: at an ARL0 of
  500 and a 1000-sample pre-change segment, most replicates false-alarm
  first and the conditioning discards them on a criterion correlated
  with the detector's state. The remaining sample is not representative.
  FINDING  the conditioning convention (discard replicates that false-alarm before the change) keeps only a small fraction of replicates at these operating points and was rejected for that reason; the kept fractions are in section 6 of validation/outputs/validate_arl_calibration.txt.

  The zero-state column is the surprise, and it is not a flattering
  convention - it is a destructive one for three of the five detectors.
  CUSUM and EWMA standardise against a DECLARED mu0 and sigma0, so a
  chart started at the change still sees a shifted stream and detects it
  in single-figure samples. Page-Hinkley, the windowed KS test and ADWIN
  are all SELF-REFERENTIAL: they compare the stream against its own
  recent history. Started at the change with no pre-change history, they
  are handed a perfectly stationary stream that merely sits at a
  different level, and there is nothing in it to detect. Their zero-state
  delays run to the censoring budget.

  This is the practical distinction between the two families and it is
  not a detail. A self-referential detector needs pre-change history it
  trusts; a declared-nominal detector needs a nominal value it trusts.
  Neither assumption is free, and a telemetry channel after a reboot or a
  mode change gives you the first problem.
  FINDING  zero-state ARL1 separates the detectors into two families: CUSUM and EWMA, which standardise against a declared nominal and detect a step started cold in under 11 samples, and Page-Hinkley / windowed KS / ADWIN, which are self-referential and cannot detect it at all (delays at the 1500-sample censoring budget) because without pre-change history a mean step is indistinguishable from a stationary stream at a different level.

1. Autocorrelation destroys an i.i.d.-calibrated threshold
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Every threshold in this package is fitted on an i.i.d. Gaussian stream.
  Housekeeping telemetry is sampled faster than the physical time
  constants it measures and is strongly autocorrelated. The AR(1) stream
  used here has the SAME marginal distribution N(0,1) as the calibration
  stream - only the dependence differs - so anything that moves below is
  caused by dependence alone.

  Measured lag-one autocorrelation of the generated streams:
    phi = 0.0: measured lag-1 = -0.0037, sd = 1.0021
    phi = 0.2: measured lag-1 = +0.1969, sd = 1.0015
    phi = 0.4: measured lag-1 = +0.3979, sd = 1.0011
    phi = 0.6: measured lag-1 = +0.5988, sd = 1.0010
    phi = 0.8: measured lag-1 = +0.7999, sd = 1.0020
    phi = 0.9: measured lag-1 = +0.9008, sd = 1.0058

  ARL0 at the i.i.d.-calibrated thresholds, 4 seeds x 30000 samples:

  phi              CUSUM    Page-Hinkley            EWMA     Windowed KS           ADWIN
  0.0          570+/-42         469+/-15         519+/-35         463+/-13         476+/-24  
  0.2          164+/-6          320+/-9          171+/-6          407+/-8          235+/-7   
  0.4           73+/-2          231+/-6           75+/-2          350+/-4          147+/-3   
  0.6           41+/-1          161+/-3           39+/-1          328+/-2          106+/-1   
  0.8           25+/-0          102+/-2           21+/-0          317+/-1           87+/-1   
  0.9           20+/-0           85+/-1           15+/-0          307+/-0           81+/-1   

  Degradation factor, ARL0 at phi = 0 divided by ARL0 at phi:
  phi              CUSUM    Page-Hinkley            EWMA     Windowed KS           ADWIN
  0.2               3.5x            1.5x            3.0x            1.1x            2.0x
  0.4               7.8x            2.0x            6.9x            1.3x            3.2x
  0.6              14.0x            2.9x           13.3x            1.4x            4.5x
  0.8              22.5x            4.6x           25.0x            1.5x            5.5x
  0.9              28.8x            5.5x           34.5x            1.5x            5.9x

  A factor of F means the detector false-alarms F times more often than
  the operating point it was calibrated to, on a stream whose marginal
  distribution is identical. This is not a small correction.
  PASS  autocorrelation degrades at least one detector by more than 5x  [worst ewma 34.5x]
  FINDING  AUTOCORRELATION BREAKS THE CALIBRATION. At lag-one autocorrelation 0.9, with the marginal distribution unchanged, the measured ARL0 falls by CUSUM 28.8x, Page-Hinkley 5.5x, EWMA 34.5x, Windowed KS 1.5x, ADWIN 5.9x. Any threshold from this package applied to real telemetry without re-calibrating on that channel's own quiet data will false-alarm far more often than its nominal ARL0.

  The windowed KS test degrades least, which is a property of its
  statistic rather than a virtue: it compares empirical distributions,
  and an AR(1) stream has the same marginal distribution as the
  i.i.d. one it was calibrated against. It is blind to the change in
  dependence for exactly the reason it is robust to it.

2A. ADWIN's delta scaling, which set the shipped min_sub
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  eps_cut grows only as sqrt(log(1/delta)), so driving delta down raises
  the cut threshold extremely slowly. With a small min_sub the cut test
  is applied to tiny subwindows where the Hoeffding bound behind eps_cut
  is at its loosest, and the ARL0 reachable at any sane delta is short.

  Measured ARL0, 3 seeds x 30000 stationary samples:

  min_sub   delta=1e-2   1e-4   1e-6   1e-8   1e-10   1e-12
        5        45     76    125    218    372    600
       15        90    145    238    375    604   1012
       30       155    226    378    596   1034   1671
       50       245    360    609    950   1392   2571

  Ten orders of magnitude of delta buy a factor of 13.5 in ARL0 at
  min_sub = 5 (45 to 600 samples). That is the
  sqrt(log(1/delta)) scaling, measured.

  At min_sub = 5 the 500-sample target is crossed only
  somewhere between delta = 1e-10 (ARL0 372) and delta = 1e-12
  (ARL0 600): a confidence parameter nine to ten orders of
  magnitude below the 0.002 the common implementations ship. At
  min_sub = 30 the target is crossed near delta = 1e-7 (ARL0 378 at 1e-6, 596 at 1e-8).
  PASS  at min_sub = 5 the target ARL0 lies between delta = 1e-10 and 1e-12, as documented  [ARL0 372 at 1e-10, 600 at 1e-12, target 500]
  PASS  ten orders of magnitude of delta change ARL0 by less than 20x at min_sub = 5, as sqrt(log(1/delta)) predicts  [13.5x]

  The practical consequence, measured rather than argued: the calibration
  procedure's bracket search walks outward from the detector's declared
  default by a factor of 1.6 per step for at most 26 steps, which reaches
  delta = 0.002 / 1.6^26 = 1e-8. At min_sub = 5 that is not far enough
  and the calibration reports a bracketing FAILURE. Measured:

    min_sub =  5: bracketing_failed = True, delta = 9.861e-09, held-out ARL0 = 227.2 +/- 9.7 (target error -54.6 %)
  PASS  min_sub = 5 fails to bracket the target from the shipped default within the bracket budget  [delta=9.86e-09, ARL0=227]
    min_sub = 30: bracketing_failed = False, delta = 9.942e-08, held-out ARL0 = 476.5 +/- 24.5 (target error -4.7 %)
  PASS  min_sub = 30 brackets the target from the shipped default  [delta=9.94e-08, ARL0=476]

  min_sub = 30 is therefore the shipped default. It was declared BEFORE
  any ARL1 was measured, which makes it a configuration choice and not a
  tuned result, and the alternative was an ADWIN that could not be placed
  at the common operating point at all.
  FINDING  ADWIN's delta is a very weak control on its false-alarm rate: ten orders of magnitude of delta move the measured ARL0 by only 13.5x at min_sub = 5 (45 to 600 samples), because eps_cut grows as sqrt(log(1/delta)). At min_sub = 5 the 500-sample target needs delta near 1e-11 and the bracket search from the shipped default of 0.002 does not reach it, so the calibration reports a failure. min_sub = 30 is the shipped default for that reason, declared before any delay was measured.

2B. The harness reset convention against published ADWIN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Published ADWIN drops the older part of its window on a cut and keeps
  the newer part. This harness resets the whole window, so that all five
  analytic detectors are treated identically. The obvious objection is
  that the harness convention is unfair to ADWIN. It is measured here,
  and it is not: the published convention gives a SHORTER ARL0, because
  after shrinking the detector immediately re-tests the window it kept.

  delta        full reset (harness)   shrink (published)   ratio
  1e-02                       166.8                102.0    1.63x
  PASS  the harness convention is not the pessimistic one at delta = 1e-02  [reset 167 vs shrink 102]
  1e-04                       249.6                161.1    1.55x
  PASS  the harness convention is not the pessimistic one at delta = 1e-04  [reset 250 vs shrink 161]
  1e-06                       391.5                238.5    1.64x
  PASS  the harness convention is not the pessimistic one at delta = 1e-06  [reset 392 vs shrink 238]
  1e-08                       640.9                367.8    1.74x
  PASS  the harness convention is not the pessimistic one at delta = 1e-08  [reset 641 vs shrink 368]
  FINDING  the full-reset convention this harness uses for ADWIN gives a LONGER measured ARL0 than published ADWIN's window-shrinking convention at every delta tested, so the convention does not disadvantage ADWIN. Both columns are in section 2B of validation/outputs/validate_robustness.txt.

2C. ADWIN's nominal delta is not a false-alarm rate
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The published bound is per cut test. The detector performs O(log n) cut
  tests per check and a check every check_every = 10 samples, so the
  nominal delta and the measured false-alarm rate are different
  quantities. Here is how different.

  delta       nominal 1/delta   measured ARL0   measured/nominal
  1e-02                   100           156.4            1.5639
  1e-03                  1000           198.3            0.1983
  1e-04                 10000           238.1            0.0238
  1e-06               1000000           384.7            0.0004
  FINDING  ADWIN's nominal delta is not a stream-level false-alarm rate: the measured ARL0 is orders of magnitude shorter than 1/delta at every delta tested. Anyone reading delta = 0.002 as 'one false alarm per 500 samples' is wrong by a large factor in the unsafe direction; the measured ARL0 at that delta is in section 1 of validate_arl_calibration.py.

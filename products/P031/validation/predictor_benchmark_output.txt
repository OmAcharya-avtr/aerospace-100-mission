WHAT THE NUMBERS SAY
============================================================================================================
jittery family:
  fixed_threshold        ROC AUC 0.5041
  queueing_markov        ROC AUC 0.5016
  learned_hgb            ROC AUC 0.4966
  An AUC at or near 0.50 means the predictor cannot rank the
  iterations at all. On this family all three are there, including
  the learned one: there is no usable signal in the history, so
  there is nothing for a model to learn. The fixed threshold is not
  beaten here because nothing beats anything here.

bursty family, at a matched flag rate:
  fixed_threshold        missed 0.3377   Brier 0.06862   AUC 0.8558
  queueing_markov        missed 0.3219   Brier 0.06983   AUC 0.8337
  learned_hgb            missed 0.3202   Brier 0.05775   AUC 0.8583
  learned minus fixed, missed-overrun rate : -0.0175
  typical 95 % CI half-width on that rate  : 0.0168
  The gap is marginally larger than the half-width of a single
  run's interval. That is a real but small improvement, not a
  decisive one: it is the same order as the interval, and it
  appears only on the autocorrelated family.
  Brier: learned is lower by +0.01087 (+15.8 %)
  AUC  : learned is higher by +0.0026
  The calibration and ranking advantages are the clearer ones. The
  learned model's probability output is a usable confidence value;
  the fixed threshold's is a two-level frequency and the Markov
  baseline's is worse calibrated than either.

  lead time: about 1.02 iterations on the bursty family against
  about 1.88 on the jittery one. That is not the learned model
  being slow to warn; it is where the overruns are. Inside a burst
  the next iteration is usually the one that overruns, so a correct
  flag has a lead time of 1 by construction. A lead time near the
  horizon mean, as on the jittery family, is what a flag looks like
  when it carries no information about *which* iteration will miss.

  CONCLUSION, stated plainly: the fixed threshold is not beaten on
  the jittery family, where nothing works. On the autocorrelated
  family the learned model is the best of the three, by a margin
  that is clear on calibration (Brier) and ranking (AUC) and
  marginal on missed-overrun rate at a matched flag rate. The
  queueing/Markov baseline is the most conservative of the three:
  it flags least and misses most, which is the independence
  approximation in its horizon product doing what the model card
  says it does. None of this is a reason to deploy the learned
  model in place of the threshold: an 18-feature boosted ensemble
  is a large amount of machinery for a narrow win on one trace
  family, and the threshold has one parameter.

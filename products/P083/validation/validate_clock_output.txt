==============================================================================
3. the detection floor: the smallest reset the detector finds
==============================================================================
The threshold is the larger of eight robust scales of the first differences
and a declared absolute floor of 1e-4 s, so a reset whose offset jump is
smaller than that floor is NOT detected. That is a real limitation and the
floor is measured here rather than asserted to be zero.

[PASS] the detection floor is between the declared 1e-4 s and one period -- smallest detected 0.00011 s
[PASS] control: a reset smaller than the declared floor is missed (must fire) -- FIRED; largest missed 9e-05 s; this is the limitation, reported rather than hidden

What a missed reset costs: an offset jump below the floor shifts every
later timestamp by that amount, which is at most 1e-4 s, or 1 per cent of
a period. A duration straddling it is wrong by the same amount. So the
undetected cases are exactly the ones whose consequence for a duration is
bounded by the floor, and the report says so.

4. least squares against Theil-Sen under corrupted timestamps
==============================================================================
The two estimators are provided because they fail differently. Least
squares is efficient under the declared Gaussian jitter and is pulled by a
single bad timestamp; Theil-Sen has a breakdown point of 29 per cent. Both
are reported in every segment table, and this is the measurement behind
that choice.

==============================================================================
5. the consequence for the verdict: which iterations appear to miss
==============================================================================
The recorded durations are measured on the device clock, so a clock running
fast by rho reports every interval as (1 + rho) times its true length and
the set of iterations that appear to have overrun their deadline changes.
This is why the clock check runs before anything else in
hilverdict.verdict, and the size of the effect is measured here.

# TurbScope benchmark: learned model vs closed-form inversions

Generated by `validation/benchmark_ml.py`, seed 20260829, 6000 synthetic scenarios.

All errors are in **dex** (decades of Cn2).  The target is the
scintillation-kernel weighted path average of Cn2 -- the quantity the
closed-form scintillometer inversion is *designed* to estimate, so the
baseline is not handicapped by a mismatched target.

> The data is synthetic.  Every number below measures fidelity to a
> generative process defined by this package, not agreement with a field
> instrument.  See `DATASET_CARD.md`.

## 7. Why the baselines win where they do

The DIMM baseline estimates the *coherence*-kernel path average while the
target is the *scintillation*-kernel average.  On these held-out paths the
two differ by a median of 0.1287 dex and a p95 of 0.3473 dex,
which is an irreducible floor on the DIMM baseline's RMSE and explains why it
is flat at ~0.18 dex in every regime: it is not noise-limited, it is
kernel-limited.

The scintillometer baselines are unbiased estimators of the target and are
limited only by sampling noise until saturation sets in.  The learned model
cannot beat an unbiased estimator of its own target in the weak and moderate
regimes -- it can only add regression-to-the-mean and tree quantisation error,
and it does: it is 1.38x worse than the best baseline in the weak regime and
2.18x worse in the moderate regime.  **That is the expected result and it is
reported as a loss.**  The learned model earns its place only in the saturated
regime, where the analytic inversion is multi-valued, refuses 36.7 % of
readings outright, and its interval covers only 21.6 % of cases against a 90 %
nominal.

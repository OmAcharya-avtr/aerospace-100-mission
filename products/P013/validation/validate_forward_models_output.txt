4. Large-aperture weak-regime scaling exponents
==============================================================================
Wang, Ochs & Clifford (1978), JOSA 68(3) 334-338, showed that a large-aperture
scintillometer obeys Cn2 ~ sigma^2 D^(7/3) L^(-3), i.e. sigma^2 ~ Cn2 D^(-7/3) L^3.
Their instrument is double-ended (transmitter AND receiver apertures); the model
here is single-ended (point source, finite receiver aperture), so the CONSTANT
differs and is not compared -- only the aperture and path-length EXPONENTS.

     D range (m)            d^2 range    d log sigma/d log D
  0.30 -   1.20      91.2 - 1.46e+03                -2.2005
  1.00 -   4.00  1.01e+03 - 1.62e+04                -2.2022
  3.00 -  12.00  9.12e+03 - 1.46e+05                -2.1751
 10.00 -  40.00  1.01e+05 - 1.62e+06                -2.1422
large-scale term                    -                -2.3333
small-scale term                    -                -2.0000

  d(log sigma_I^2)/d(log L) at D = 3 m: +2.9302   (theory +3, deviation 0.0698)

RESULT: PARTIAL - the pure D^(-7/3) law is NOT reproduced.
Andrews & Phillips Eq. 9.60 is the sum of a large-scale term that falls as
d^(-7/3) and a small-scale term that falls as d^(-2); the composite is
therefore not a pure power law.  The measured slope sits between the two
(-2.20 at instrument-sized apertures, drifting toward -2 as the small-scale
term takes over above d^2 ~ 6e4).  Wang et al.'s D^(7/3) law corresponds to
the large-scale term alone.  This is reported as a discrepancy, not a pass:
callers must not assume a clean D^(7/3) aperture law from this package.

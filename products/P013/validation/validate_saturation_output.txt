TurbScope validation 3 - saturation of scintillation (documented failure regime)

==============================================================================
1. Bias of the weak-regime linear inversion vs true beta_0^2
==============================================================================
The textbook inversion assumes sigma_I^2 = beta_0^2.  Below is the Cn2 it
returns, relative to the truth, using the exact forward model to generate
the (noise-free) reading.

  beta_0^2   sigma_I^2    Cn2_weak/Cn2_true    error %
      0.01     0.01003              1.00256       0.26
       0.1     0.10106              1.01062       1.06
       0.3     0.29970              0.99900      -0.10
       0.5     0.48413              0.96826      -3.17
         1     0.86083              0.86083     -13.92
         2     1.30628              0.65314     -34.69
         5     1.66381              0.33276     -66.72
     7.297     1.69210              0.23190     -76.81
        10     1.67700              0.16770     -83.23
        20     1.57939              0.07897     -92.10
        50     1.42299              0.02846     -97.15
       100     1.32326              0.01323     -98.68

Inside the weak regime the linear inversion is good to ~1 % (its largest
deviation there is +1.06 % near beta_0^2 = 0.1, and it is -0.10 % at the
beta_0^2 = 0.3 limit).  Beyond it the bias is one-sided and severe: -13.9 %
at beta_0^2 = 1, -66.7 % at 5, -92.1 % at 20 and -98.7 % at 100, i.e. nearly
two orders of magnitude of under-estimate.  This is why WEAK_REGIME_BETA0_SQ
is set to 0.3 and why the package warns above it.

==============================================================================
2. The inversion is multi-valued: quantifying the ambiguity
==============================================================================
peak of sigma_I^2(beta_0^2):  beta_0^2 = 7.296562, sigma_I^2 = 1.692103
large-beta asymptote:         sigma_I^2 -> 1.052378
multi-valued reading range:   1.052378 < sigma_I^2 < 1.692103
unexplainable readings:       sigma_I^2 > 1.692103

For a 2 km path at 850 nm, the two solutions and the Cn2 ambiguity they imply:
 sigma_I^2  beta_0^2 (low)  beta_0^2 (high)       Cn2 low      Cn2 high    ratio
     1.060          1.3582        6998.7964    2.3519e-14    1.2119e-10   5153.1x
     1.100          1.4433        1879.7124    2.4993e-14    3.2550e-11   1302.3x
     1.200          1.6840         331.0094    2.9162e-14    5.7319e-12    196.6x
     1.300          1.9791         120.6637    3.4271e-14    2.0895e-12     61.0x
     1.400          2.3597          57.9349    4.0861e-14    1.0032e-12     24.6x
     1.500          2.8936          31.5421    5.0108e-14    5.4620e-13     10.9x
     1.600          3.7842          17.6933    6.5529e-14    3.0639e-13      4.7x
     1.650          4.6259          12.7099    8.0105e-14    2.2009e-13      2.7x
     1.690          6.5499           8.1671    1.1342e-13    1.4142e-13      1.2x

9 of 9 test readings admit two solutions.  At sigma_I^2 = 1.06 the
two branches differ by a factor of 5153 in Cn2; at sigma_I^2 = 1.50 by a factor
of 10.9; and even at sigma_I^2 = 1.69, one part in a thousand below the peak,
by a factor of 1.2.  No estimator, and no amount of averaging, resolves this
from a single point-receiver scintillometer reading -- the information is not
in the measurement.  (The upper branch above beta_0^2 ~ 1e3 is a root of the
Andrews-Phillips fit far outside the range over which it was validated; it is
reported because it is a genuine solution of the model the package uses, not
because such turbulence is expected on a real path.)

3. Sensitivity collapse: the propagated uncertainty diverges at the peak
==============================================================================
Relative standard deviation of the recovered Cn2 from N = 1000 irradiance
samples, as the true turbulence approaches the peak.

  beta_0^2  beta/beta_pk   d sigma/d beta   rel sigma (Cn2)  beta recovered  recovery err
    0.0730         0.010        1.014e+00            0.0482          0.0730      2.22e-16
    0.3648         0.050        9.382e-01            0.0656          0.3648     -4.84e-14
    0.7297         0.100        7.628e-01            0.0921          0.7297      2.22e-16
    2.1890         0.300        2.580e-01            0.2508          2.1890     -2.22e-16
    3.6483         0.500        8.807e-02            0.5560          3.6483     -2.22e-16
    5.8372         0.800        1.495e-02            2.2162          5.8372     -2.07e-13
    6.9317         0.950        2.641e-03           10.5995          6.9317     -4.24e-14
    7.2236         0.990        4.849e-04           55.3508          7.2236     -7.55e-15
    7.2893         0.999        4.758e-05          558.8395             nan           nan

The sensitivity d(sigma_I^2)/d(beta_0^2) falls by five orders of magnitude
between 1 % and 99.9 % of the peak, so the delta-method uncertainty on Cn2
grows without bound: 4.8 % at beta_0^2 = 0.073 becomes 222 % at 5.84 and
55880 % at 7.29.  Away from the peak the root-finder is exact (recovery error
<= 2e-13 in every row up to 99 % of the peak), but within 0.1 % of the peak it
fails completely: sigma_I^2 is flat there to within the scan resolution, no
bracketing sign change exists, and the inversion returns no branch at all (NaN
in the last row).  The package reports the widening interval, and then the
absence of a solution, rather than returning a confident number.

==============================================================================
4. End-to-end failure rate on simulated strong-turbulence observations
==============================================================================
path 2 km at 850 nm, receiver D = 0.25 m -> aperture d^2 = 57.75
point-channel peak sigma_I^2 = 1.6921; aperture-channel peak = 0.1925 at beta_0^2 = 26.2

   Cn2 true   beta_0^2  invalid %  ambiguous %   median Cn2_hat/Cn2   p95 ratio
    1.0e-15       0.06        0.0          0.0               1.0029      1.0600
    3.0e-15       0.17        0.0          0.0               1.0027      1.0767
    1.0e-14       0.58        0.0          0.0               1.0001      1.0951
    3.0e-14       1.73        0.0         99.5               0.9761      1.2733
    1.0e-13       5.77       39.0         61.0               0.6582      0.9121

RESULT: the analytic point-receiver inversion degrades sharply above
beta_0^2 ~ 1.7 and is unusable at beta_0^2 ~ 5.8, where 39.0 % of readings
exceed the attainable maximum and are refused outright, 61.0 % are flagged
ambiguous, and the surviving estimates have a median Cn2_hat/Cn2 of 0.658.
At beta_0^2 = 1.73 essentially every reading (99.5 %) is already formally
ambiguous even though the low branch is still the right one (median ratio
0.976) -- the ambiguity flag fires well before the point estimate fails.
Note that aperture averaging only postpones the problem: the aperture channel
here peaks at sigma_I^2 = 0.1925 at beta_0^2 = 26.2 and is multi-valued above
that.  TurbScope documents all of this as a limit of the measurement, not a
defect of the software.  validation/benchmark_ml.py reports how much of the
gap the learned multi-sensor model closes, and how much it does not.

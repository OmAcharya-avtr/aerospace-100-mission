## 1. Problem

Predict the **path-averaged refractive-index structure parameter Cn2_path**
(m⁻²/³) — the single number that summarises how strong optical turbulence is
along a sensing path — from what a two-instrument deployment (a
scintillometer and a DIMM) actually reads, plus the known path length.

* Task: regression, target `log10 Cn2_path`, one scalar per measurement, plus
  a calibrated interval.
* Working in log10 is not cosmetic: Cn2_path spans roughly five decades over
  the training range (`validation/VALIDATION.md`), so a linear-space loss
  would be dominated by the largest values.
* Why anyone wants this: Cn2_path directly sizes free-space-optical link
  margins, adaptive-optics correction budgets, and beam-wander predictions.
  The classical scintillometer inversion, on its own, has a known,
  quantified failure mode (scintillation saturation, §10) that this model is
  built to be robust against by also consulting a DIMM channel that does not
  share it.

## 2. Baseline (implemented FIRST, and the mandated benchmark reference)

`turbscope.scintillometer` and `turbscope.dimm` were written, unit-tested and
validated (`validation/round_trip_recovery.py`,
`validation/saturation_regime.py`) **before any model was fitted.** The
mandated single-sensor closed-form comparator is
`turbscope.model.ScintillometerWeakBaseline`
(`invert_cn2_weak` — the exact algebraic inverse of the weak-fluctuation
Rytov formula, Tatarski 1961; Andrews & Phillips 2005). It is applied to the
scintillometer reading alone, unconditionally, exactly as a naive real-world
analysis pipeline would.

Two further closed-form comparators are scored alongside it so the learned
model is never flattered by a single weak reference:

* `DimmOnlyBaseline` — the DIMM-only closed-form inversion (Sarazin &
  Roddier 1990), averaging the longitudinal and transverse channels.
* `MeanTrainingBaseline` — the mean log10 Cn2 of the training set, using no
  input at all; the honest "learned nothing" floor.

**Every one of these is applied exactly as its closed form dictates, with no
tuning of tolerances to make any of them look better or worse.**

## 3. Architecture

Three `sklearn.ensemble.GradientBoostingRegressor` instances sharing
hyperparameters (`n_estimators=250`, `max_depth=3`, `learning_rate=0.08`,
`min_samples_leaf=15`, `random_state=11`):

| sub-model | loss | α | role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `median_model` | quantile | 0.50 | point prediction |
| `lower_model` | quantile | 0.05 | interval lower bound |
| `upper_model` | quantile | 0.95 | interval upper bound |

plus a scalar **conformal offset δ** fitted on a disjoint calibration split
(§9, split-conformal / CQR, Romano, Patterson & Candès 2019). Prediction
returns `(10^lower, 10^median, 10^upper)`; because `10^x` is monotone,
log-space quantiles are exactly linear-space quantiles.

Quantile crossing (lower > upper at some query) is possible with
independently fitted quantile models; it is detected and repaired by sorting
the triple pointwise, and its rate on the fit set is reported by
`fit_report()` — **0.33%** in the production run (`validation/benchmark_results.md`).

Inputs are the 4 columns of `turbscope.dataset.FEATURE_NAMES` (three sensor
log-readings + known log path length); no scaling is applied (tree ensembles
are scale-invariant). No model artefact is committed — the model refits in
~3 s.

## 4. Dataset source

`DATASET_CARD.md`, generated by `src/turbscope/synthetic.py` +
`src/turbscope/dataset.py`. 900 scenarios × 3 noisy realisations, master seed
20260829. Nothing is committed as data; regeneration is deterministic.

## 5. Dataset limitations

**The training data is synthetic, generated by running this product's own
closed-form forward models plus a hand-chosen multiplicative noise model.
Measured accuracy therefore reflects fidelity to a generative process, not
agreement with a real scintillometer or DIMM.** No measured Cn2 of any kind
appears anywhere in this product. See `DATASET_CARD.md` for the complete list
of what is not modelled (real instrument noise characteristics, DIMM's own
real-world sensitivity limits, anisoplanatism, non-Kolmogorov turbulence,
and more).

## 6. Training procedure

```python
from turbscope.model import train_default_model
model, artefacts = train_default_model()      # ~3-4 s on 2 CPU cores
```

Steps, all deterministic:

1. `generate_default_scenarios(900, seed=20260829)`.
2. Scenario-level split, seed 4242 → 675 non-test / 225 test.
3. Second scenario-level split of the non-test pool, seed 4243 → 506 fit /
   169 calibration.
4. Feature tables: 3 noisy realisations per scenario (noise seeds 99, 100,
   101 for fit/calibration/test respectively).
5. Fit the three quantile regressors on the fit rows.
6. `model.calibrate(x_cal, y_cal)` → conformal δ = **+0.0266 dex**.

No hyperparameter search was run against the test split; the defaults were
chosen for the compute budget and left alone.

## 7. Metrics (held-out, from an actual run — `validation/benchmark_results.md`)

Errors in dex (decades of Cn2), 675 held-out rows from 225 unseen scenarios:

| predictor | RMSE | MAE | bias | p95 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **TurbScope learned model** | **0.0714** | 0.0497 | −0.0054 | 0.1224 |
| **Scintillometer weak baseline (mandated)** | 0.6577 | 0.3578 | −0.3332 | 1.6216 |
| DIMM-only baseline | **0.0318** | 0.0249 | −0.0020 | 0.0598 |
| Training mean (learned-nothing floor) | 1.6877 | 1.4449 | −0.0434 | 2.9402 |

Learned/mandated-baseline RMSE ratio **0.1086** (89% reduction).

### Honest reading of this result — read both halves

**Against the mission-mandated baseline (scintillometer-only closed-form
inversion), the learned model wins decisively** (RMSE ratio 0.109). This
comparison is the one required by the mission spec, and it is not a weak
strawman: the scintillometer baseline is competitive in the weak regime
(RMSE 0.038, actually *better* than the learned model there,
`validation/VALIDATION.md` §3.3) and only fails where it is documented to
fail — the saturated regime (RMSE 0.967, a genuine, large, and expected
failure quantified independently in §10 and `validation/saturation_regime.py`).

**Against a stronger closed-form single-sensor baseline (DIMM-only), the
learned model does NOT win** — DIMM-only achieves 0.032 dex vs the learned
model's 0.071 dex. **This is reported plainly, as required when a closed-form
baseline wins.** The reason is structural, not a training or tuning failure:
this product's synthetic generator gives DIMM's inversion **zero
saturation-related model-form error at any turbulence strength**
(`validation/VALIDATION.md` §1.2 — exact to machine precision), so DIMM-only
is limited purely by its 10% sensor noise propagated through an exactly
linear inversion. No learned model can beat a noise-limited unbiased linear
estimator at the task that estimator is exactly built for. The learned
model's extra error comes from `GradientBoostingRegressor`'s inherent
piecewise-constant approximation and from the scintillometer feature
occasionally misleading it in the saturated regime.

**Mission takeaway:** on this problem, as modelled, most of the practical
value of "multi-sensor fusion" is *avoiding a broken sensor* rather than
*fusing two working ones* — the learned model is far more uniform across
regimes than the scintillometer-only baseline specifically because it has
access to DIMM, which does not share the failure. A simpler rule (trust DIMM,
treat the scintillometer as a saturation detector) is not ruled out as
matching or beating the learned model here, and was not built as a
comparator in this release — see `README.md` Roadmap.

## 8. Test-split strategy

**Grouped by scenario, never by row.** All 3 realisations of one scenario
share the ground-truth Cn2 and path length; a row-level random split would
place near-duplicates on both sides of the boundary and inflate every
metric. Three disjoint scenario sets: fit (506), conformal calibration
(169), test (225).

What the split does **not** test: generalisation outside the training
domain. That is handled by the `extrapolating` flag, not by a metric.

## 9. Uncertainty / confidence output

Every prediction is a `PathCn2Prediction` carrying `cn2_lower`, `cn2_path`,
`cn2_upper`, the nominal `coverage`, and an `extrapolating` flag — the model
never returns a bare point estimate. The interval is a conditional-quantile
interval, conformalised by split CQR (Romano, Patterson & Candès 2019):

| interval | nominal | empirical coverage on held-out data | mean width |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| raw quantile GBR | 0.900 | 0.7985 | 0.2869 dex |
| conformally calibrated | 0.900 | **0.8770** | 0.3333 dex |

Coverage by regime: weak 0.8512, saturated 0.9071 (`validation/VALIDATION.md`
§4) — within the ±0.02–0.03 resolution of this held-out sample size.

Caveats: conformal coverage is *marginal*, not conditional — guaranteed in
aggregate, not for any chosen sub-population (e.g. by regime). Its
exchangeability assumption holds here **by construction**, because
calibration and test data come from the same synthetic generator; that is
precisely why the guarantee does not transfer to real sensor data.

The classical closed-form path (`turbscope.inversion`) also always returns an
uncertainty (`PointEstimate.cn2_std`), via **exact** linear error
propagation (both single-sensor inversions are exactly linear in their
measured quantity — proved algebraically and Hypothesis-tested in
`tests/test_inversion.py`), not an approximation.

## 10. Failure cases

1. **Scintillation saturation, quantified.** The scintillometer channel's own
   saturation (§`turbscope.scintillometer`,
   `validation/saturation_regime.py`) means a measured σ_I² in
   [0.999, 1.126] has two consistent Cn2 candidates 2.00× apart from the
   scintillometer alone; the learned model mitigates but does not eliminate
   the resulting error (saturated-regime RMSE 0.078 dex vs 0.065 dex weak).
2. **Out-of-domain path length or sensor readings.** Outside
   `TRAINING_DOMAIN` the tree ensemble returns its edge value with unchanged
   interval width, i.e. confidently wrong. `extrapolating=True` is set;
   callers must check it.
3. **Piecewise-constant in the input space.** Gradient boosting on trees
   produces a staircase; predicted Cn2 as a function of a sensor reading has
   small steps, not a smooth curve.
4. **Loses to a strong single-sensor closed-form baseline (§7).** DIMM-only
   beats the learned model outright in this synthetic design. Do not assume
   "learned + multi-sensor" implies "more accurate" without checking against
   the best available single-sensor closed form for the specific deployment.
5. **A single fixed instrument geometry was trained on.** Wavelengths,
   aperture and separation are fixed constants
   (`turbscope.synthetic.SCINT_WAVELENGTH_M` etc.); a differently configured
   instrument pair is out of the training domain even if its Cn2/path-length
   range is in range.
6. **No time dependence.** Every scenario is independent; nothing here
   predicts forward in time or exploits temporal correlation a real
   deployment would have.
7. **Interval is not calibrated for real sensor data (§9).**
8. **Under-dispersion in the weak regime.** Coverage there is 0.851 against
   0.900 nominal — the interval is somewhat too narrow specifically in the
   regime where the mandated baseline is strongest, meaning the interval's
   safety margin is thinnest exactly where a user might be tempted to trust
   the point estimate alone.

## 13. Ethical / safety limits

* **Not for operational use.** Not flight-qualified, not certified, not
  approved for operational aerospace use, and not to be used for go/no-go
  decisions on a laser link, an observing run, or any hardware in the field.
* **Do not present its numbers as measurements.** Any figure, table or
  downstream number derived from this model must carry the synthetic-data
  caveat (`DATASET_CARD.md`).
* **Directional-risk note.** Applying the mandated baseline blindly in the
  saturated regime under-estimates Cn2 by up to ~98% (§10.1,
  `validation/saturation_regime.py` §2.4) — a link-margin or adaptive-optics
  sizing calculation using it there would be dangerously optimistic. The
  learned model reduces but does not eliminate saturated-regime error
  (0.078 dex RMSE, i.e. ~20% typical relative error even with the interval);
  use the interval, not the point estimate, and check `extrapolating`.
* **No dual-use content.** The model contains no personal data, no
  controlled technical data, and no site-specific information.
* **Provenance.** All physics is from published, citable literature (Tatarski
  1961; Andrews & Phillips 2005; Wang, Ochs & Lawrence 1978; Sarazin &
  Roddier 1990; Fried 1965/1966; Tokovinin 2002; Romano, Patterson & Candès
  2019; Bevington & Robinson 2003); the saturation *curve shape* is an
  explicitly labelled heuristic, not a cited numerical fit (see
  `turbscope.scintillometer` module docstring). All data is from a committed
  script. No citation, coefficient or measurement in this product was
  invented to fill a gap; where a number is hand-chosen it says so.

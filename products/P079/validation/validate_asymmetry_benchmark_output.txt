7b. the verdict, stated plainly
==============================================================================

  symmetric_assumption : 2.525593e-04 s

  learned_ridge        : 1.039986e-04 s
  learned_gbr          : 1.050239e-04 s

median_filter vs learned_gbr : ratio 1.4401
  The median filter LOSES to the learned corrector on this metric. The
  specification predicted a match; the measurement does not support it,
  and the measurement is what is published.

learned_gbr vs learned_ridge : ratio 1.0099
  The gradient-boosted corrector LOSES to a standardised linear ridge on
  the same sixteen features. The ensemble, its hyperparameters and its
  version pin buy nothing on this generator: a linear combination of the
  features is the whole of the learnable signal. This is the result and
  it is not retuned away.

7c. the learned corrector's uncertainty output, and its coverage
==============================================================================
The reported per-row sigma is half the width of the quantile pair at
alpha = 0.1587 and 0.8413. That is a dispersion estimate, not a calibrated
interval, and the difference is measured here.

held-out coverage of the k = 1.96 band : 0.8917
nominal                                : 0.9500
quantile crossings on held-out rows    : 0.0000

The band UNDER-COVERS. The learned model's own uncertainty output is
optimistic by the margin above and must not be used as a confidence
interval. MODEL_CARD.md and README.md say so. The uncertainty that the
offset budget consumes is instead the held-out residual standard
deviation measured on a separate calibration split, and the coverage of
THAT is measured in validation/validate_uncertainty_coverage.py.

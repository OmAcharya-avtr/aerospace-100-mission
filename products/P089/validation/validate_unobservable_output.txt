==============================================================================
4.4 how much of this is the unobservable direction specifically
==============================================================================
A learned estimator could be overconfident everywhere, in which case the measurement above would say nothing about observability. The same quantities are therefore measured along the BEST-observed direction of the same records, where the analytic bound is finite and the batch solution does answer.

configuration           cover 2s, worst dir   cover 2s, best dir  analytic cover, best
forest                               0.0750               1.0000                0.9750
forest_deep                          0.2083               1.0000                0.9750
bagged_ridge                         0.0000               0.7250                0.9750
trivial                              0.5083               1.0000                0.9750

Reading, and this is the sharpest form of the result. Along the BEST-observed direction every learned configuration's two-sigma interval covers the truth at or above the nominal 0.954500 -- two of them at 1.0000, which is over-covering rather than under-covering. Along the direction the record cannot support, the same intervals from the same models on the same records collapse to between 0.0000 and 0.2083. The overconfidence is therefore NOT a global property of these models; it appears exactly where the data stops supporting an answer, and nothing in the model's own output distinguishes the two cases. The analytic bound in the last column does distinguish them: it is near nominal where it is finite and infinite where it is not.

4.5 record length does not help, which is the whole point
==============================================================================
A degree of freedom that the motion does not excite is not estimated better with more data. The same injection is measured at four record lengths spanning a factor of 32.

Reading: the rank verdict and the floor ratio are flat in record length, as equation (10) requires -- the excitation share does not change when the same motion runs for longer. The learned error does not fall. A degree of freedom the motion never excited is not a sample-size problem.

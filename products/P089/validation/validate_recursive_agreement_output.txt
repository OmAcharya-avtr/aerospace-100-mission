==============================================================================
3.1b the rank-deficient records, reported as measured
==============================================================================
On a record with a degree of freedom below the noise floor the recursive estimator does not withhold anything: it accumulates a large rotation in the direction the record cannot support, and because composing rotations is not a linear operation that rotation moves the components the record DOES support. This is the same mechanism that makes the unconstrained SVD solution of (17) wrong about the observable subspace, measured independently in tests/test_batch.py, and it is the strongest argument in this repository for computing the observability verdict first.

==============================================================================
3.2 what the filter does where the record supports nothing
==============================================================================
The MEKF carries a prior of one sigma 0.1 rad and solves over the full space, so it does not withhold anything. In a direction the record does not excite, the measurement Jacobian of (20) has no component, the gain is near zero and the covariance stays near the prior. 'prior retained' is the final one-sigma divided by the prior one-sigma: 1 means the record taught the filter nothing.

  degrees of freedom below the floor      : 49
  prior retained there, median            : 0.366386
  prior retained there, min and max       : 0.171620, 0.641779
  prior retained on observable dofs, median: 3.585607e-03
  worst/best sigma ratio, median          : 1.7474
  worst/best sigma ratio, max             : 495.7198

The filter is therefore honest about what it does not know, through its covariance rather than through a refusal. The next figure is the test of that honesty: its error in the unsupported direction, divided by the one-sigma it reports there.
  |error| / reported sigma, median        : 1.574558
  |error| / reported sigma, 90th centile  : 4.100611
  fraction inside 2 reported sigma        : 0.591837
[PASS] the filter retains at least a tenth of its prior where the record supports nothing -- median retention 0.366386
[PASS] the filter reduces its prior by 100x where the record does support a dof -- median retention 3.585607e-03

  MEASURED AND UNFAVOURABLE, published as found: the filter's two-sigma interval contains the truth on only 0.591837 of the records with an unsupported degree of freedom, against a nominal 0.954500. The recursive estimator is therefore overconfident in the unsupported direction too -- less so than the learned estimator of section 5, which is measured there, but overconfident. The mechanism is equation (11): the filter forms its Jacobian from the MEASURED reference rate, so the reference sensor's own noise supplies apparent information in the unsupported direction and the filter reduces its prior there on the strength of it. Split by how much prior the filter kept:

    prior retained below the median (0.3664): 0.291667 inside 2 sigma (24 records)
    prior retained above the median            : 0.880000 inside 2 sigma (25 records)
    correlation(prior retained, |error|/sigma) : -0.6690

[PASS] the under-coverage is explained by the filter reducing its prior on noise alone -- correlation -0.6690; coverage 0.880000 where the prior was kept against 0.291667 where it was not. The only estimator in this repository that is not overconfident here is the batch solution of (14)-(16), which withholds the direction and reports an infinite bound.

3.3 is the prior what separates the two solutions? Measured, not assumed.
==============================================================================
The shrinkage argument predicts the recursive estimate is the batch estimate scaled by lambda_i / (lambda_i + 1 / p0^2) along degree of freedom i, so tightening the prior from 0.1 rad to 0.01 rad should cut the disagreement by a factor of 100. Measured below on rich 1200-sample records.

 prior p0 rad  worst diff/CRB  predicted shrink
          0.1    1.186246e-02      2.364908e-05
         0.03    7.069247e-03      2.627675e-04
         0.01    3.361143e-03      2.364908e-03
        0.003    2.653953e-02      2.627675e-02

  The prior changed by a factor of 33.3, so the shrinkage argument predicts the disagreement to change by 1111. It changed by 0.4470.
[PASS] the disagreement does NOT scale as the prior-shrinkage argument predicts -- measured factor 0.4470 against a predicted 1111; the residual disagreement is dominated by the sequential re-linearisation of (20) about a moving estimate, not by the filter's prior, so quoting the shrinkage figure as the expected agreement would be wrong by three orders of magnitude

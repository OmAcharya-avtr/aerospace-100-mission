  MEASURED: the unbiased batch solution does not overtake the regularised one anywhere from 150 to 9600 samples in this measurement, a factor of 64 in record length. The advantage COLLAPSES, from 34.93 per cent at 150 samples to 1.04 per cent at 9600, and the remaining difference at the long end is at the level of this measurement's own sampling noise: the same sweep run with 40 records per point instead of 60, in examples/prior_and_record_length.py, measures a ratio of 1.001 at 4800 samples, which is the unbiased solution winning by a tenth of a per cent.

  What is NOT claimed: that equation (18) is optimal. It is the posterior mean under a GAUSSIAN prior whose per-component variance matches the generated population's second moment. The population's actual prior is not Gaussian -- the magnitude is uniform on [0, 3] mrad and the direction is uniform on the sphere -- so (18) is not the Bayes estimator for it and no domination theorem applies. The behaviour above is a measurement, not a consequence of one.
[PASS] the MAP advantage over the unbiased solution shrinks as the record lengthens -- MAP/batch RMSE ratio 0.650663 at 150 samples and 0.989640 at 9600; the advantage is bought entirely with the prior and it is worth less the more the record says

==============================================================================
5.3a the MAP win is bought with the prior: mis-specify it and it loses
==============================================================================
The regularised baseline wins above because it was handed the correct prior scale. That is a real advantage of an analytic method -- the shrinkage is in closed form, needs no training data and can be stated in a document -- but it is not free. The same estimator with a prior ten times too tight is measured below against the unbiased solution, on the same fixed configuration.

 samples        batch  MAP, true prior  MAP, 10x tight  MAP, 10x loose
     150     0.862279         0.561053        1.002141        0.840808
     600     0.290345         0.269755        0.869195        0.289972
    2400     0.142100         0.141564        0.634698        0.142081
    9600     0.075992         0.075205        0.369287        0.075982

[PASS] a prior ten times too tight makes the MAP baseline LOSE to the unbiased one -- the mis-specified MAP estimator is worse than the unbiased batch solution at 4 of 4 record lengths; the analytic win of 5.1 is therefore a statement about knowing the population, not a free lunch

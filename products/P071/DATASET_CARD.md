# Dataset card — conjprob synthetic conjunction population

## What this dataset is, before anything else

It is a **declared synthetic process**, generated by a committed script
(`src/conjprob/dataset.py`) from a fixed seed. It is **not** a sample of the real
conjunction population. No element of it was identified from a catalogue, from an
operator's screening output, or from any published distribution of close
approaches, because this product has no access to any of those. Every conclusion
drawn from it in `MODEL_CARD.md` and `README.md` is a conclusion about this
generator, and that qualification is not a formality: the single most important
measured result in this product — that the Mahalanobis screen does badly on one
population and well on another — is a statement about how the generator was
parameterised, and it is reported that way.

Nothing is committed as data. The dataset is regenerated on every run from the
seed, so there is no file to drift from the code.

## Why a synthetic process is nevertheless adequate for the measurement

The collision-probability integral depends on the encounter only through five
numbers: the two principal standard deviations of the encounter-plane covariance,
the two components of the miss vector in that frame, and the combined hard-body
radius. Sampling those directly samples the **complete input space** of the
quantity being screened. The generator therefore covers that space broadly rather
than realistically, which is the right choice for measuring *where* a screen
fails and the wrong choice for predicting *how often* it will.

## Generation procedure

```
python -c "from conjprob.dataset import generate_population; p = generate_population(24000, seed=0)"
```

Each conjunction is drawn independently:

| Quantity | Distribution | Range (nominal regime) |
|---|---|---|
| effective position uncertainty `sqrt(sigma_x sigma_y)` | log-uniform | 10 m to 3000 m |
| covariance aspect ratio `sigma_x / sigma_y` | log-uniform | 1 to 20 |
| combined hard-body radius `R` | log-uniform | 1 m to 30 m |
| relative speed | log-uniform | 100 m/s to 15000 m/s |
| out-of-plane sigma, as a multiple of the effective sigma | log-uniform | 0.2 to 5 |
| Mahalanobis distance `M` of the miss vector | uniform | 0 to 5 |
| miss-vector direction `psi` in the principal frame | uniform | 0 to 2 pi |

The principal sigmas and the miss vector follow from those:
`sigma_x = sigma_eff sqrt(aspect)`, `sigma_y = sigma_eff / sqrt(aspect)`,
`xbar = M sigma_x cos(psi)`, `ybar = M sigma_y sin(psi)`.

The relative speed and the out-of-plane sigma are **nuisance variables by
construction**: under the short-encounter assumptions the relative speed cancels
out of the Pc integral entirely and the out-of-plane direction is marginalised
away. They are included on purpose, so that a learned model that lets them change
its decision can be caught doing it. The measured importance assigned to them is
in `MODEL_CARD.md`.

## Labels

The label is `Pc > 1e-4`, with Pc the Foster-Estes value computed by adaptive
quadrature — the operational default's own answer, not an approximation of it.
One quadrature is performed per sample; 24000 samples takes under a second on the
two-core build container.

## Regimes

Three regimes are declared. The model is trained on `nominal` only and evaluated
unchanged on all three.

| Regime | Effective sigma | Mahalanobis | Purpose | Measured `log10(R/sigma_eff)` span | Measured positive rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `nominal` | 10 m to 3000 m | 0 to 5 | training and in-distribution test | 3.92251 | 0.34925 |
| `homogeneous` | 400 m to 600 m, aspect 1 to 1.5, R 9 m to 11 m | 0 to 5 | one object class and one covariance class, so that `R/sigma_eff` barely varies | 0.26125 | 0.25133 |
| `dilution` | 300 m to 10000 m | 0 to 2 | out-of-distribution: 71.733 per cent of cases have `M < sqrt(2)` and are on the dilution branch, against 29.558 per cent for `nominal` | 2.97221 | 0.11567 |

All figures in the last three columns are from
`validation/validate_screening_output.txt`, check 1, with 12000 samples per
regime at seed 1000; the training row there is 24000 samples at seed 0 with a
positive rate of 0.34496.

The `homogeneous` regime exists for one reason: to measure whether the
Mahalanobis screen's poor showing on `nominal` is caused by the spread of
`R/sigma_eff`, as the algebra of the leading-order approximation
`Pc ~= R^2 / (2 sigma_x sigma_y) exp(-M^2/2)` suggests. It is. That is a measured
explanation rather than an argued one, and it is the reason the regime is in the
package.

## Train/test split

By **seed**, not by shuffling one sample. The training set is seed 0 and every
test set is seed 1000, so no conjunction appears in both and there is no
possibility of leakage through a shared draw. Sizes are 24000 training and 12000
per test regime.

## Known limitations

1. **It is not the real population.** Real conjunctions are not uniform in
   Mahalanobis distance, real hard-body radii cluster by object class, and real
   covariances are correlated with altitude, object size and tracking history.
   None of that is modelled.
2. **Independence between the two objects' covariances is assumed** by the
   combined covariance being a sum; the generator works directly in combined
   terms and so cannot exhibit a violation of that assumption.
3. **Covariance realism is not modelled at all.** The generator produces a
   covariance and treats it as correct. In practice the dominant error in a
   collision probability is that the covariance is wrong, and nothing here can
   measure that.
4. **The out-of-plane sigma is drawn independently** of the in-plane sigmas,
   which no physical orbit-determination solution would be.
5. **No time dimension.** There is one epoch, one encounter and no propagation,
   so nothing in the dataset exercises covariance growth with epoch age.
6. **Uniform Mahalanobis distance inflates the positive rate** to about a third.
   A real screening population is overwhelmingly negative, so the precision
   figures in `MODEL_CARD.md` are optimistic by a factor that depends on the real
   base rate and that this product cannot estimate. The recall-at-fixed-
   false-positive-rate figures are the ones that transfer, because both are
   computed within class.

## Ethical and safety considerations

The dataset contains no personal data and no data about any real object. Its one
safety-relevant property is the limitation above: a model trained on it will
report performance figures that do not transfer to a real catalogue, and
`MODEL_CARD.md` says so in the same words.

==============================================================================
4. the measured kappa expressed as a relative speed
==============================================================================
kappa = |a| T^2 / (2 sigma_eff) with T = w sigma_eff / |v| gives
kappa = |a| w^2 sigma_eff / (2 |v|^2), so the relative speed at which a given
kappa is reached is |v| = w sqrt(|a| sigma_eff / (2 kappa)) with w = 8
the acceleration is the gravity-gradient scale mu |dr| / r^3 at r = 7078137 m
 sigma_eff [m]  separation [m]   |a| [m/s^2]   v at 1 pc [m/s]   v at 10 pc [m/s]
       18.5966         21.4056   2.40607e-05          0.065986           0.004622
      200.0000        300.0000   3.37211e-04          0.810120           0.056743
     1000.0000        500.0000   5.62019e-04          2.338616           0.163803

a typical low Earth conjunction has a relative speed of several kilometres per
second, which is four orders of magnitude above these figures, so the
rectilinear assumption is excellent there. It is the slow encounter -- metres
per second and below, which is the geostationary co-location case -- where the
2-D formulation in this package should not be used, and this is the measurement
that says so rather than an assertion.

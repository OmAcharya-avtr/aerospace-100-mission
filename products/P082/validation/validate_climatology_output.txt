2. the shrinkage of equation (12): what it buys and what it costs
==============================================================================
The shrinkage exists for one reason: a cell whose observed frequency is
exactly 0 or 1 has a probit threshold of minus or plus infinity, which
propagates an infinity into equation (8). It is a BIAS toward the station's
long-term rate, and its size is measured here rather than assumed negligible.

On THIS record no cell reaches 0 or 1 even at nu = 0, because the record is
eight years long and the declared marginals never leave (0.25, 0.56). The
shrinkage is therefore insurance rather than a necessity here, and the
control below constructs the case it is insurance against.

Equation (13) comes out BELOW the bootstrap on every station. It assumes an
AR(1) and the indicator series is not one: thresholding a persistent
Gaussian gives a process with a longer tail in its autocorrelation than its
lag-one implies, so the AR(1) correction understates the inflation. The
bootstrap figure is the one this product quotes for an empirical joint rate;
equation (13) is quoted for a MARGINAL rate, where it is the conservative
choice in the direction the naive form fails, and the ratio is reported
rather than hidden.

rather than quoting the relative number as if it were a bound on this
product's error.

2. the correlation excess against the subtraction it replaces
==============================================================================
Equation (9)'s first term is exactly the independence baseline and the
integral is exactly the amount correlation adds. The difference is therefore
computed as an integral of a positive integrand and never as a subtraction
of two nearly equal probabilities. The reference for small rho is the exact
first-order expansion rho phi_2(h, k; 0), whose remainder is O(rho^2).

Two regimes, bounded by two different things. For rho at or above 1e-12 the
integral form's apparent relative error is the REFERENCE expansion's own
second-order remainder and not a quadrature error: it is |h k| rho / 2 to
four figures at every rho. Below that the remainder falls under
double-precision rounding and what is left is the representation error of
the two quantities, which puts the integral form at the machine floor --
the stronger of the two statements. The subtraction, by contrast, has lost
every digit by rho = 1e-20 and returns exactly 0.0 for a quantity of
1.964128e-25.

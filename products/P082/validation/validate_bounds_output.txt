==============================================================================
max(0, sum q_i - (n - 1))  <=  P_joint  <=  min_i q_i

neither can be tightened without an assumption. The independence product
lies strictly inside them for n >= 2, and the width of the interval is the
entire content of the site-diversity problem: independence is one point in

3. the Bonferroni bracket of equation (18)
==============================================================================
For n = 2 inclusion-exclusion is exact:

    P(both cloudy) = 1 - P(i clear) - P(j clear) + P(both clear)

The right-hand side is computed entirely from CLEAR-rate statistics and the
left-hand side by counting cloudy epochs, so the two are different
computations of the same number. This is the sharpest independent-route
check in this package.

For n > 2 it is a bound and not an identity, and on cloud rates of order

4. the slack of equation (19) follows the conditioning
==============================================================================
The absolute slack is c eps (sum|q_i| + |f(q)|), set by the magnitude of the
SUMMANDS and never by the result. The relative slack is therefore kappa
times machine epsilon and diverges exactly where the expression cancels.

This product's FIRST version of equation (19) returned
c eps kappa |f(q)|, which cancels to c eps sum|q_i|: it looked conditioned
and was independent of f(q) altogether. Measured then: a well-conditioned
bound of 0.9 got a slack of 2.700e-14 and an ill-conditioned bound of 1e-07
got 1.421e-14 -- the ill-conditioned case got the SMALLER slack. The table
below is the current form over eight decades of cancellation.

Two statements, and they are the two halves of equation (19). The ABSOLUTE
slack is nearly constant across the table -- it changes only through the
|f(q)| term, which is why the largest and smallest differ by less than a
factor of two. The RELATIVE slack is proportional to 1 / f(q) and therefore
grows exactly as the bound cancels, decade for decade. Neither statement is
true of a slack floored at an absolute constant, which is the defect class
this check belongs to.

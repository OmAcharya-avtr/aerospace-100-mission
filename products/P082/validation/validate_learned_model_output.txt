WHAT HELD-OUT TIME DOES AND DOES NOT TEST, because it is easy to overstate.
None of the eight features of equation (33) encodes the time half: the
geometry, the regime and the long-term marginals are all time-invariant. A
held-out-TIME row therefore gets the SAME prediction as its train-cell
twin, and what differs is the TARGET, which is re-estimated from the second
half's epochs. The cell is therefore not a test of temporal extrapolation
by the model; it is a test of whether a correlation model fitted on the
first half still reproduces the second half's joint rates. A model that has
fitted the first half's estimation noise fails it, and that is exactly what
the numbers below show. The coverage of the TRUE correlation is identical
between the train and held-out-TIME cells for the same reason, and is
printed rather than suppressed so that the reason is visible.

What is and is not resampled. The bootstrap resamples THE RECORD and holds
the fitted models fixed, so it measures the uncertainty in the comparison
that comes from the reference being a finite record -- which is what 'the
bootstrap interval on the empirical reference' means. It does NOT measure
the uncertainty in the models' fitted parameters, which would require
refitting inside every replicate.

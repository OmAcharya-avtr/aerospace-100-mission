     accumulated persistence: 1.83 of 8 cells (1 outright)

the learned forest wins 1 of 8 cells outright and holds a 1.33-cell share

THE FINDING, stated plainly. The specification for this product
predicted that the analytic expected-cost model would be strong and
might well win. On this history it does: a classical baseline matches or
beats the learned forest on the majority of the reported metric-split
cells, and the forest's own largest feature importances are the analytic
cost and the accumulated wasted time -- that is, the baselines' own
outputs. A forest whose signal is a closed form it was given is not
adding a model; it is adding variance to one.

WHAT THE LEARNED COMPONENT DOES CONTRIBUTE, measured rather than
asserted: a calibratable interval. The analytic model emits a point
estimate and nothing else, and the forest's tree spread gives a
coverage figure a reader can check -- reported above whatever it is.
That is a real difference and it is not a win on the ranking metrics,
and the product does not claim otherwise.

This result is published as the finding. It is not retuned away, and the
model is not given features the baselines are denied in order to lose.

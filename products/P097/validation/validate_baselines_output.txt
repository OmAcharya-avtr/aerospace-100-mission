==============================================================================

Coverage is reported SEPARATELY for tests with at least one observed
failure and for tests with none, because the two groups behave
differently and an average over them would hide the second.

tests with at least one observed failure: 31
  the 95 per cent interval covers the generator's expectation: 30 of 31 = 0.967742
tests with no observed failure          : 41
  the interval covers the generator's expectation: 38 of 41 = 0.926829

THE SECOND GROUP IS THE PRODUCT'S OWN SUBJECT. For a test that never
failed in the window, every resample of the recorded sessions gives
zero, so the interval is [0, 0]. That is a correct statement about the
resampling distribution and a misleading statement about the test,
whose true rate is only bounded above. It is the reason baseline 2
exists: an analytic model extrapolates where an accumulation cannot.
Those tests with a true expectation above zero are counted here and
the under-coverage is published rather than averaged away.

  of the 41 never-failing tests, 3 have a true expected wasted time above zero
[PASS] coverage among tests with an observed failure is at least 0.70 -- 30/31 = 0.967742

The 0.70 floor is deliberately below the nominal 0.95 and is declared
here with its reason, not tuned to the measurement. Three effects pull
the realised coverage down and all three are properties of the problem:
the percentile bootstrap is uncorrected for the strong right skew of a
wasted-time total; the generator's expectation includes the runtime
distribution's mean while the accumulation saw a finite sample of it;
and a test with two or three recorded failures has a resampling
distribution with a handful of atoms, for which no percentile interval
can have calibrated coverage.

3. BASELINE 1's BLINDNESS TO RUNTIME, AS A MEASUREMENT
==============================================================================

every duration in the history is scaled. The ground truth scales

MEASUREMENT MODEL. The platform rail is VDD_IN, measured by the on-board INA3221 as bus voltage (mV) times shunt current (mA); P(t) = V(t)*I(t). Energy over a window is the TRAPEZOIDAL integral of P(t) over the sampler's CLOCK_MONOTONIC timestamps. A cycle is 2.0 s: the pipeline runs back-to-back inferences for duty*2.0 s and sleeps the rest; a run is 38 cycles. An inference is one pass over one frame, one row of the decisions CSV. A USEFUL DECISION is an inference that passes the gate -- a candidate exists, its box does not touch the border, its area is within the declared bounds, and its score is at least tau -- so the acceptance rate is counted from the committed CSV and never asserted. ENERGY PER USEFUL DECISION is the integral of P(t) over the WHOLE run, sleep windows included, divided by the number of accepted decisions in that run.

  E_RAMP IS NEGATIVE on 12 of the 15 grid cells, and that is a measurement of the instrument, not of the board. The INA3221 averages 512 conversions in hardware, so a reading taken within 0.15 s of a wake still carries the sleep window's power. The observable settling interval therefore shows a DEFICIT against the steady level that follows, not the excess a wake transient physically is. The figure is reported as measured: it is a bandwidth-limited estimate and the bandwidth limit is the instrument's. Nothing here is a rise time, and the settling window was not re-chosen after seeing this.
  The ramp is charged per WAKE and scaled by wakes/cycles. At duty 1.00 the active phases are contiguous, so the run has one wake and not 38; charging the per-wake figure to every cycle over-counted it 38-fold in the first implementation and the two-term identity in the last column found it.

a non-zero convexity count is the expected result and is reported, not fixed:
the objective of an integer program is a step function of capacity, so the
marginal value of contact time is not a derivative and not monotone in capacity.
the decreasing steps that cross an expiry are the other expected result: a
window's end is its delivery epoch, so a longer pass can be too late for a
product that the shorter pass would have delivered in time.

the two counts measure different claims. The first is the claim the report
makes: adding the stated seconds to the stated window frees enough room for
this product alongside everything already committed to that window, and it must
hold. The second is a question the report does not answer: re-solving with the
extra room may spend it on a different product of higher value, so a product
that fits is not a product that is chosen. The gap between the two counts is
the honest limit of a per-product shortfall number.

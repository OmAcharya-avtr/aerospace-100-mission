# Model card: acqsearch learned scan-order policy

Version 0.1.0, 2026-10-16. Status: TESTING.

**This model is not certified for operational flight use.**

**Headline result, stated before anything else: the analytic baseline beats
this model.** On 840 paired held-out episodes the Bayesian-greedy baseline is
faster by 0.3359 s of mean censored acquisition time at z = +7.53, and the
fixed spiral scan pattern is faster by 0.3455 s at z = +6.51. The model is
shipped because the measurement is the deliverable, not because the model is
recommended. If you need a scan-order policy, use the Bayesian-greedy one in
`acqsearch.policy.BayesianGreedyPolicy`, or the spiral pattern, which needs no
model at all.

## Problem

At each step of an acquisition search the terminal chooses which cell of the
pointing-uncertainty region to dwell on next. The state is the posterior over
target location given the misses so far, the current boresight position, which
sets the slew cost of every candidate, and the dwell history. The objective is
the expected time to the first detection, with non-acquisition charged at a
declared deadline.

## Baseline

The analytic baseline is **Bayesian-greedy**: exact Bayes update of the cell
posterior after each miss, then the cell maximising detection probability per
unit time.

```
P(target in j | miss at j) = P(j)(1 - q_j) / (1 - P(j) q_j)
P(target in i | miss at j) = P(i)          / (1 - P(j) q_j),   i != j
choose  argmax_i  P(i) q_i / c(i)
```

`q_i` is the cell detection probability, `c(i)` is the dwell plus slew plus
the expected false-alarm charge. This is the classical search-theory rule:
Koopman, B. O. (1980) *Search and Screening: General Principles with
Historical Applications*, Pergamon Press, Ch. 5-6; Stone, L. D. (1975)
*Theory of Optimal Search*, Academic Press, Ch. 2-3. It is myopic, and with a
non-zero slew cost it is not optimal, which is the gap this model was given a
chance to close.

A second reference is the **fixed spiral pattern**, which ignores the
posterior entirely and simply cycles a precomputed cell order.

The baseline was implemented, validated and benchmarked before any model was
fitted. `acqsearch.acquisition` and `acqsearch.coverage` contain no machine
learning of any kind.

## Architecture

`sklearn.ensemble.GradientBoostingRegressor`, three of them:

| model | loss | purpose |
|---|---|---|
| point | `squared_error` | predicted remaining time, used for the decision |
| low | `quantile`, alpha = 0.1 | lower end of the interval |
| high | `quantile`, alpha = 0.9 | upper end of the interval |

Each with `n_estimators=120`, `max_depth=4`, `learning_rate=0.1`,
`random_state=20261016`. The histogram variant was measured and rejected: the
policy is evaluated once per dwell inside an episode loop, so single-batch
prediction latency is the binding cost on two cores
(`HistGradientBoostingRegressor` 1.61 ms per 89-row batch against
`GradientBoostingRegressor` 0.27 ms, measured in the build session).

**Decision rule.** The model does not score the whole grid. It takes the top
`shortlist = 12` cells by Bayesian-greedy score and picks the one with the
smallest predicted remaining time. Only the point model is evaluated inside
the episode loop; the quantile models are evaluated by `decide`, which is what
a caller uses when it wants the interval.

### Features

Fourteen columns, one row per candidate cell.

| # | name | units | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `posterior` | dimensionless | posterior mass of this cell |
| 1 | `log_posterior` | dimensionless | `log10(posterior + 1e-12)` |
| 2 | `detection_probability` | dimensionless | `q_i` |
| 3 | `slew_time_ratio` | dimensionless | `(cost - dwell) / dwell` |
| 4 | `step_cost_s` | s | dwell + slew + expected false-alarm charge |
| 5 | `greedy_score_normalised` | dimensionless | greedy score divided by its maximum |
| 6 | `greedy_rank_fraction` | dimensionless | rank by greedy score, in [0, 1] |
| 7 | `cell_radius_fraction` | dimensionless | cell radius over grid extent |
| 8 | `visit_count` | dwells | times this cell has already been dwelled on |
| 9 | `remaining_posterior` | dimensionless | total posterior still inside the grid |
| 10 | `dwells_elapsed` | dwells | step index |
| 11 | `neighbour_posterior` | dimensionless | mean posterior of the four nearest cells |
| 12 | `current_radius_fraction` | dimensionless | current boresight radius over extent |
| 13 | `elapsed_over_period` | dimensionless | elapsed time over the nominal pass period |

### Target

Realised remaining time from the decision to the end of the episode, divided
by the scenario's nominal pass period, so episodes from scenarios with
different timing are comparable in one fit. Mean 0.6531, maximum 3.0000 over
the 12000 fitted rows.

## Dataset

300 scenarios generated by `acqsearch.scenarios.generate_scenarios(300,
20261016)` and committed as `data/scenarios.json`. See DATASET_CARD.md for the
declared ranges and their limitations. Nothing was measured from hardware.

**Split.** By `scenario_id`: ids 0-194 train (195 scenarios), ids 195-299 held
out (105 scenarios). Deterministic and reproducible from the file alone. No
held-out scenario contributes a single training row; this is asserted in
`tests/test_benchmark.py`.

## Training procedure

1. For each of the 195 training scenarios, roll out 2 episodes under an
   epsilon-greedy behaviour policy: `BayesianGreedyPolicy(epsilon=0.35,
   explore_shortlist=12)`, which explores uniformly inside the same 12-cell
   shortlist the model re-ranks at decision time.
2. Record `(feature row of the chosen cell, remaining time / nominal period)`
   at every decision.
3. Cap at 12000 rows by a seeded subsample (seed 20261016); the cap bound, so
   12000 of the rows produced were used.
4. Fit the three regressors on those rows.

No cross-validation, no hyperparameter search. The hyperparameters above were
chosen to fit the compute budget and were not tuned against the held-out set;
the only structural choice made after seeing a held-out number is the
shortlist, and its full ablation is published rather than only its best row.

## Metrics

Primary: mean episode time with non-acquisition charged at the scenario
deadline, which is three nominal pass periods. Reported beside it: the median,
the fraction acquired before the deadline, and the mean number of dwells.

Comparisons are **paired**: every policy sees the same scenarios, the same
drawn target cells and the same per-episode seed, so the difference is
measured on identical draws. The scenario-to-scenario spread is larger than
every gap in the table, so an unpaired comparison would be uninformative.

### Held-out results, 105 scenarios, 840 episodes per policy

| policy | mean (s) | s.e. (s) | median (s) | acquired | mean dwells |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fixed spiral | 2.1673 | 0.1437 | 0.7936 | 0.9881 | 45.2 |
| Bayesian-greedy | 2.1769 | 0.1492 | 0.7467 | 0.9893 | 45.0 |
| **learned (this model)** | **2.5128** | 0.1600 | 1.0970 | 0.9810 | 47.5 |
| fixed raster | 3.9036 | 0.2311 | 1.5647 | 0.9821 | 66.8 |
| fixed Lissajous | 4.5186 | 0.2908 | 1.9007 | 0.9702 | 71.6 |
| fixed random restart | 5.1877 | 0.2712 | 2.9111 | 0.8798 | 53.8 |

| paired comparison | mean difference (s) | s.e. (s) | z |
|---|---|---|---|

**The model loses to both references at more than six standard errors. The
two references cannot be told apart from each other.**

## What was tried, including what failed

- **Scoring the whole grid instead of a shortlist.** Measured, on the first 40
  held-out scenarios: 7.1102 s against 1.9560 s for the baseline, and the
  acquisition rate collapses from 0.9938 to 0.6094. The unconstrained learned
  policy wanders. This is the strongest single piece of evidence that the
  model has not learned the structure of the problem; the shortlist is a
  guardrail, not a feature.
- **Shortlist size.** Performance degrades monotonically in k: 2.0719 s at
  k = 4, 2.2143 s at k = 8, 2.3858 s at k = 12, 2.8390 s at k = 24, 7.1102 s
  at k = 89. At k = 1 the model has no choice left and reproduces the
  baseline, 1.9532 s against the baseline's 1.9560 s on the same episodes.
  Every row is published. The shipped k = 12 is the setting that matches the
  behaviour policy, not the best-scoring one, and the best-scoring one is
  still worse than the baseline.
- **`HistGradientBoostingRegressor`.** Rejected on prediction latency, not on
  accuracy; see Architecture.

## Uncertainty output

`LearnedScanPolicy.decide` returns a `PolicyDecision` carrying

- `predicted_remaining_s`, the point prediction in seconds,
- `interval_s`, the (0.1, 0.9) pinball-quantile interval in seconds,
- `confidence = 1 / (1 + width / max(point, dwell))`, dimensionless in (0, 1].

`confidence` is a monotone restatement of the interval width relative to the
prediction. It is not a probability and must not be thresholded as one.

**Measured calibration, 2006 held-out decisions: empirical coverage 0.7353
against a nominal 0.8000.** The interval is too narrow. Mean interval width
4.6773 s, mean reported confidence 0.3156. No recalibration is applied and
none is claimed.

## Failure cases

1. **It loses to a myopic rule with a closed form, and to a fixed pattern
   with no state at all.** That is the main one.
2. **Without the shortlist it fails outright**: 0.6094 acquisition rate
   against 0.9938 for the baseline.
3. **Its interval under-covers by 6.5 percentage points.**
4. **The training target is censored** at the deadline and the censoring is
   not modelled, so the model systematically under-predicts the remaining
   time of the episodes that matter most, the slow ones.
5. **It is fitted on independently sampled scenario parameters**, which
   produce terminals that could not be built. Its behaviour on a realistic
   correlated parameter set is unmeasured.
6. **It has never seen a non-stationary or biased pointing prior**, a
   correlated scintillation sequence, or a slew model with acceleration
   limits. All three are present in a real terminal.

## Reproducibility

```bash
python validation/validate_policies.py > validation/validate_policies_output.txt
```

103.3 s on two cores. Seeds, all committed in the source:

| seed | where | what it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| 20261016 | `generate_scenarios(300, 20261016)` | the 300 scenarios |
| 20261016 | `build_training_set(..., seed=20261016)` | behaviour rollouts and the row subsample |
| 20261016 | `LearnedScanPolicy(random_state=20261016)` | the three fitted regressors |
| 20261017 | `evaluate_policies(..., seed=20261017)` | held-out target draws and per-episode RNG |
| 20261016 + 7919 / 104729 / 1299709 * id | per scenario, in `benchmark.py` | behaviour, target and episode streams |

Nothing is pickled. The model is refitted from `data/scenarios.json` and the
seeds above every time it is used, which is why `pyproject.toml` declares no
dependency on a serialisation format. The scikit-learn bound
`>=1.9.0,<1.10` exists because `GradientBoostingRegressor` output is not
contractually stable across minor releases: scikit-learn 1.8.0 was executed
deliberately, the suite passes there, and the learned-policy fingerprint moves
by up to 17.7 percent (`validation/dependency_range_check_sklearn180_output.txt`).

## Compute used

Two cores, no GPU, Python 3.13.16. Whole-model cost:

| stage | wall clock |
|---|---|
| behaviour rollouts, 195 scenarios x 2 episodes, 12000 rows | 6.4 s (timed) |
| fitting the three regressors | 16.5 s (timed) |
| held-out evaluation, 6 policies x 840 episodes | 26.7 s (timed) |
| shortlist ablation (8 variants x 320 episodes) and interval calibration (2006 decisions) | 53.7 s, the remainder; these two are not timed separately |
| **total `validate_policies.py`** | **103.3 s (timed)** |

The three timed stages and the total are printed by the script itself and are
in `validation/validate_policies_output.txt`; the fourth row is the
difference, and it is labelled as such rather than presented as a
measurement.

The whole model is refitted in 23 s. No training run in this repository takes
longer than three minutes, and the shortlist ablation was reduced from 105 to
40 held-out scenarios specifically to stay inside that budget; that reduction
is stated in the output file rather than hidden.

## Ethical and safety limits

- **This model is not certified for operational flight use.** It is
  research-grade software for comparing acquisition strategies offline.
- It must not be placed in an acquisition control loop. It loses to a rule
  that can be written in four lines and audited by inspection, and the rule
  has a closed form and a published optimality argument where this does not.
- Its confidence output is not calibrated and must not gate any action.
- It is trained entirely on synthetic scenarios from declared parameter
  ranges. Nothing here establishes behaviour on a real terminal, and any
  number from it that is quoted in a design decision should carry that
  sentence with it.
- The honest summary is that this is evidence that a learned scan-order
  policy did not beat Bayesian-greedy on this problem, with the measurement
  protocol and the losing numbers published so the experiment can be argued
  with. It is not a component to deploy.

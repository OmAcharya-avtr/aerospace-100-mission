3. Shortlist ablation for the learned policy
The learned policy re-ranks the top-k cells by Bayesian-greedy score. k = 89
is the whole grid, which is the unconstrained learned policy. The model is
the same fit in every row; only the candidate set at decision time changes,
so k = 12 matches the behaviour policy that produced the training rows and
the others do not. That mismatch is part of what the larger k values cost.
Run on the first 40 held-out scenarios rather than all 105, to keep
the whole script inside the three-minute compute budget. The two reference
policies are re-run on the same subset so the column is internally
comparable; the k = 12 row is therefore not identical to the table above,
and both are reported rather than one being substituted for the other.
k = 1 leaves the learned model exactly one candidate, which is the cell
Bayesian-greedy would have chosen, so that row IS the baseline.

MEASUREMENT MODEL
-----------------
  Hardware-mode figures come from the committed campaign in data/traces/, run on
  a Jetson Orin Nano: nine grid cells, 60 passes of 1500 frames each, 90000 timed
  frames per cell, 810000 frames in all, with the cell order randomised under seed
  20261115. Each of the six stages is bracketed individually by
  time.perf_counter_ns, whose resolution the campaign measured at 1.000 ns and
  whose empty-bracket cost it measured per cell at 107.365 to 115.204 ns. A pass
  summary is computed over EVERY timed frame of that pass; data/traces/stages.csv
  is subsampled every third frame and any figure taken from it says so. EVERY
  HARDWARE FIGURE HERE IS A FIGURE AT AN UNPINNED, SCHEDUTIL-GOVERNED CLOCK IN
  MAXN_SUPER: passwordless sudo was unavailable on the board, so neither the
  governor nor the power mode could be pinned, per-core frequency spanned 729600
  to 1728000 kHz across the campaign, and the share of per-stage variance that
  co-varies with core frequency is measured and reported rather than assumed away.
  The board also drifted: a fixed 160x160 float64 matmul reference, sharing no code
  with the pipeline, measured 141844.5 ns before the first cell and 152517.0 ns
  after the last, a ratio of 1.0752408447278534, and every divergence ratio is
  reported raw AND drift-adjusted with the raw figure as the headline.
  Simulation-mode figures are measured in the analysis container, now, by the same
  instrumented driver over the same committed pipeline file, and move between runs
  because they are wall-clock readings.

2. is SPEC §4's premise true: are adjacent frames serially correlated
==============================================================================
  Measured on the committed per-frame table, which is SUBSAMPLED EVERY 3 FRAMES
  (meta.json: raw_sample_every). A lag-1 autocorrelation on a 3-subsampled series is the
  correlation at a true lag of 3 frames, so it UNDERSTATES the adjacent-frame correlation.

  cell w64_sparse, 30000 subsampled rows over 60 passes

  stage        lag-1 autocorrelation (within-pass, 3-subsampled)

[PASS] at least one stage shows a positive within-pass serial correlation -- positive for ingest, threshold, centroid, gate, track
[FINDING] SPEC §4's premise is only PARTLY supported by this measurement, and the measurement is published as it is. Lag-1 autocorrelation within a pass, on the 3-subsampled series, is positive for 5 of 6 stages (ingest, threshold, centroid, gate, track) and the largest is +0.537079 (track). These are small numbers. Two reasons they understate the real serial dependence, both structural: the committed series is subsampled every third frame, so a lag-1 correlation here is a true lag of 3; and the quantity the bootstrap resamples is a PASS MEDIAN over 1500 frames, whose dependence on the governor and thermal state of that pass is not visible in within-pass frame-to-frame correlation at all. The pass unit is therefore defended by the between-pass structure measured in step 3 rather than by these autocorrelations.

[FALSIFIED] SPEC §4's stated reason -- resampling frames would UNDERSTATE the interval -- measured on w64_sparse, the frame-unit interval is narrower on 2 of 6 stages (background, track) and WIDER on 4 (ingest, threshold, centroid, gate), by up to a factor of 15.03. The direction is stage-dependent, so the stated reason does not hold as a general claim about this campaign
[PASS] the falsification of "SPEC §4's stated reason -- resampling frames would UNDERSTATE the interval" is exactly the pinned one -- pinned narrower ['background', 'track'], pinned wider ['centroid', 'gate', 'ingest', 'threshold']; this script exits 0 while the falsification is exactly this partition and non-zero the moment a stage moves between the two sets
[FINDING] SPEC §4's REASON IS FALSIFIED WHILE ITS DECISION STANDS, and both halves are published. The specification says a frame-unit bootstrap would understate the interval. Measured on w64_sparse, the frame-unit width is 0.000 to 15.035 times the pass-unit width, median 1.455: narrower on background, track and WIDER on ingest, threshold, centroid, gate. The mechanism for the wider cases is that the per-frame distribution of threshold and centroid is heavily right-tailed, because both stages cost in proportion to a candidate count that varies frame to frame, so a median over 30000 raw frames is less stable than a median over 60 pass medians. THE DECISION TO USE THE PASS IS STILL RIGHT, for a reason the specification does not give: the pass is the unit over which the governor and thermal state are approximately constant, and the quantity a schedulability budget consumes is a typical PASS's per-stage time, not a typical frame's. A frame-unit interval answers a different question. The specification's justification is wrong about this campaign and its choice of unit is not.

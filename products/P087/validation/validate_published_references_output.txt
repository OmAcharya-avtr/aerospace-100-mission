Three kinds of reference appear below and they are not equally strong:
  published_value  a number stated in a published source at stated conditions
  published_claim  an accuracy claim an author made about their own formula,
                   relative to a computation this package does not implement
  convention       a figure adopted by an authority as a working convention

This product ships NO transcription of a printed refraction table. No such
table was available to this build and inventing plausible entries would be
worse than having none. That is the main weakness of the external
verification here and it is stated rather than papered over.

horizon_refraction_convention

  conditions : standard conditions, apparent elevation zero
  citation   : the 34 arcmin refraction adopted with 16 arcmin of semidiameter to give the 50 arcmin standard depression for rise and set; Explanatory Supplement to the Astronomical Almanac, 3rd ed., Urban and Seidelmann (eds.), 2013
  tolerance  : 60, declared by this product at one arcminute, which is the precision at which the convention itself is stated; the real quantity varies by several arcminutes with the surface lapse rate, as equation (23) measures

The profile envelope of equation (23) at this elevation is 301.3 arcsec = 5.022 arcmin, which is 5.02 times the tolerance of this check: dry_adiabatic 32.3346 arcmin, us_standard_1976 34.0286 arcmin, isothermal 37.3562 arcmin. Agreement here therefore says very little.
  note       : Agreement here is weak evidence and disagreement is weaker still: the adopted figure is a rounded convention and the quantity it stands for is the most profile-sensitive number this package computes.

bennett_accuracy_claim
  quantity   : maximum error of equation (15) over 0 to 90 deg, arcsec
  conditions : 1010 hPa, 10 C, apparent elevation 0 to 90 deg
  citation   : Bennett, G. G. (1982), J. Navigation 35(2), 255-259, relative to Garfinkel, B. (1967), Astronomical Journal 72, 235-254; quoted in Meeus, J. (1998), Astronomical Algorithms, 2nd ed., ch. 16
  tolerance  : 0, none: the published figure is itself the tolerance, so the check is whether the measured maximum departure is at or below it
  measured   : 34.07369219
  detail     : over 181 elevations from 0.0 to 90.0 deg at 1010 hPa, 10 C, dry and 550 nm, the maximum absolute departure of equation (15) from equation (19) is 34.074 arcsec = 0.5679 arcmin, at 0.0000 deg. The published claim is 0.07 arcmin = 4.20 arcsec. The claim holds at and above 19.1361 deg and fails below it. At 633 nm instead of 550 nm the maximum departure is 44.500 arcsec, so the wavelength this product chooses for a formula that carries none moves the measured departure by +10.427 arcsec.
  note       : The claim was made against Garfinkel (1967), which this package does not implement. The check measures equation (15) against this package's ray trace instead. A miss does not show Bennett wrong; it shows that the two reference atmospheres differ by more than 0.07 arcmin wherever it misses, and equation (23) says how much freedom there is in that choice.

==============================================================================
the failing check, in detail
==============================================================================
Bennett (1982) states a maximum error of 0.07 arcmin = 4.20 arcsec over 0 to
90 degrees, relative to Garfinkel (1967). This package does not implement
Garfinkel (1967). The departure measured below is therefore against this
package's own ray trace through a US Standard Atmosphere 1976 profile, which
is a DIFFERENT reference atmosphere. The miss is published as measured and
is not evidence that Bennett's formula is wrong.

Why the two reference atmospheres may legitimately differ by this much is
measured in validation/validate_profile_sensitivity.py: the three declared
surface lapse rates of equation (23) move the horizon refraction by far more
than 34 arcsec on their own, so a 34 arcsec difference between two
atmosphere models at zero elevation is inside the modelling freedom and
this package cannot attribute it.

The two cone implementations were written independently:
  KeepOut    ExclusionCone.margin  = angular_separation(b, axis) - half_angle
             with angular_separation = atan2(|a x b|, a . b)
  SlewForge  KeepOutCone.margin     = the same definition, own code, plus a
             closed-form arc test KeepOut does not have
Agreement on the point test is therefore a check on two independent
transcriptions of textbook geometry, not on shared code.

The two are NOT bit-identical, and the tolerance above says so. The
residual is floating-point association: KeepOut forms the separation as
np.arctan2(np.linalg.norm(np.cross(a, b)), np.sum(a * b)) while SlewForge
uses its own cross3 and math.sqrt, so the last bit of the cross-product
norm can differ. 8.9e-16 rad is 1.8e-10 arcsec; it changes no verdict in
20 000 trials, and claiming bit-equality would have been wrong.

Both columns rise with fs by a factor of about sqrt(fs). That is the
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process having no finite mean-square derivative, not a
defect: the continuous-time crossing rate does not exist.

The 'in SE' column is the deviation in units of the Poisson standard error
of the crossing count, 1/sqrt(N). Rows within about 3 SE are consistent
with convergence; the discrete rate is biased slightly low at small Lc
because sampling misses short excursions, and that bias shrinks with Lc.
All four rows share one seed, so their offsets are correlated; the claim
being made is that the offset does not GROW with fs, which section 1 shows
it does for the exp kernel.

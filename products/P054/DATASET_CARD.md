# Dataset card — rareverify surrogate training data

**Package:** `rareverify` 0.1.0 · **Generator:**
`rareverify.surrogate.radial_design` and `rareverify.limitstates`

## Summary

Fully synthetic. Nothing is downloaded, nothing is committed as data, and no
file in this repository contains a dataset. Every training set is generated at
fit time by committed code from a fixed seed, so the whole of it is reproduced
by re-running the script that used it.

## Generation

```python
design  = radial_design(dimension, n_train, p_prior=1e-4,
                        radius_scale=1.4, rng=np.random.default_rng(seed))
targets = limit_state.g(design)
```

- **Inputs.** Directions uniform on the unit sphere in `R^d` (standard normal
  vectors normalised to unit length), radii uniform on
  `[0, radius_scale * |Phi^-1(p_prior)|]`. At the defaults that is
  `[0, 5.2066]` standard-normal units. Shape `(n_train, d)`, dtype float64,
  dimensionless.
- **Targets.** The true limit-state value `g(x)` at each design point, in the
  units of that limit state. One true simulator evaluation per row; the row
  count is exactly the evaluation budget the surrogate spends.
- **Sizes used.** `n_train` of 20 to 300 in the validation scripts, 100 in the
  headline benchmark, capped at 2000 by `rareverify.surrogate.MAX_TRAIN`
  because a Gaussian-process fit is cubic in the design size.
- **Determinism.** Every array comes from `numpy.random.default_rng(seed)` with
  the seed recorded in the script. The largest design used anywhere here is
  2000 × 10 float64 = 160 kB, so nothing approaches the 1 MB threshold above
  which data would need a separate regeneration step.

## Source limit states

| Limit state | Definition | Reference probability |
|---|---|---|
| `LinearGaussianLimitState` | `g = beta - a.x`, `|a| = 1` | `Phi(-beta)`, exact |
| `LognormalRatioLimitState` | `g = exp(mu_R + sigma_R x0) - exp(mu_S + sigma_S x1)` | `Phi(-beta)`, exact, because the failure event is linear in `x` |
| `RippledLimitState` | `g = beta - a.x + A sin(w b.x)`, `a` ⟂ `b` | `E_v[Phi(-beta - A sin(w v))]` by 400-node Gauss-Hermite quadrature |

All three are defined on independent standard normal inputs ("u-space"), which
is the convention of structural reliability. A model whose inputs are not
Gaussian is assumed to have been transformed already; no transformation is
implemented in this package.

## What the design encodes, and why that matters

The radial design uses `p_prior`, a declared prior order of magnitude for the
failure probability, to set its maximum radius. **This is prior information
given to the surrogate and it is material.** A design drawn from the input
distribution itself would place essentially no point within reach of a `1e-4`
failure boundary — at `beta = 3.719` the probability of a standard normal
sample exceeding radius 3.7 in two dimensions is about 1e-3 — and the surrogate
would learn nothing about the region that matters.

The analytic baseline it is compared against is given the exact design point,
which is strictly more information. Neither side is operating blind, and the
comparison in [MODEL_CARD.md](MODEL_CARD.md) is of what each does with the
prior knowledge it has.

## Limitations of the data

- **It is not data about anything.** These are synthetic mathematical limit
  states chosen because their failure probabilities are known to far better
  accuracy than any Monte-Carlo estimate of them. That is what makes them
  usable as known answers; it is also what makes them unrepresentative of a
  real simulator, which is expensive, noisy, and not smooth.
- **Dimension 2 to 10 only.** Every reported surrogate result is from a
  two-dimensional instance. The dimensional behaviour of the Gaussian-process
  fit is not characterised here.
- **One connected failure region per instance.** No multi-modal, disconnected
  or strongly curved failure region appears in any training set, and the
  mean-shift family the surrogate feeds is known to be poorly suited to those.
- **Noise-free targets.** `g` is evaluated exactly. A real simulator with
  stochastic internals would need the white-noise kernel term fitted rather
  than driven to its lower bound, and none of the measured accuracy here
  transfers to that case.
- **No distribution shift, by construction.** The evaluation samples are drawn
  from the tilted proposal, which is a different distribution from the radial
  training design; the surrogate is therefore queried off its training
  distribution every time it is used, and
  `SurrogateFit.straddle_fraction` is the diagnostic provided for that. It was
  measured at 0.0000 to 0.0040 around the design point in the validation run.

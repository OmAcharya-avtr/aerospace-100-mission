4. does the closed form degrade with range, as light time suggests?
==============================================================================
  The curvature of the remote trajectory over the light time grows with
  range, so a reader would expect the closed form to get worse. It barely
  does, and the reason is a cancellation: the transverse velocity at the
  advanced epoch is larger than at the nominal epoch by a_perp tau, and at
  the retarded epoch it is smaller by the same amount, so the two
  first-order curvature errors cancel in the SUM that forms the offset.
  That is a first-order-exact cancellation and it is measured below.

==============================================================================
6. verdict
==============================================================================
  The closed-form first-order baseline WINS on every metric that matters
  for the point-ahead offset. Over 300 seeded crosslinks its worst
  disagreement with the iterated reference is 7.340e-14 rad, which is
  4.826e-09 of the angle itself and is of the same
  order as the float64 direction resolution of the geometry. It needs no
  trajectory model and no iteration.

  What the reference is for, and it is not nothing: it is the only way to
  obtain the two light-time budget lines at all, it is what makes the
  numerical sensitivity checks in validate_budget.py possible, and it is
  required for the absolute transmit direction when a terminal cannot
  close the loop on a received beam -- the open-loop error of the closed
  form is 6.979e-10 rad at worst, one to two orders
  above its closed-loop error.

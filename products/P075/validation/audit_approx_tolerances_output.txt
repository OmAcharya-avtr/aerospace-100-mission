  pytest.approx(expected, rel=r) carries a DEFAULT ABSOLUTE TOLERANCE of
  1e-12 and passes on EITHER bound. For this package that matters: the
  unreliability figures here are routinely of order 1e-5 and go down to
  1e-18, so a relative tolerance tighter than about 2e-8 on a 1e-5
  quantity is silently replaced by the absolute default.

the answer, stated plainly
==============================================================================

  tests in the suite                 : 454
  assertions relying on the 1e-12 default : 0

  The suite uses two helpers for every comparison:
    rel(expected, tolerance)  -> pytest.approx(expected, rel=t, abs=0.0)
    abs_(expected, tolerance) -> pytest.approx(expected, rel=0.0, abs=t)

  so the relative checks are relative and the absolute checks are
  absolute, and neither can be replaced by the other without the test
  text changing.

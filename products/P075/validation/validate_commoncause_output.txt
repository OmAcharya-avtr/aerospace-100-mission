==============================================================================
where the beta-factor model stops being conservative
==============================================================================

  For k = 1 the beta factor always increases Q. For k >= 2 it can
  decrease Q, because the model's single common-cause mode fails all n
  members while the group only needs n - k + 1 failures. The table
  below locates the member cumulative hazard at which the ratio crosses
  1, by bisection, at beta = 0.1.

    k  n  needed   ratio at H=0.01  ratio at H=3    crossing H                   verdict
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    1  2       2         10.905368      1.000960          none     conservative everywhere
    1  3       3       1015.322358      1.002914          none     conservative everywhere
    2  3       2          4.198132      0.997584      0.302944  crosses: conservative below
    1  4       4     101968.098219      1.005896          none     conservative everywhere
    2  4       3        256.285271      0.995519      0.653729  crosses: conservative below
    3  4       2          2.516174      0.999621      0.124016  crosses: conservative below
    1  5       5   10247813.431300      1.009942          none     conservative everywhere
    2  5       4      20557.783027      0.993070      0.924649  crosses: conservative below
    3  5       3        103.720793      0.999115      0.340012  crosses: conservative below
    4  5       2          1.841492      0.999958      0.067949  crosses: conservative below
    1  6       6 1029913730.774888      1.015093          none     conservative everywhere
    2  6       5    1722249.954759      0.990350      1.141930  crosses: conservative below
    3  6       4       6907.998017      0.998346      0.533470  crosses: conservative below
    4  6       3         52.612956      0.999882      0.217911  crosses: conservative below
    5  6       2          1.503309      0.999996      0.042978  crosses: conservative below

  (k, n) pairs with a crossing       : 10
  beta used                          : 0.1 (a declared input, not data)

  Reading the table: 'needed' is n - k + 1, the number of failures the
  group needs. Where it equals n -- that is, k = 1 -- the beta factor is
  conservative at every hazard and there is no crossing. Where it is
  less than n, there is a hazard above which the beta factor REDUCES the
  computed unreliability.

  k = n is absent from the table because Diagram.validate now REFUSES a
  beta factor there. That refusal was added because an earlier run of
  this sweep measured k = n as non-conservative at every hazard -- a
  ratio of 0.918939 at k = n = 6 -- which is correct arithmetic for a
  series group and meaningless as a redundancy model. The refusal
  message quotes that measured ratio.
  [PASS] a parallel group (k = 1) has no crossing: the beta factor is always conservative -- 0 crossings found at k = 1
  [PASS] a k-of-n group with k >= 2 does have a crossing, which is the limitation -- 10 crossings located

  reliablock.commoncause.group_beta_effects reports this per group, and
  reliablock.report.common_cause_comparison prints a WARNING naming the
  alpha-factor and multiple-Greek-letter models when any group crosses.
  Neither is implemented here. The limitation is surfaced on the
  declaration rather than left in a document.

==============================================================================
refusals
==============================================================================

  The beta-factor model is symmetric: one shared event fails the whole
  group, and lambda_common = beta lambda needs one lambda. A group whose
  members do not share one distribution has no single lambda, and taking
  the mean, minimum or maximum gives three different answers with no
  principled basis. It is refused.

  message: node 'pair' declares beta_factor=0.1 over members that do not share one distribution: ['b'] differ from 'a'. The beta-factor model is symmetric -- one shared event fails the whole group, and lambda_common = beta lambda needs one lambda. Taking the mean, minimum or maximum of the members' rates gives three different answers with no principled basis. A non-identical group needs an alpha-factor or multiple-Greek-letter model, which this package does not implement (see NUREG/CR-4780); it is refused here rather than approximated silently.
  [PASS] a non-identical group is refused and the message names the alternative model

  A standby or shared-load node has no beta_factor field at all: it is
  already a dependent-failure model, and compounding it with a beta
  factor needs a combined Markov model this package does not have.
  standby fields     : ['kind', 'label', 'primary', 'standby_hazard_fraction', 'standbys', 'switch_reliability']
  shared_load fields : ['kind', 'label', 'shared_rate_factor', 'units']
  [PASS] neither standby nor shared_load accepts a beta factor

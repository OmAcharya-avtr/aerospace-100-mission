WHAT IS AND IS NOT ASSERTED HERE, and this is the whole point.

The headline is NOT asserted to be identical across two runs. It
cannot be. An audited script that is intermittently non-deterministic
AT A FIXED SEED moves the count between back-to-back runs, and finding
exactly that is what this product is for. Asserting equality would be
asserting that the audited corpus is deterministic, which is the claim
this product exists to refute. A run of this script under the release
gate's environment measured 110 against 111 with identical coverage,
and the assertion that they must match was removed rather than the
observation being explained away. Any script named above is a script
whose committed numbers are not reproducible at its declared seed.

What IS asserted is the part this product controls: that the coverage,
the evaluated token count and the set of contributing scripts are
identical, and that the drift stays far below the declared ceiling at
which a single run would stop being informative.
[PASS] the audit's coverage on a bounded subset is identical across two runs -- {'compared': 5, 'run_failed': 3} against {'compared': 5, 'run_failed': 3}
[PASS] the evaluated token count is identical across two runs -- 300 against 300
[PASS] the set of contributing scripts is identical across two runs -- 5 against 5 scripts
[PASS] the headline drift between two runs is below the declared ceiling -- 0.000000 against 0.05

WHERE VARIATION COMES FROM, when it does. Two mechanisms, both real:
a per-run TIMEOUT is wall-clock and depends on machine load, so a
candidate can move between `timeout` and `compared` between runs and
take its findings with it; and an audited script can itself be
non-deterministic, which is the thing being looked for. The audit
cannot tell those apart from one run, and it does not pretend to.
The coverage check above separates them: coverage identical with a
moved headline is the SECOND mechanism and nothing else.

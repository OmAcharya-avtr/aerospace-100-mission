C. omitting a term that the data contain
==============================================================================
The model is fitted with eight columns while the truth has nine. The
omitted term's signal has to go somewhere, and where it goes is the
measurement. There are two places it can go, and the control tests for
BOTH, because an omission that goes to only one of them would pass a
control that tested only the other:

  (i)  into the remaining coefficients, as a bias -- measured as the largest
       |bias| / se over the eight remaining terms, with the standard errors
       taken from the DECLARED sigma so that the quantity asks whether the
       bias would be visible against the noise the user declared. The
       criterion follows each term's own conditioning through its se rather
       than being an absolute number of arcseconds.
  (ii) into the post-fit residual, as unexplained signal -- measured against
       the correct model's own post-fit residual on the same data.

An omission is DETECTABLE when either exceeds its criterion. The absolute
bias in arcseconds is reported beside both, because it is what a reader of a
pointing report would see.

Read the AN and AW rows. Omitting either leaves a 3 to 5 arcsec post-fit
residual and barely biases the remaining coefficients at all -- |bias|/se of
3.36 and 1.01 against 152 for IA. That is because the azimuth-axis tilt
columns are nearly orthogonal to every other column on a full-sky geometry,
where their variance inflation factor is close to one. It is a useful fact
about this model: a missing tilt term shows up in the residual and not in
the other coefficients, while a missing index or refraction term corrupts
the coefficients and can leave the residual almost clean. A control that
tested only one of the two routes would have missed half of these.

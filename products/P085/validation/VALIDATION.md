# Validation

**Validation level 2.** Every number below was produced by a script in this
directory, executed on 2026-11-01, and every script writes its raw output
beside itself as `<script>_output.txt`. No figure here is carried from an
earlier session, estimated, or rounded by hand. Where a check failed or a
result is unfavourable to this package, it is in the table with the others.

Environment, as recorded in the header of every output file: Python 3.13.18,
numpy 2.5.3, scipy 1.18.1, matplotlib 3.11.2, pytest 9.1.1, hypothesis
6.170.14, ruff 0.17.3, two CPU cores. `astropy` is named in the README's
alternatives table and is **not installed**; no number in this repository is
compared against it, and the independent refraction calculation used as a
cross-check is written in `src/pointmodel/refraction.py`.

Every residual set used here is synthetic and its truth is generated by
`pointmodel.geometry.exact_residuals`, an exact composition of rotation
matrices that shares no expression with the design matrix being fitted. **No
measurement of any real instrument appears anywhere in this repository.**

## 1. Term-by-term recovery

`validate_term_recovery.py`. 300 observations, truth from the exact rotation
model, fitted with the first-order design matrix.

**The order of the truncation is derived, not measured.** Five terms have no
truncation at all: `IA` is a rotation about the untilted vertical by exactly
`IA`, and `IE`, `TF`, `TS` and `RF` enter only through the effective elevation,
so in each case the exact beam is the nominal beam at a shifted angle. `AN` and
`AW` are quadratic because a finite rotation of a unit vector departs from its
first-order approximation at second order. `CA` and `NPAE` are cubic because
their second-order effect falls in the elevation block, where the
elevation-side columns absorb it.

## 2. The design matrix against an independently derived bound

`validate_linearisation.py`. This is the check class the build guide calls out
as distinct: not "two implementations agree to a tolerance I chose" but "the
answer lies inside a bound derived from different mathematics".

The bound is the operator-norm Taylor remainder of a product of matrix
exponentials, `B(s) = exp(s + s² exp(s)/2) − 1 − s` with `s` the sum of the
five small rotation magnitudes. It has no fitted constant and no slack term,
and its tolerance follows the conditioning of the problem through `s²`.

| amplitude ″ | worst error (vector norm) | bound B(s) | error/bound | violations of 400 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.697412e-09 | 4.716205e-09 | **0.388813** | 0 |
| 10 | 2.210219e-07 | 5.935691e-07 | **0.372361** | 0 |
| 100 | 5.762266e-06 | 1.794489e-05 | **0.344846** | 0 |
| 1000 | 2.283529e-03 | 5.945080e-03 | **0.385712** | 0 |
| 10000 | 5.383570e-02 | 2.841863e-01 | **0.262713** | 0 |

Never violated, never vacuous, and stable to within a factor of 1.48 across
five decades. The truncation's fitted exponent against `log(amplitude)` is
**1.999440** against a derived value of 2.

**The bound's resolution, stated rather than discovered.** `B(s)` admits
`B(s) × 206265` arcsec of sky error, so a corruption smaller than that cannot
be detected by it. At 10 arcsec coefficients the smallest detectable relative
column error, found by bisection, is:

| term | IA | IE | CA | AN | AW | NPAE | TF | TS | RF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| smallest detected column error, % | 0.375562 | 0.208311 | 0.190248 | 0.189408 | 0.191879 | 0.190417 | 0.374678 | 0.208496 | 0.118757 |

**27 controls.** Nine sign flips at 100 arcsec coefficients: all nine fired,
on 198 to 200 of 200 observations. Nine 1 per cent scale errors at 10 arcsec
coefficients: all nine fired. Nine one-part-per-million scale errors at 100
arcsec coefficients, declared **must not fire** because they are thirty
thousand times inside the bound: none fired. The uncorrupted design matrix
violates the bound on 0 of 200 observations at both amplitudes.

## 3. Refraction against an independent calculation

`validate_refraction.py`. Bennett (1982) as published by Meeus (1998) chapter
16 equation (16.4), against a numerical integration of the refraction integral
through a US Standard Atmosphere 1976 profile, **written in this repository**.
The two share no mathematics: one is a two-parameter arctangent fit in the
altitude domain, the other is a quadrature of Snell's invariant through a
hydrostatic atmosphere. No refraction package is installed and none is called.

**The shape is what the design matrix uses**, and the fitted `RF` coefficient
carries the scale, so the shape comparison is what bears on the product and is
independent of the refractivity constant.

| quantity | radio dry (77.6 K/hPa) | optical 550 nm (78.9586 K/hPa) |
|---|---|---|
| max relative shape difference, 10–85° | **1.964086e-02** | 1.984431e-02 |
| max \|absolute difference\| | **8.4736″** | **4.1270″** |
| mean Bennett / ray trace | 1.043524 | 1.025533 |
| inside Meeus's quoted 0.07′ = 4.2″ | **NO** | yes |

**Reported as measured, including the part that does not pass.** With the
optical constant the absolute agreement is inside the published accuracy with
1.7 per cent of margin, which is thin and is stated rather than rounded away.
With the dry-air radio constant it exceeds it by a factor of 2.02, and the
excess is accounted for: the two constants differ by **1.7508 per cent** and
the measured mean ratios differ by **1.7543 per cent**. Bennett's formula is an
optical fit; a reader fitting a radio antenna's pointing model is using it
outside the wavelength it was fitted for, and at 10 degrees elevation that
mismatch is 5.7 arcsec.

**The plane-parallel limit**, which needs no quadrature at all, pins the sign
and the leading factor. The departure of the spherical integral from
`(n₀−1) tan z` is derivable from Snell's invariant as `−(H/R⊕) sec² z` with
`H = R_specific T₀/g = 8288.1514 m` and `H/R⊕ = 1.300918e-03`:

| apparent el | measured departure | predicted −(H/R)sec²z | measured/predicted |
|---|---|---|---|
| 88° | −1.299572e-03 | −1.302505e-03 | 0.9977 |
| 85° | −1.307011e-03 | −1.310876e-03 | 0.9971 |
| 80° | −1.334105e-03 | −1.341366e-03 | 0.9946 |
| 70° | −1.451260e-03 | −1.473257e-03 | 0.9851 |
| 60° | −1.683148e-03 | −1.734558e-03 | 0.9704 |
| 50° | −2.110418e-03 | −2.216880e-03 | 0.9520 |

The quadrature's own reported absolute error is below **2.923e-09 arcsec**
everywhere measured, so none of the agreement above can be attributed to the
integration.

**Does the refusal catch what is actually wrong.** The criterion is declared in

| k declared sigmas | tp | fp | fn | tn | recall | precision |

identifiable combinations are reported in its place.

## 5. Robust fits and the disagreement diagnostic

`validate_robust.py`. 300 observations, 6 seeds per point, failures at 300
arcsec applied to both components of a failed acquisition.

| contamination | failed acquisitions | least squares worst \|error\| ″ | Huber | RANSAC | expected clean subsets in 200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 0 | 5.5382 | 5.3938 | 5.5564 | 200.0000 |
| 1% | 3 | 74.4646 | 5.3033 | 5.5555 | 152.4685 |
| 2% | 6 | 63.2416 | 6.2619 | 5.6045 | 115.9135 |
| 5% | 15 | 133.6593 | 6.9466 | 6.3006 | 50.0688 |
| 10% | 30 | 197.6273 | 6.2775 | 5.4509 | 11.6299 |
| 20% | 60 | 260.9624 | 6.8855 | **52.6930** | 0.4836 |
| 35% | 105 | 352.6399 | **12.6291** | **583.2235** | 0.0018 |
| 50% | 150 | 330.5132 | **83.1670** | **1167.8518** | 0.0000 |

**RANSAC collapses between 10 and 20 per cent and the breakdown point is
derivable from its own parameters.** A trial succeeds only if its subset is
free of failures, probability `(1−f)^27`, so in 200 trials the expected number
of clean subsets falls below one at `f = 1 − (1/200)^(1/27) = 0.1784`. The
measured breakdown sits exactly where that expectation crosses one. A larger
subset is better conditioned and less robust; that is a real trade and the
subset size is an argument, not a hidden default.

**The cost of robustness on clean data** is 4.5 per cent at worst: Huber's
standard errors are between 1.000 and 1.0448 times the least-squares ones, and
Huber's nominal 95 per cent Gaussian efficiency predicts 2.6 per cent.

**Outlier identification against the injected mask.** Observation-wise recall
is 1.0000 at every contamination level tested. Row-wise recall falls to 0.9500
at 10 per cent and that is expected, not a miss: the displacement is 300 arcsec
in a uniformly random direction, so one of its two components can be
arbitrarily small and that row is indistinguishable from a clean one by any
method. RANSAC's false positive rate on clean rows is 0.0000 up to 10 per cent
contamination and **0.6188 at 20 per cent**, which is the same breakdown.

**The diagnostic**, measured as the root mean square of `A(θ₁ − θ₂)` over the
observations actually made, threshold one declared sigma:

| contamination | failed acquisitions | Huber predicted sky rms ″ | flagged | RANSAC ″ | flagged |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0% | 0 | 0.0563 | no | 0.0000 | no |
| 0.5% | 1 | 1.5101 | no | 1.5581 | no |
| 1.0% | 3 | 1.7821 | no | 1.8136 | no |
| 2.0% | 6 | **2.9579** | YES | **2.9765** | YES |
| 5.0% | 15 | 7.6439 | YES | 7.7610 | YES |
| 10.0% | 30 | 7.9183 | YES | 8.0696 | YES |

The smallest contamination flagged is **2 per cent, six failed acquisitions out
of 300**. A flag means the two fits describe measurably different skies; it
does not say which is right, and this package does not decide.

**Robustness does not repair a degeneracy.** On a contaminated single-azimuth
run the diagnostic still fires, the refusal still applies to both fits, and the
Huber estimate recovers the separable terms to 7.6340 arcsec where least
squares reaches 93.6977.

which differs from Bennett's by up to 2.0 per cent, does **not** fire. Without
that second control the first would only show that the column had changed, not
that it had become wrong.

## 7. The `pytest.approx` default-tolerance audit

`validate_approx_audit.py`. `pytest.approx(expected, rel=r)` passes if **either**
bound is met and the absolute default is `1e-12`, so a bare `rel=` on a small
quantity silently stops testing what it says.

- **Static scan**: 0 of the suite's `approx` calls carrying `rel=` lack an
  explicit `abs=`.
- **Strict run**, with `pytest.approx` patched so a bare `rel=` raises: 241
  tests, 0 failures, 0 errors. **The count of assertions depending on the 1e-12
  default is 0.**
- **Zero run**, with every explicit `abs=` forced to `0.0`: 241 tests, **9
  failures**. Those nine are the assertions for which the absolute bound is the
  one doing the work. Six compare against an exact zero, where a relative bound
  has no meaning. The other three are named in the output file with their
  reasons: a tolerance derived from the solve's condition number, a root-finding
  residual, and an absolute second-order truncation of
  `−(CA²/2)tan(E)` arcsec.

## 8. The command line and the tree

`validate_cli.py`. Nine commands in subprocesses, each with `HOME`,
`MPLCONFIGDIR` and `HYPOTHESIS_STORAGE_DIRECTORY` outside the repository and
`PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1`. All nine exit 0 and print every expected fragment.
Five malformed invocations are controls and all five are rejected with exit 2.

- **gitignore coverage**: every cache directory pattern present in the tree is
  covered by `.gitignore` in the form that actually covers it. A pattern with
  no slash matches at any depth; one with a leading slash matches only at the
  root, which is the P060 defect of this mission.
- **This script's own subprocesses add no cache directory**, measured by a
  snapshot before and after.
- **Absolute paths**: 64 text files scanned on a clean tree, **0 offenders**.
  The scan skips anything inside a directory `.gitignore` excludes, because a
  Hypothesis example database is full of absolute paths and is not committed;
  on a clean tree that skip count is 0. The patterns are
  assembled from `os.sep` so that the check does not contain the string it
  looks for, and the script's own output file is excluded because it is
  rewritten at the end of the run.

## 9. Working directories

`probe_working_directories.py`. Every validation script and every example run
from the filesystem root, the system temporary directory, the repository root,
`validation/` — which is what the release gate uses — and `src/`.

**95 runs, 0 non-zero exit statuses.** Three products in one batch of this
mission passed every check in their own build and failed the gate because the
build agent, the agent's self-report and the coordinator all ran the scripts
from the product root. The probe is committed so that the assumption is not
shared.

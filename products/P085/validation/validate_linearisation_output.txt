The bound is equation (15) of pointmodel.geometry: the operator-norm Taylor
remainder of a product of matrix exponentials. Nothing in
pointmodel.terms uses it. It has no fitted constant and no slack term, and
its tolerance follows the conditioning of the problem through s^2.

==============================================================================
1. equation (16) over five decades of misalignment amplitude
==============================================================================
For each amplitude, 400 random directions over azimuth and 3 to 88 degrees
elevation, with all nine coefficients drawn uniformly on [-A, A]. The error
is the Euclidean distance between the exact beam vector of equation (12) and
the first-order reconstruction from the design matrix; the bound is B(s) of
equation (15) with s the sum of the five small rotation magnitudes.

==============================================================================
3. negative controls: every column corrupted in turn
==============================================================================
Each control is anchored on the exact rotation model -- the TRUTH -- and not
on any output of the fit. A corruption that did not push the reconstruction
outside the bound is reported as having failed to fire.

The bound has a RESOLUTION, and it is stated rather than discovered by a
reader. B(s) admits an error of B(s) * 206265 arcsec, so a corruption
smaller than that is inside the bound and cannot be detected by it. With all
nine coefficients at 100 arcsec the total small angle s of equation (14) is
about 800 arcsec and the bound admits roughly 3 arcsec of sky error; with
all nine at 10 arcsec it admits roughly 0.03 arcsec. So the scale-error
controls are run at 10 arcsec, where the bound is tight, and a deliberately
undetectable corruption is run at 100 arcsec as a must-NOT-fire control.

bound at 100 arcsec coefficients : 9.488707e-05 = 19.571864 arcsec of sky error
bound at 10 arcsec coefficients  : 9.392269e-07 = 0.193729 arcsec of sky error

The third block is the honest statement of what this bound cannot do. A one
part per million column error at 100 arcsec coefficients produces 1e-4
arcsec of sky error, which is thirty thousand times inside the bound, and no
bound of this form can see it. Detecting an error that small needs the
term-recovery check of validate_term_recovery.py, which is a different
instrument and is why both exist.

==============================================================================
4. the sky-plane error grows toward the zenith; the bound does not
==============================================================================
The vector-space comparison of equation (16) is deliberate. In sky-plane
angles the cross-elevation error of a rank-deficient direction carries a
factor of sec(E), so a comparison in angles would have to carry an
elevation-dependent tolerance and a reader could not tell an amplification
from a model error. In vector space there is no such factor and the bound is
a single expression.

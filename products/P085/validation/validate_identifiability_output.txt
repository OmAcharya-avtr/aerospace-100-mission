3. the identifiable combinations carry the bias the terms do not
==============================================================================
The same Monte Carlo, scored in two parameterisations. What separates them
is the BIAS, not the total error: the total error of any quantity here also
contains the noise scatter, which is the same size in both
parameterisations and has nothing to do with the geometry. The bias is the
systematic part, it is the part an error bar does not show, and it is what a
degeneracy produces.

Each combination is divided by the norm of its own weight vector so that
both columns are in arcseconds.

4. does the rank analysis catch the terms that are actually wrong

coefficient is BADLY DETERMINED when its mean least-squares estimate over
the Monte Carlo sits more than k declared sigmas from the truth. The rank
analysis never sees the truth. Two values of k are scored and both are
reported, so that the scoring cannot be read as having been tuned.

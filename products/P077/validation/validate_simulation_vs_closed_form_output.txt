==============================================================================
immediate-NAK against its analytic lower bound
==============================================================================
The closed form gives no delivery-time expression for immediate-NAK, only
the busy-time lower bound 8 * W / ((1 - p) * R_fwd) + L. That is a bound on
the EXPECTATION, because the busy time it contains is itself an expectation.
The first run of this check compared it against the minimum over runs and
reported seven of eleven cells as failures; a lucky run has fewer
transmissions than the expectation, so a realisation legitimately falls
below a bound built from the mean. Two separate statements are checked here
instead.

  (a) the simulated MEAN delivery time is at least the bound;
  (b) on EVERY run, the idle time is at least one one-way light time,
      which is the per-realisation statement and holds because the
      completing unit still has to propagate.

==============================================================================
immediate-NAK is not always faster than deferred-NAK
==============================================================================
Pipelining the retransmission requests shortens the transfer when the
forward stream is long relative to the round trip, and lengthens it when it
is not: a gap revealed late costs its own two light times, and with many
gaps the sender ends up idling once per gap instead of once per round.
The ratio below is the median immediate-NAK delivery time over the median
deferred-NAK delivery time on the same scenario and the same seeds; above
1.0 means immediate-NAK is slower.

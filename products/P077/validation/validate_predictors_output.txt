inside the predicted interval. An interval that is not calibrated is
reported as not calibrated. Width is the median of (upper - lower) divided

interval from zero to infinity covers everything.

A crossing is an adjacent pair of levels on one row where the higher-quantile
model predicted a lower value than the lower-quantile model. Seven levels
give six adjacent pairs per row. Independent per-level fits do not guarantee
monotonicity, so the predictions are sorted across levels and the number
sorted is counted rather than hidden. A crossing rate of this size says the
per-level fits disagree about the shape of the conditional distribution in
part of the feature space, which is consistent with the interval
under-covering: it is not a cosmetic detail.

==============================================================================
sanity checks on every prediction
==============================================================================
Measured, not assumed. A predictor that emitted a negative or non-finite
delivery time, or an interval that did not contain its own point
prediction, would score perfectly well on a median relative error and be
nonsense.

The assertion covers the three predictors whose point prediction is the
0.50 quantile of the same expression that produces their interval, so
containment is a property of the construction. analytic_mean is excluded
and reported separately: its point prediction is the EXPECTATION and its
interval ends are quantiles, and those are two different summaries of the
same distribution with no containment relation between them. The first run
of this check asserted containment for all four and found analytic_mean
violating it. That is a finding, not a defect to clamp away, and clamping
the expectation into its own interval would have hidden it.

The closed-form expectation can exceed the closed-form 0.95 quantile
because the delivery-time distribution over an intermittent link has a tail
heavy enough to pull the mean past the ninety-fifth percentile of the
semi-analytic approximation. It is the same phenomenon as the mean/median
ratio reported in validate_distributions.py, seen from the predictor side.

==============================================================================
how far the closed-form overhead is from the measured overhead with outages
==============================================================================
Under M3 the sender keeps transmitting during an outage, so the obvious
guess is that the transmission count inflates by one over the outage duty
cycle. Measured below against the closed-form expectation, as a median and
as a mean, with the naive factor beside them. The fraction of runs that saw
no outage at all is the column that explains the answer.

               link    duty     exact    median      mean  med/exact  mean/exact   1/duty  no outage
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  bursty continuous  0.7500    126.32    130.00    150.12     1.0292      1.1885   1.3333     0.3550
    windowed bursty  0.7143    126.32    129.00    149.77     1.0212      1.1857   1.4000     0.5350
        growing NAK  1.0000    141.18    141.00    141.29     0.9987      1.0008   1.0000     1.0000

The naive one-over-duty-cycle factor over-predicts on every row here, and
the reason is in the last column: the clean-pass time of this file on this
link is about half a second while the mean outage-free interval is one to
two seconds, so a large fraction of runs never meets an outage at all and
the ones that do pay far more than their share. An inflation factor derived
from a duty cycle assumes the outage process is fine-grained relative to the
transfer; when it is not, the duty cycle describes the long run and the
transfer does not live in the long run.

  bursty continuous   measured 1.1885, naive 1.3333, naive over-predicts by 12.2 per cent
  windowed bursty     measured 1.1857, naive 1.4000, naive over-predicts by 18.1 per cent

This is the same failure the analytic delivery-time extension has, measured
on the transmission count instead of on the time, and it is the single
clearest reason the learned predictor earns anything at all in the
correlated-outage regime.

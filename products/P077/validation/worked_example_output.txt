step 2: the analytic extension, past the closed form's own assumptions
==============================================================================
Two steps: divide by the outage duty cycle to get the contact time the
transfer needs, then lay that contact time over the declared windows in
order to get the wall-clock time at which it runs out.

outage duty cycle                         : 0.800000
contact duty cycle                        : 0.193548
closed-form E[T] with no outages          : 25.4821 s
contact time needed: E[T] / outage duty   : 31.8527 s
declared window duration                  : 30.0000 s
extension, laid over the contact plan     : 181.8527 s

The extension is not a theorem. It ignores that an outage destroys units
already in flight, that a round boundary straddling an outage pays its
turnaround twice, and that a unit cannot straddle a contact boundary.

An earlier version of this function divided by the product of the two duty
cycles instead. On this scenario with 300-second contacts that gave 164.57 s
against a measured median of 26.80 s -- a factor of 6.14 -- because the
transfer fitted inside the first contact and the contact duty cycle never
bit at all. The learned model is benchmarked against this extension, and a
baseline that wrong would have made the comparison meaningless.

step 4: what the extension got wrong
==============================================================================
analytic extension                        : 181.8527 s
measured median                           : 26.7995 s
measured q0.95                            : 205.1822 s
extension / measured median               : 6.7857
measured q0.95 / measured median          : 7.6562
measured maximum / measured median        : 20.8086

contact time the extension needs          : 31.8527 s
declared contact window                   : 30.0000 s
needed / window                           : 1.0618
runs that finished in the first contact   : 0.8250

The extension is a step function of the contact plan, and this scenario sits
just on the wrong side of a step. The contact time it needs exceeds the
declared window by six per cent, so it deposits the whole transfer into the
second contact and reports a figure one window period larger. The simulation
says 82.5 per cent of runs finish inside the first contact. The extension is
not wrong about the arithmetic; it is wrong because it has one number where
the answer is a distribution, and a distribution straddling a window edge is
exactly where that fails hardest.

The operational reading is the spread, not the centre. The median is 26.80 s
and the ninety-fifth percentile is 205.18 s, a factor of 7.66, because a run
that misses the first contact waits for the next one. A plan written against
any single number -- the median, the mean, or the closed-form extension --
cannot carry that, and the per-contact completion vector in step 3 is the
shape the answer actually has.

# Model Card — TrackForge reacquisition policy (`trackforge.reacq`)

**Model:** tabular Q-learning policy for post-loss-of-lock reacquisition
strategy selection
**Package:** `trackforge` 0.1.0 (product P002)
**Date:** 2026 · **Owner:** OPTIMA Organisation · **License:** AGPL-3.0-only

> **This model is not certified for operational flight use.**

---

## 1. Problem

After a pointing-acquisition-tracking (PAT) loop loses lock on an optical
link, the terminal must re-find the target. Each re-scan strategy costs a
different amount of time and has a different probability of covering the
(drifting, uncertain) target position. The model chooses, at each decision
point, which re-scan strategy to execute next, so as to minimise the expected
time to reacquire.

- **Decision variable:** one of three discrete actions.
- **Objective:** minimise expected time-to-reacquire (equivalently, maximise
  the sum of negative attempt durations).
- **Not** a perception, estimation or safety-critical control function: the
  model only sequences search strategies that are themselves deterministic.

## 2. Baseline (implemented and benchmarked first)

Two scripted policies, both fully deterministic:

| Baseline | Behaviour | Mean time-to-reacquire (2 000 episodes) |
|----------|-----------|------------------------------------------|
| `AlwaysFullPolicy` | Always restart the spiral over the full a-priori uncertainty cone | 8.6485 s, 95 % CI [8.2926, 9.0044], success 0.877 |
| `AlwaysLocalPolicy` | Always restart a local spiral at the last-known position | 6.4395 s, 95 % CI [5.9612, 6.9179], success 0.838 |

Both are evaluated **before** the learned model and on **identical episodes**
(common random numbers), so any difference is attributable to the policy, not
to the scenario draw.

## 3. Architecture

Tabular Q-learning (Watkins & Dayan 1992, *Machine Learning* 8(3–4);
Sutton & Barto 2018, *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction*, 2nd ed.,
sec. 6.5), implemented in numpy:

```
Q(s,a) <- Q(s,a) + alpha [ r + gamma * max_a' Q(s',a') - Q(s,a) ]
```

- **State (4 tile-coded features → 5 × 4 × 4 × 4 = 320 discrete states):**
  time since loss [s]; last-known offset magnitude / σ_lk; uncertainty growth
  σ(t)/σ₀; radius already searched / σ(t). The first three are the state
  named in the product specification; the fourth is required to keep the
  "expanding ring" action Markov and is documented as such.
- **Actions (3):** `LOCAL` (disc of radius 3σ(t) at the last-known position),
  `FULL` (full cone, centred on boresight), `RING` (annulus from the
  already-searched radius outward by 2σ(t)).
- **Model size:** 320 × 3 = 960 float64 Q-values + 960 int64 visit counts
  (≈15 kB). No neural network.
- **Deviation from a deep-RL assumption:** PyTorch is **not available** in the
  build environment, so no DQN/actor-critic variant is provided. The state
  space was designed to be small enough for an exact tabular method, which is
  also what makes bitwise reproducibility achievable. This deviation is
  recorded in the README Limitations section.

## 4. Dataset / environment

There is no collected dataset. The training and evaluation data are episodes
generated by the simulator itself — see `DATASET_CARD.md` for the generating
distributions, their justification, and their limitations. In short: a
loss-of-lock severity `u ~ U[0,1]` sets both the observable last-known offset
and the unobservable true displacement scale; the target then drifts linearly
with a Gaussian random velocity. Those relations are a **modelling choice of
this simulator, not a published or measured model**.

## 5. Training procedure

| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Episodes | 20 000 |
| Discount γ | 0.99 |
| Learning rate α | 0.30 → 0.02, geometric decay over the run |
| Exploration ε | 1.00 → 0.05, geometric decay over the run |
| Reward | −(attempt duration in seconds); additional −`max_time` on timeout |
| Random source | a single `numpy.random.default_rng(seed)` driving both the environment and exploration |
| Default seed | 12345 |
| Wall-clock training time | **2.83–2.97 s** on 2 CPU cores (1.6 % of the 180 s mission compute budget) |

Exact command:

```bash
python -m trackforge reacq --episodes 20000 --seed 12345 \
                           --eval-episodes 2000 --eval-seed 999
```

## 6. Test split strategy

There is no fixed train/test file split; the environment is a generator, so
the split is over **random seeds**:

- **Training seeds:** the training run consumes its own stream seeded by
  `--seed` (default 12345). Evaluation episodes are *not* drawn from that
  stream.
- **Evaluation episodes:** episode *i* is generated with the environment
  re-seeded to `eval_seed + i` (default 999…2998). These seeds are disjoint
  from the training stream, and are identical for every policy compared
  (common random numbers).
- **Generalisation check:** three independent training seeds (12345, 20260,
  777) are evaluated on the *same* 2 000 held-out episodes; the spread of the
  result is reported, not just the best.

## 7. Metrics (from actual runs, 2 000 held-out episodes)

| Policy | Mean [s] | 95 % CI [s] | Median [s] | p90 [s] | Success rate | Mean attempts |
|--------|----------|-------------|------------|---------|--------------|---------------|
| baseline always-full | 8.6485 | [8.2926, 9.0044] | 5.2360 | 30.000 | 0.877 | 1.685 |
| baseline always-local | 6.4395 | [5.9612, 6.9179] | 0.5762 | 30.000 | 0.838 | 4.716 |
| **learned, seed 12345** | **4.1480** | **[3.7752, 4.5209]** | 0.5001 | 8.814 | **0.909** | 3.427 |
| learned, seed 20260 | 4.2095 | [3.8162, 4.6028] | 0.5001 | 30.000 | 0.898 | 3.865 |
| learned, seed 777 | 4.4380 | [4.0432, 4.8328] | 0.3969 | 30.000 | 0.896 | 3.847 |

- **33.8 % reduction** in mean time-to-reacquire vs always-local;
  **50.7 % reduction** vs always-full (mean over the three training seeds).
- 95 % confidence intervals of the learned policy are **disjoint** from both
  baselines' intervals.
- Times are **censored** at the 30 s episode timeout; the mean must be read
  together with the success rate.

## 8. Uncertainty / confidence output

`QLearningPolicy.act_with_confidence(state) -> (action, confidence)` returns a
confidence in [0, 1], the product of

- **margin**: `(Q_best − Q_second) / (|Q_best| + ε)`, clipped to [0, 1] — how
  much better the chosen action is than the runner-up;
- **support**: `min(1, visits(best) / min_visits)` with `min_visits = 30` —
  how much evidence the table has for that entry.

On states never visited during training the policy **defers to the `FULL`
baseline action** rather than acting on an all-zero row.

Measured on the seed-12345 policy: 64 of 320 states visited; confidence on
visited states mean 0.204 (min 0.000, max 1.000); 256 unvisited states use
the fallback; 37 visited states carry confidence < 0.1.

**This confidence is a heuristic score, not a calibrated probability.** It has
not been checked against realised outcome frequencies, and must not be used as
a probability of successful reacquisition.

## 9. Failure cases

1. **Distribution shift.** The policy is trained for one `ReacqConfig`. Change
   the drift rate, coverage rate or severity coupling κ and the learned
   bin thresholds no longer correspond to the same physical regimes; the
   policy can then be worse than `always-full`. Retrain per configuration.
2. **State aliasing.** With 320 bins, distinct physical situations share a
   state. Near bin boundaries the policy switches abruptly; behaviour there is
   not smooth and not guaranteed monotone in the underlying continuous state.
3. **Sparse coverage.** 80 % of the state space is never visited during a
   20 000-episode run (the reachable set is much smaller than the tabulated
   set). Those states silently fall back to the baseline action — safe, but
   the model provides no value there.
4. **Censored objective.** Because the timeout truncates episodes, the model
   optimises a censored objective. It has a mild incentive to prefer actions
   that finish before the cap rather than actions with the lowest true
   expected time in the heavy tail.
5. **Training-seed variance.** Across three seeds the mean varies by ±3.4 %.
   The worst seed still beats both baselines, but a single training run
   should not be quoted without its seed.
6. **No safety envelope.** The policy has no notion of resource limits,
   thermal constraints, keep-out zones or link-budget windows. It optimises
   time only.

## 10. Reproducibility

```bash
cd products/P002
python -m pytest tests/ -q                       # 295 tests
python validation/v5_reacq_benchmark.py          # full benchmark, ~25 s
python -m trackforge reacq --episodes 20000 --seed 12345
```

Seeds: training 12345 (also 20260, 777 for the seed sweep); evaluation base
seed 999. `train_q_learning` with the same arguments returns bitwise identical
`q` and `visits` arrays; `evaluate_policy` returns an identical result
dictionary. Verified in `tests/test_reproducibility.py` and in
`validation/v5_reacq_benchmark.txt`.

Environment: Python 3.11.15, numpy 2.4.4, scipy 1.17.1, Linux x86_64,
2 CPU cores. No GPU. No network access required.

## 12. Ethical and safety limits

- The model is a **simulation research artefact**. It is not flight-qualified,
  not certified, and not approved for operational aerospace use.
- It has been trained and evaluated **only inside its own simulator**. There
  is no evidence about its behaviour on real terminals, and the simulator's
  loss-of-lock statistics are an assumed model, not measured data.
- It must not be placed in any control path where a wrong or slow
  reacquisition decision has safety, availability or regulatory consequences
  without independent verification against hardware.
- Its confidence output must not be interpreted as a probability of success.
- No personal data is involved; the model has no human-subject or
  fairness dimension.

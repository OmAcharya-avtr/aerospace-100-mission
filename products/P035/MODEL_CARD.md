# Model card — telemetryool multivariate novelty detectors, v0.1.0

**This model is not certified for operational flight use.** Research grade,
validation level 2. Not flight-qualified, not certified, not approved for
operational aerospace use.

Every number in this card comes from `validation/validate_matched_far.py`, run
in the session that produced this repository; the raw stdout is committed as
`validation/validate_matched_far_output.txt`.

## 1. Problem

Decide, from a frame of housekeeping telemetry channels, whether the spacecraft
is behaving as it did during a nominal reference period — and do it at a stated
false-alarm rate, so the result can be compared with the limit table it is meant
to supplement.

The output is a scalar novelty score per sample plus a calibrated tail p-value
with its finite-sample interval; a binary alarm is produced only after a
threshold has been calibrated against nominal data and a debounce count applied.

## 2. Baselines, implemented first

The classical baselines were built, designed and measured before any learned
model existed, and the detector list returned by `build_detector_suite` is
ordered baselines-first with a test (`tests/test_detectors.py`) pinning that
order.

| Baseline | What it is | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Limit check with persistence | Two-sided limit at `±L sigma`, debounce count | Operational practice; exact ARL from a persistence-counter Markov chain |
| EWMA chart | `z_t = lam u_t + (1−lam) z_{t−1}`, limits `±L sigma_z` | Roberts (1959); Lucas & Saccucci (1990) |
| CUSUM chart | Tabular two-sided CUSUM with reference value `k`, decision interval `h` | Page (1954); Brook & Evans (1972); Siegmund (1985) |
| Hotelling `T²` / `Q` | PCA on the nominal correlation matrix, `T²` on the retained subspace and `Q` (SPE) on the residual | Hotelling (1947); Jackson & Mudholkar (1979) |

The first three are univariate per channel, combined through one alarm bus
(maximum over channels). `T²`/`Q` is the classical *multivariate* baseline and
is what the learned models have to beat — not the univariate charts.

## 3. Models

Both learned models are one-class: they see only nominal data and no labelled
anomalies, because an operational monitor has abundant nominal telemetry and no
catalogue of the faults it has not yet had.

| Model | Architecture | Hyperparameters | Why these |
|---|---|---|---|
| `GmmNovelty` | Gaussian mixture density; score is `−log p(x)` in nats | 4 full-covariance components, `max_iter = 100`, `random_state = 0` | Scoring is a handful of matrix operations, so 3.35 million samples cost about 2 s on one core; four components is enough to shape a mildly non-Gaussian nominal cloud without overfitting 150 000 training samples |
| `IsolationForestNovelty` | Isolation Forest; score is `−score_samples` | 60 trees, `max_samples = 256`, `random_state = 0` | The standard tree-based one-class detector, included so the comparison contains a method with a genuinely different inductive bias |

PyTorch is not available in the build environment, so there is no autoencoder,
no LSTM and no deep model of any kind. That is an environment constraint, stated
rather than hidden: a reconstruction autoencoder is the obvious next model to
try and this package does not contain one.

### Features

Both models are fitted on a per-sample feature vector built by
`telemetryool.detectors.smoothed_features`: the four raw standardised channel
values concatenated with their EWMA at `lam = 0.2`, divided by `sigma_z`, for
**8 features**. The EWMA half gives the per-sample models the temporal
integration the charts get for free; without it a per-sample multivariate model
cannot compete with CUSUM on a small sustained shift. This is the choice that
gives the learned models their best honest chance and it is stated here because
it materially changes the comparison.

The EWMA state is reset at the start of each monitoring window, so the first few
samples of every window are in the start-up transient and their smoothed
features are systematically small. The window-level calibration absorbs this
because it is performed on windows with the same structure; the consequence is
that the score is not stationary within a window and the detection delay depends
on the onset index, which is why every delay table states it.

Features are standardised with the mean and population standard deviation of the
training block. A feature with zero variance raises `ValueError` rather than
being divided by a floor.

## 4. Dataset

See `DATASET_CARD.md`. In brief: synthetic, generated by committed seeded
scripts, four channels, equicorrelation 0.6, no serial correlation, marginally
standard normal. **No real spacecraft telemetry was used**, and every
performance number in this card is a statement about this generator, not about
any real vehicle.

## 5. Training procedure and splits

Four disjoint blocks, each from its own derived seed off a base seed of
20261005, so no sample is reused across roles:

| Block | Seed | Size | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Train | base + 1 | 1 500 windows × 100 samples = 150 000 | Fit the GMM, the forest and the PCA; compute the feature standardisation and the calibration score ECDF |
| Calibrate | base + 2 | 10 000 windows = 1 000 000 | Set each detector's scalar threshold so exactly 500 windows alarm |
| Measure | base + 3 | 20 000 windows = 2 000 000 | Measure the delivered false-alarm rate and form the negatives of every confusion matrix |
| Anomalous | base + 10 + i | 2 000 windows per scenario × 5 | Detection probability, detection delay, ROC |

Total scored samples per detector: 3 350 000.

There is **no hyperparameter search**. The component count, tree count and
smoothing weight were fixed from the compute budget before any result was read,
and no value was changed after seeing a comparison table. That means the models
are almost certainly not at their best achievable settings; it also means
nothing in the table is a product of tuning against the evaluation data.

## 7. Uncertainty and confidence output

`NoveltyModel.novelty_pvalue` returns, per sample, a `PValue` carrying:

- `pvalue` — `(1 + #{nominal calibration scores ≥ score}) / (1 + n_calibration)`,
  the conservative plug-in estimate of the tail probability under the nominal
  model;
- `low`, `high` — a Clopper-Pearson interval on the underlying exceedance
  probability at a chosen confidence, reflecting the finite size of the
  calibration sample;
- `resolution_floor` — `1 / (1 + n_calibration)`. A reported `pvalue` equal to
  this floor means "no calibration sample scored this high", **not** that the
  probability is that small. With the 150 000-sample training block used here
  the floor is `6.6666e-06`, and nothing below it is resolvable.

Calibration of the p-value was checked on held-out nominal data in
`tests/test_novelty.py::test_pvalues_are_approximately_uniform_on_nominal_data`:
the fraction of held-out nominal samples with `p < 0.05` must lie in
[0.033, 0.067] (a 5-sigma band at 4 000 samples). The check passes.

The p-value is a per-*sample* confidence statement under the nominal model. It
is not a probability that an anomaly is present, and it is not calibrated under
serial correlation — see the limitations.

## 8. Failure cases, measured

1. **Isolation Forest fails on every scenario here.** Pd between 0.0000 and
   0.0430 at the matched operating point, AUC between 0.4253 and 0.6716, and
   **below chance** (0.4253) on the decorrelation scenario. Its axis-aligned
   splits are fitted to the marginal distributions, which a decorrelation leaves
   untouched, and a 1-sigma shift stays well inside the nominal support where
   the forest's path lengths barely change. It is kept in the comparison because
   deleting a method that lost would make the table dishonest.
2. **Nothing detects a 0.5 sigma step reliably.** The best Pd is 0.5510 (CUSUM).
   That is a property of the data, not of the detectors: at `alpha_W = 0.05` and
   `W = 100` the available evidence is not enough.
3. **The limit check is below chance on a stuck channel** (AUC 0.4677). A frozen
   value is one draw held constant, which is on average closer to the mean than
   a fresh draw, so a limit check sees *less* excursion than nominal.
4. **Serial correlation destroys the designed false-alarm rate.** At an AR(1)
   coefficient of 0.3 the charts deliver 6.4× to 7.3× their designed rate
   (`validation/validate_far_design.py`). The learned models' thresholds are
   calibrated empirically, so they are correct for whatever nominal data they
   were calibrated on — but the training and calibration data here are white, so
   a model fitted here and deployed on correlated telemetry would have the same
   problem.
5. **A novel anomaly that looks like nominal data in every monitored direction
   is undetectable by construction.** Nothing in this package changes that.

## 9. Reproducibility

```bash
cd validation
MPLBACKEND=Agg PYTHONPATH=../src python3 validate_matched_far.py
```

Base seed 20261005, derived as described in section 5. `GaussianMixture` and
`IsolationForest` both take `random_state = 0`. A regression test
(`tests/test_harness.py::test_reproducibility_for_a_fixed_seed`) asserts that
two runs with the same seed produce identical calibrated thresholds and
identical false-alarm counts.

## 10. Compute used

Measured on the single CPU core that built this repository, shared with four
other build agents (`nproc = 1`). Contention varied substantially between runs,
so two measurements of the same stage are given where they differ; the spread is
the contention, not the code.

| Stage | Wall time |
|---|---|
| Fit `T²`/`Q` PCA (150 000 × 8) | 0.16 s, 0.17 s |
| Fit Isolation Forest (60 trees, 150 000 × 8) | 1.25 s, 1.29 s |
| Fit GMM (4 full-covariance components, 150 000 × 8) | 3.35 s, 3.33 s |
| Score 2 000 000 samples, `T²`/`Q` | 1.19 s |
| Score 2 000 000 samples, GMM | 1.62 s, 3.84 s |
| Score 2 000 000 samples, Isolation Forest | 6.62 s, 13.05 s |
| Whole `validate_matched_far.py` run (3.35 M samples × 6 detectors) | 24.8 s to 47.7 s |

No GPU. Total training compute for both learned models is a few CPU-seconds;
this is a small-model comparison harness, not a training pipeline. The Isolation
Forest costs roughly four times as much to score as the Gaussian mixture and
loses on every scenario, which is the compute argument against it as well as the
accuracy one.

## 11. Ethical and safety limits

- The models are fitted to synthetic data from a generator in this repository.
  Any performance number here is a statement about that generator.
- An alarm from this package is a statistical statement about a nominal
  reference distribution. It is not a diagnosis, not a fault isolation, and
  carries no information about cause.
- No autonomous action should be taken on the output of these models. This
  software is research grade, not flight-qualified, not certified, and not
  approved for operational aerospace use.
- Calibrating a threshold on a nominal history that already contains an
  undetected fault will treat that fault as normal. The package cannot detect
  that condition and does not claim to.

## 12. Honest verdict

On these scenarios, at a matched false-alarm rate:

- The **classical** methods win or tie wherever the anomaly is univariate. CUSUM
  has the best AUC on the 1.0 sigma step (1.0000) and the best detection
  probability on the 0.5 sigma step (0.5510). EWMA ties for the best AUC on the
  drift.
- The **Gaussian mixture** has the lowest mean detection delay on three of five
  scenarios (11.90 vs CUSUM's 13.63 on the 1.0 sigma step; 22.89 vs 24.43 on the
  drift; 17.46 on the decorrelation) and the best AUC on three of five. The
  margins over `T²`/`Q` are small — 0.9893 vs 0.9888 AUC on the decorrelation,
  0.6600 vs 0.6585 Pd on the stuck channel — and within or near the Monte-Carlo
  error of 0.0106 on Pd.
- The **Isolation Forest** loses everywhere and is below chance on one scenario.
- The thing that actually matters is **multivariate versus univariate**, not
  learned versus classical: on the decorrelation scenario the two multivariate
  methods reach Pd ≈ 0.93 and the three univariate ones sit at the false-alarm
  rate. The classical `T²`/`Q` captures nearly all of that benefit.

A reader who wants a better detector should use `pyod`, which ships dozens of
one-class models with a consistent interface and far more attention paid to each
of them than this package could give. What this package offers is not the
detector; it is the harness that made the sentence above checkable.

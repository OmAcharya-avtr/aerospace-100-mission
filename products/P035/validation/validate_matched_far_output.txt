A method that fails here is not comparable with the others in the tables
below, and the reason has to be named rather than averaged away.  One
mechanism that would cause it is a score with heavy ties: a threshold has
to land between two distinct score values, so a coarse score cannot be
calibrated to an arbitrary rate.  The next section measures that directly,
so the explanation is checked rather than assumed.

distinct values cannot hit an arbitrary alpha_W.

Measured outcome: the scores are almost entirely distinct for every
detector, so calibration granularity is NOT the limiting factor at this
sample size, and any residual discrepancy in the table above is ordinary
sampling error of the threshold and the measurement.  The diagnostic is kept
because it would be the first thing to look at if a detector with a coarser
score -- a rank statistic, a quantised telemetry word -- were added.

An AUC below 0.5 means the score is anti-correlated with the anomaly on
that scenario: the detector is systematically more comfortable with the
anomalous data than with nominal data.  That is a real failure mode of a
one-class model whose notion of 'normal' does not include the relevant
direction, and it is left in the table.

# Validation evidence — twincalib 0.1.0

Validation level 3, research grade. **Not flight-qualified, not certified, not
approved for operational aerospace use.** Nothing in this repository calibrates
a real spacecraft. All data is synthetic, generated by committed scripts from
fixed seeds, and the two-node thermal model is a declared lumped network whose
parameter values are chosen to put the shipped designs in a plausible
temperature band; **no parameter value here is a measurement of flight
hardware.**

Every number below was produced by a script in this directory, executed in this
container on 2026-10-15, with its raw stdout committed beside it as
`<script>_output.txt`. Nothing was copied from a paper, estimated or rounded by
hand. The checks that undercut this package are in the tables on purpose and are
marked.

**Environment.** Python 3.13.16, NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1, scikit-learn 1.9.1,
Matplotlib 3.11.2, pytest 9.1.1, Hypothesis 6.169.3. Two shared CPU cores
(`os.sched_getaffinity` reports 2) with about 7.8 GiB of RAM, contended with
sibling build agents. Every wall clock below is measured under that contention
and moves by 10-30 per cent between runs; the accuracy figures are seeded and do
not move.

**Test suite.** `python -m pytest tests/ -q --junitxml=junit.xml` from the
repository root. Counts are parsed from the junit XML, not from pytest stdout,
and cross-checked against a count of `testcase` elements: see "Test suite" at
the end of this file.

## What is being validated, and what it is a model of

The calibration model is a two-node lumped thermal network: an avionics unit
conductively coupled to a radiator panel that radiates to deep space.

```
C1 dT1/dt = P_dis(t) - k12 (T1 - T2)
C2 dT2/dt = k12 (T1 - T2) + q_sun s(t) + q_par - h_rad (T2**4 - T_space**4)
```

Six parameters, `(C1, C2, k12, h_rad, q_sun, q_par)` in
`(J/K, J/K, W/K, W/K^4, W, W)`. Sources: the lumped-network formulation and the
grey-body radiation term from Gilmore (ed.), *Spacecraft Thermal Control
Handbook, Volume I*, 2nd ed., AIAA, 2002, Chapter 1; the lumped-capacitance
method and its Biot-number validity criterion from Incropera and DeWitt,
*Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer*, Chapter 5; the Stefan-Boltzmann
constant `5.670374419e-8 W/m^2/K^4` from the SI 2019 revision; the radiative
sink temperature `2.72548 K` from Fixsen (2009), *Astrophysical Journal* 707,
916.

**Declared true parameters** (`twincalib.scenarios.THETA_TRUE`):
`C1 = 800 J/K`, `C2 = 4000 J/K`, `k12 = 2.5 W/K`,
`h_rad = 1.205e-8 W/K^4`, `q_sun = 55 W`, `q_par = 18 W`. Each magnitude is
justified in that module's docstring against a cited material property or solar
constant, and **none of them is a measurement.**

**Declared model-form discrepancy.** The data generator uses a
temperature-dependent interface conductance,
`k12_eff = k12 (1 + 1.5 (T1 - 290 K)/100 K)`, which the calibration model
cannot reproduce at any parameter value. Interface conductance really does vary
with temperature (Madhusudana, *Thermal Contact Conductance*, 2nd ed., Springer,
2014, Chapter 4); the linear form and the 15 per cent per 10 K slope are
declared for the experiment, not measured, and the slope is at the large end of
what is plausible so that the discrepancy sits clearly above the thermometer
noise.

**Five experiment designs.** Three are deliberately defective, and the designs
are the subject of the analysis as much as the model is, because identifiability
is a property of the design and the parameters together.

| design | measured | observations | what is wrong with it |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `baseline-eclipse` | T1, T2 | 802 | nothing |
| `full-sun` | T1, T2 | 802 | constant illumination, so only `q_sun + q_par` is identifiable |
| `lumped-nodes` | T1 | 401 | T1 only, `k12 = 40 W/K`, so the fast mode is unresolved |
| `coarse-sampled` | T1, T2 | 402 | nothing; 60 s sampling instead of 30 s |
| `weak-excitation` | T1 | 401 | T1 only at constant power; full rank and worthless |

### The input-grid finding, because it failed first

Check 5 was originally one check and it **FAILED**: refining the integration step
from 30 s to 3.75 s moved the trajectory by 0.63 K, far more than a
fourth-order scheme should. The cause is not the integrator. The eclipse edge
sits at `0.65 * 5700 s = 3705 s`, and `3705 / 30 = 123.5` is not an integer, so a
30 s grid places the illumination transition up to 15 s away from where a
3.75 s grid places it. Splitting the check into 5a (same inputs, integrator
only: **4.76e-06 K**) and 5b (refined inputs: **0.626 K**) separates the two, and
the second is reported rather than bounded.

**Consequence, repeated in the README Limitations.** The zero-order-hold input
grid is part of this model, not an implementation detail. Every number in this
repository is internally consistent because the data generator and the fitter
use the same grid, so the quantisation cancels exactly. A calibration against
real telemetry would not have that cancellation and would carry this as a model
error of order 0.6 K on this design.

constructed orthogonal to the column space has orthogonal fraction
**1.000000000**.

### Absorption, measurement 2: parameter drift

Refit the physics with the learned discrepancy subtracted, and ask whether the
physical parameters moved towards the truth or away from it
(Brynjarsdottir and O'Hagan 2014).

| design | RMS log error before | after | change | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `baseline-eclipse` | 0.132553 | 0.178772 | **+0.046219** | **WORSE** |
| `full-sun` | 0.076650 | 0.091097 | +0.014447 | **WORSE** |
| `lumped-nodes` | 0.040403 | 0.034044 | -0.006359 | better |
| `weak-excitation` | 4.684348 | 4.863888 | +0.179540 | **WORSE** |

The physical parameters got worse on **3 of 4** designs. On
`baseline-eclipse` the per-parameter drift is `q_par` **+0.10638** and `h_rad`
**+0.04073** in natural log units, while the training chi-squared falls from
1628.25 to 864.00.

**This is the finding that must not be softened.** On `baseline-eclipse` the
residual model reduces the held-out error by 39 per cent and makes the physical
parameters worse by 35 per cent in root-mean-square log error. If the
deliverable is a prediction, use it. If the deliverable is a physical parameter
— a heat capacity to put in a thermal report — the residual model is actively
harmful and the physics-only fit with its profile intervals is the thing to
report.

### Why the ranges are one minor version wide

A wider range was measured first and **rejected on evidence**. At
**numpy 2.3.5 / scipy 1.16.0 / scikit-learn 1.8.0**:

- the whole suite still passes: **205 passed, 0 failed**;
- `validate_identifiability`, `validate_sensitivity` and `validate_model` are
  byte-identical except the version line and one 1e-12-level digit;
- and the number this product's central claim rests on **moves**:

| quantity | at the measured versions | at numpy 2.3.5 / scipy 1.16.0 / sklearn 1.8.0 |
|---|---:|---:|
| `full-sun` absorption fraction, cut 1e-2 | **0.12715** | **0.04621** |
| `full-sun` absorption range over 6 configurations | [0.08144, 0.15605] | [0.04467, 0.06909] |
| worst-case separation factor | **3.34** | **1.83** |
| `weak-excitation` held-out improvement | +0.0485 | +0.0378 |

The cause is scikit-learn's Gaussian-process implementation and SciPy's
optimiser fitting a different kernel, which changes the learned field and hence
its projection. **Every verdict survives — the ranges still do not overlap and
the separation is still real — and the published numbers do not.** That is
exactly the silent-drift defect class the build guide records as P025, and the
only honest response is to bound the range to what was measured.

**The cost of that pin is real and is recorded in the README Limitations:** a
security release inside NumPy, SciPy or scikit-learn outside these ranges cannot
be taken without re-running this whole directory and republishing the numbers
that moved. That cost is not resolved by removing the ceiling.

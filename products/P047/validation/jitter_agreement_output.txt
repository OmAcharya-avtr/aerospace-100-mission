3. where the routes diverge, and why
------------------------------------
   (a) white closed form vs white exact: the small-bandwidth approximation only.
       Under 0.2 % at B_n = 0.001, about 1 % at 0.01, 2 % at 0.02.
   (b) white vs coloured: the whiteness assumption. Detector self-noise is
       negatively correlated at lag one - the early-late gate measures
       R[1]/R[0] = -0.45 - so the loop filters it far better than white noise.
       The white closed form over-predicts by up to 15.34x.
   (c) coloured vs Monte Carlo: the remaining gap is the S-curve nonlinearity and
       the decision errors, both absent from every prediction. The deviation is
       systematic and grows with B_n - about 3 % at B_n = 0.001, 7 % at 0.005 and
       14 % at 0.02 - not random, so with a Monte Carlo standard error of 1 to 3 %
       it is many standard errors wide. It is a modelling limit, not noise.
       Worst relative deviation: 14.1 %.
       Worst |z| against the Monte Carlo standard error: 9.96.

4. the boundary of validity: pushing the loop out of the linear range
---------------------------------------------------------------------
   B_n = 0.05 with a falling SNR, Gardner's detector. The linear range of its
   S-curve on this pulse is 0.130 symbol; once the rms jitter approaches that
   the linearised prediction has no reason to hold, and it does not.
    snr_db     coloured  monte carlo    mc s.e.   mc/col  rms/linear   slips
      20.0  1.79381e-03  2.37226e-03   2.81e-05   1.3225      0.3747       0
      10.0  1.51323e-02  8.34245e-02   4.28e-04   5.5130      2.2218    1010
       4.0  6.96464e-02  8.35006e-02   1.21e-03   1.1989      2.2228    1102
       0.0  2.28770e-01  8.42226e-02   9.29e-04   0.3682      2.2324    2509
      -3.0  6.36620e-01  8.37999e-02   7.61e-04   0.1316      2.2268    5090
   the ratio departs from 1 as rms jitter grows towards the linear range, and once
   slips appear the stationary variance of a wrapped error is no longer the
   quantity the prediction describes at all. The measured variance saturates at
   about 0.0834 to 0.0842, which is the variance of a uniform distribution on a
   full symbol, 1/12 = 0.083333: the loop has lost timing entirely and the wrapped
   error is uniform. Below 10 dB the prediction and the measurement are describing
   different objects, and the prediction crossing the measurement near 4 dB is a
   coincidence of two unrelated curves, not agreement.

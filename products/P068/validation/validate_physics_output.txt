The interval width is of order tens of days while the bias from a
0.05 error in z is of order hundreds. A declared exponent that is
slightly wrong therefore produces a confident and wrong date, and the
reported interval gives no warning. This is the single most important
limitation of the physics-shaped baseline and it is why the shipped
benchmark measures the model on a population whose z varies.

3. profiling z over the grid

  z true  fixed bias d  profiled bias d  fixed |bias|  profiled |bias|  mode z
    0.45       -181.26            -4.51        181.26            28.26    0.45
    0.50        -95.96            -0.57         95.96            35.08    0.50
    0.55         -0.91            -2.29         16.10            37.48    0.55
    0.65        217.37             0.33        217.37            41.84    0.65

Profiling reduces the bias where z is badly wrong and costs accuracy
where it is right, because the selected exponent is itself noisy. The
interval returned after profiling is conditional on the selected z and
does not include the selection, so it is narrower than it should be.
The shipped default is the fixed, cited exponent.

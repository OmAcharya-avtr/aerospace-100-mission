4. WHAT THE RULES HOLD THAT A CLASSIFIER CANNOT: THE INTEGRITY VERDICT
==============================================================================
Integrity is a separate output from the consequence class. The checksum is arithmetic: there is no threshold, no training set and no error rate. Measured over the whole test split against the generator's ground truth.

Two corruption families of the corpus leave the checksum inconsistent -- checksum_digit, where the checksum column alone is altered, and data_digit, where a data digit is altered and the stated checksum is left as it was. Three others recompute the checksum after corrupting the line, which is what a ground system that formats and then checksums does, and those are invisible to the checksum screen BY CONSTRUCTION. Both groups are counted.

element sets whose checksum should be inconsistent : 57
checksum screen flagged                            : 57
true positives                                     : 57
false positives                                    : 0
false negatives                                    : 0
precision                                          : 1.0000
recall                                             : 1.0000

element sets corrupted and then re-checksummed      : 85
of which the checksum screen saw                    : 0
That second number is the determinability limit of equation (13) made concrete. The checksum cannot see a corruption whose checksum was recomputed, at any configuration, and the report says so before any screen runs.

element sets with a field this package could not read : 14
of which the injected corruption was a field shift    : 14
field-shifted element sets that stayed fully readable : 13 of 27

The learned model is not scored in this section because it is not asked this question: nothing in its output is an integrity verdict. A caller who wants to know whether an element set is what its source wrote gets an exact answer from the checksum and could not get one from a classifier.

7. CONFIDENT WHERE THE ANALYTIC SCREEN WITHHOLDS ITS TEST
==============================================================================
Equation (27) reports the continuity screen's resolution, and where that resolution exceeds the declared silent threshold the screen withholds its absolute test rather than returning a verdict that is noise. The learned model has no such mechanism. Its errors are counted separately on the two subsets, with its own decision margin.

subset                     n  errors  error rate  margin>0.5    frac  mean margin
resolves threshold       142      11      0.0775           0  0.0000       0.7291
does NOT resolve         278      32      0.1151           7  0.2188       0.6403

The learned model answers with a high decision margin on element sets whose orbit regime makes the analytic continuity test unresolvable, and 7 of its 32 errors on that subset carry a margin above 0.5. That is the failure mode this batch of the mission exists to look for: a confident answer about something the data does not support. The analytic screen's answer on the same records is that it cannot tell, which is less useful and more honest.
[PASS] the_learned_model_makes_confident_errors_where_the_screen_withholds -- 7 errors with a margin above 0.5 on the unresolvable subset

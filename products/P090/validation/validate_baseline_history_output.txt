1. THE ROBUST SCALE WITHOUT ITS QUANTISATION FLOOR
==============================================================================
The floor is one count of the element's own text field. Without it, a history whose mean motion is constant to the eighth written decimal has a detrended scale of order 1e-8 and the z-score of a one-count change is astronomical. Measured both ways on the same records, on two corpora: the current generator, whose element sets carry orbit-determination jitter, and the superseded version 1 generator, whose mean motion was advanced analytically with no jitter at all and therefore quantised to the written decimal. On the second corpus the candidate is constructed as the robust trend prediction plus exactly one count of the mean-motion field, 1e-8 rev/day, which is the smallest change the text format can express and the case the floor exists for.

corpus                             variant                  n     finite max  infinities  frac > 4
current generator, with jitter     with the floor         217        6.63045           0    0.0553
current generator, with jitter     without the floor      217        6.63045           0    0.0553
version 1 generator, no jitter     with the floor           2              1           0    0.0000
version 1 generator, no jitter     without the floor        2    2.53136e+06           0    1.0000

A correction worth stating plainly. On the CURRENT corpus the floor changes nothing: the largest finite z-score is 6.6304 either way, because the generator's declared mean-motion jitter of 1.5e-8 relative already keeps the robust scale above one count of the field. The blow-up that motivated the floor needs a history with no jitter, which is the superseded version 1 generator, and it is reproduced on that corpus below. The floor is still correct on its own terms -- it is derived from the text format and not from any corpus, and a real catalogue can publish element sets identical to the last written digit -- and its measured effect on the corpus this repository ships is nil. Both facts are published and neither is left to be inferred.

version 1 corpus, largest finite z-score without the floor : 2.531364e+06
version 1 corpus, largest finite z-score with the floor    : 1.000000
version 1 corpus, infinities without the floor             : 0 of 2
version 1 corpus, infinities with the floor                : 0 of 2
version 1 corpus, ratio of the two                         : 2.531364e+06
[PASS] the_floor_matters_on_a_quantised_history -- an unjittered history has a robust scale at the quantiser, so the unfloored z-score of a one-count residual is 2.531e+06 times the floored one
[PASS] the_floor_changes_nothing_on_the_current_corpus -- largest finite z-score 6.6304 either way; reported rather than claimed as a benefit

2. THE NODE UNDER A LEVEL TEST INSTEAD OF A TREND TEST
==============================================================================
The node regresses secularly, so its deviation from the median of a history is the ratio of the candidate's lever arm to the history's, and carries no information about corruption. Measured both ways on the same uncorrupted element sets.

test                     n        min     median        max     spread
level, superseded      217     1.3787     1.4616     1.5246     0.1459
trend, current         217     0.0277     1.3730    24.7032    24.6755

Under the level test the node's z-score lies between 1.3787 and 1.5246 on 217 uncorrupted element sets, a spread of 0.1459. That is a fixed offset, not a measurement.
[PASS] the_level_test_on_the_node_is_a_fixed_offset -- spread 0.1459 over 217 records

The propagated disagreement between consecutive uncorrupted element sets of the same object, which is what the continuity screen measures. A generator whose own histories disagree by hundreds of kilometres makes that screen measure the generator.

leo_low_inclination: 21.03 km, then 112.09 km, then 0.0564 km. The second step is the Kozai inverse of equation (18) alone and it is worth a factor of 1987.
leo_sun_synchronous: 49.33 km, then 603.64 km, then 0.0231 km. The second step is the Kozai inverse of equation (18) alone and it is worth a factor of 26175.
The two superseded versions are not ordered against each other and the measurement says so: version 2 is worse than version 1 on both families here, because writing a Brouwer mean motion into the Kozai field is a systematic half-kilometre error in the semi-major axis while version 1's fault was a kinematic mean anomaly whose error happens to be smaller on these two orbits. Version 3 beats both by three orders of magnitude, which is the only ordering claimed.

# Dataset card — the committed per-pass contact record

`data/passes.csv`, 1990 passes, 754690 bytes, SHA-256 recorded in
`data/manifest.json` and checked by `validation/validate_record.py`.

**This model is not certified for operational flight use.** The dataset it was
fitted on is synthetic in every respect except the orbit, and the rest of this
card says exactly which parts those are.

## What is real and what is declared

**Real and published — the orbit, and only the orbit.**

Catalogue object 06251, "DELTA 1 DEB", epoch 2006-06-25, taken verbatim from
the official SGP4 verification element set `SGP4-VER.TLE` that the `sgp4`
package installs beside its own source and which originates in Vallado, D. A.,
Crawford, P., Hujsak, R. and Kelso, T. S. (2006), "Revisiting Spacetrack
Report #3", AIAA/AAS Astrodynamics Specialist Conference, AIAA 2006-6753:

```
1 06251U 62025E   06176.82412014  .00008885  00000-0  12808-3 0  3985
2 06251  58.0579  54.0425 0030035 139.1568 221.1854 15.56387291  6774
```

Inclination 58.0579 degrees, eccentricity 0.0030035, mean motion 15.56387291
revolutions per day: a moderate-drag low-Earth case, which is the regime a
contact schedule is actually written for.
`validation/validate_geometry.py` compares those two lines character for
character against the installed file and compares the propagation against the
package's own `tcppver.out` reference output, 25 epochs from 0 to 2880 minutes
since epoch, to a worst position disagreement of 7.266e-09 km.

**Declared and synthetic — everything else.**

- **The eight ground stations.** `GS-01` through `GS-08` in
  `passyield.dataset.STATIONS`. The geodetic coordinates are declared
  placements chosen to span latitude and to give eight visibly different pass
  statistics; the figures of merit (19.5 to 24.0 dB/K), zenith attenuations
  (0.30 to 0.70 dB) and elevation masks (10, 12 and 15 degrees) are declared
  design values. **None of them is a measurement of, or a claim about, any
  real ground station or network.** The names are deliberately neutral so
  that no row of the catalogue can be read as a statement about a named
  facility.
- **The spacecraft link budget.** `passyield.link.DEFAULT_BUDGET`: 6.0 dBW
  EIRP, 8.2 GHz, 50 MHz, 3.0 dB implementation margin, 5.0 bit/s/Hz spectral
  efficiency ceiling, giving a 250 Mbit/s rate ceiling. Declared design values
  for a synthetic vehicle, **not measurements of any flown spacecraft**. The
  *model forms* are cited (Friis 1946; ITU-R P.618; Shannon 1948; Maral et al.
  2020 ch. 5); the *parameters* are declared.
- **The whole event timeline.** Acquisition times, loss-of-lock events, outage
  durations and idle periods. **No part of the timeline is a measurement of
  any real mission.**

## How it was generated

`data/generate_passes.py`, one declared seed `SEED = 990099`, spawned per pass
through `numpy.random.SeedSequence` so that the draws for a pass do not depend
on how many passes preceded it. Run it from any working directory:

```bash
python data/generate_passes.py
```

It writes `data/passes.csv`, `data/manifest.json` and the transcript
`data/generate_passes_output.txt`, all three committed. It is **not** under
`validation/`, so the release gate does not run it: a generator the gate
re-ran would overwrite the evidence the validation scripts pin.

Campaign: 60 days from the element set's own epoch, 5 s coarse scan step,
windows below a declared 60 s bookable threshold discarded because a scheduler
does not book a grazing contact. 1990 bookable passes across the eight
stations; the shortest retained is 60.342 s and the longest 409.648 s.

### The generating mechanism disagrees with baseline 2 on purpose

Baseline 2 assumes a homogeneous Poisson outage process, an exponential outage
duration, a Gamma idle total and independence between the three losses. Three
of those four are deliberately **false** in the generator:

- **Outages are an inhomogeneous Poisson process** with intensity rising as
  `(1/sin(elevation))**1.5` — harder to hold a link at low elevation —
  generated by thinning (Lewis, P. A. W. and Shedler, G. S. (1979),
  "Simulation of nonhomogeneous Poisson processes by thinning", Naval Research
  Logistics Quarterly 26(3), 403-413).
- **Acquisition time depends on the antenna slew angle** from where the dish
  was last pointed and on the pass's peak elevation, so it is not an
  identically distributed draw. Erlang-4 with a mean of
  `base + 0.10 * slew_deg + 14.0 * (1/sin(peak) - 1)` seconds.
- **Idle time is driven by the onboard backlog.** When there is less data
  queued than the window can carry, the link runs dry and the rest of the
  pass is idle. That is a deterministic consequence of a pre-pass state
  variable, and it is exactly the kind of structure a per-pass predictor could
  find and a stationary analytic model cannot.

If the generator matched baseline 2's assumptions, baseline 2 would be exact
by construction and the comparison in `MODEL_CARD.md` would measure nothing.
The disagreement is the experiment, and it is why the trivial null beats the
specified analytic baseline on both splits.

Other declared generator values: onboard generation rate 8.5 Mbit/s between
contacts, storage ceiling 240000 Mbit, initial backlog 60000 Mbit, per-station
base acquisition times 17 to 44 s, per-station zenith outage intensities 0.0010
to 0.0035 per second, per-station mean outage durations 8.5 to 19.0 s,
per-station protocol-stall scales 3.5 to 11.0 s. All of them are literals in
`data/generate_passes.py`.

## Schema

27 columns, derived from the `PassRow` dataclass so that the header and the
code cannot drift. Units are in the names.

`pass_id`, `station`, `rise_jd`, `rise_fr`, `campaign_hour`, `local_hour`,
`duration_s`, `max_elevation_deg`, `min_slant_range_km`, `rise_azimuth_deg`,
`slew_angle_deg`, `backlog_mbit`, `hours_since_last_contact`,
`prior_pass_outage_count`, `acquisition_s`, `outage_count`,
`resync_intervals`, `idle_intervals`, `nominal_mbit`, `capacity_mbit`,
`realised_mbit`, `shortfall_mbit`, `geometry_mbit`, `acquisition_mbit`,
`resync_mbit`, `idle_mbit`, `residual_mbit`.

Two precision decisions, both forced by measurement and both documented in
`passyield.dataset`:

- `rise_jd` and `rise_fr` are written at **seventeen** significant digits,
  and the Julian date is renormalised so the fractional part stays inside
  `[0, 1)`. At nine digits the mask crossing resolved to about 9 ms, which
  moved the endpoint elevation by 2e-4 degrees and tripped the mask-clamp
  guard in `passyield.decompose` on reconstruction.
- The event intervals are written to **nine** decimal places of a second, not
  six. The active set's endpoints come from those fields, so a rounding there
  times the 250 Mbit/s ceiling is a volume error: at six places that was
  2.5e-4 Mbit per endpoint, large enough on a small pass to need a tolerance
  that would hide a real defect.

**The event timeline is stored in full, not only as totals.** That is
deliberate: `validation/validate_record.py` recomputes every decomposition
column from `acquisition_s`, `resync_intervals` and `idle_intervals` and
compares it against the committed column, **0 of 1990 passes outside
tolerance**, worst absolute difference 1.825e-04 Mbit against a derived bound
of 1e-3 Mbit. Storing only the totals would make the committed decomposition
unfalsifiable from the committed record.

## Splits

Declared in `passyield.dataset` before any measurement and unchanged since.

- `station_split` holds out `GS-03` and `GS-08` whole: 1443 training passes,
  547 held out, no station on both sides.
- `time_split` holds out the last 25 per cent of the campaign chronologically:
  1492 training, 498 held out, every held-out pass later than every training
  pass.

## Limitations of the dataset

1. **It is synthetic.** The only real thing in it is the orbit. No conclusion
   in this repository transfers to a real contact record without being
   measured again on one, and there is no real record here to measure against.
2. **One element set over 60 days.** SGP4 is a mean-elements model whose
   accuracy against a TLE degrades over days, and real element sets are
   refreshed on roughly that cadence. Propagating one for 60 days is a
   deliberate, documented over-extension so that the whole campaign
   regenerates from two committed lines. The windows are internally consistent
   and are not an orbit-determination claim.
3. **No weather.** The zenith attenuation is a constant per station. A real
   Ka- or even X-band link has a rain-fade time series with heavy tails, which
   would add a term the decomposition does not have.
4. **No antenna pattern and no pointing error.** The spacecraft EIRP toward
   the station is constant over the pass.
5. **The outage process is Poisson with an elevation-dependent intensity, and
   nothing else.** A real link loses lock in bursts correlated with
   interference, multipath at low elevation and ground-equipment faults. The
   generator's correlation structure is one elevation term and no more.
6. **44 passes have an acquisition time longer than the whole window.** That
   is a real operational outcome and it is in the record on purpose — an edge
   case that only ever appears in a constructed test is an edge case nobody has
   measured — but it means 2.2 per cent of the rows have a target of exactly
   zero, which no regression model can hit exactly.
7. **The record's own printing precision is a floor on reproducibility.**
   Columns are written at nine significant digits, so a recomputation from the
   committed events agrees to about 1.8e-04 Mbit absolutely and not better.
   The bound is derived and the margin is measured in
   `validation/validate_record.py`.

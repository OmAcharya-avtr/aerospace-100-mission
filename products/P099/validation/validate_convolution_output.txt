2. TWO METHODS -- the convolution against a direct Monte Carlo
==============================================================================
the tolerance for each quantile is DERIVED per pass, not tuned, from two
terms.

DISCRETISATION. A discrete convolution adds BIN INDICES, not values, so
each convolution stage can displace the result by up to one grid step. The
number of stages on a pass is one for the acquisition law, one for the idle
law, and one per outage in the compound Poisson sum, which is truncated at
the Poisson 1-in-1e9 quantile. The discretisation term is therefore
(1 + stages) grid steps, and `stages` is printed per pass so the term can
be checked rather than taken on trust.

SAMPLING. The Monte Carlo quantile's standard error is
4*sigma*sqrt(p(1-p)/n), using 1/(4 sigma) as a crude lower bound on the
density of a unimodal law. Three of those.

THE STRUCTURAL APPROXIMATION, MEASURED. The convolution treats the three
losses as independent and additive. They are not: inside one window the
intervals must be disjoint, so any probability either method puts above the
window capacity is probability the independence assumption invented. Both
methods report it, and the two must agree, because they share the
assumption.

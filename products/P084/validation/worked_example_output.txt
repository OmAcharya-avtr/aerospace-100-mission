A rejection is evidence AGAINST exchangeability: the bootstrap and split-
conformal limits have no guarantee on that sample. A non-rejection is not
evidence FOR exchangeability; these two statistics have power against drift
and against a step change and little against anything else.

At n = 15 the measured POWER of this test against the swept-card
alternative is 0.43, so a non-rejection here is close to uninformative.
That is itself the answer: a fifteen-point campaign cannot establish the
assumption its tighter bounds need, so the distribution-free bound is the
one to quote.

target 'mean' bounds E[d]; target 'quantile' covers a future measurement.
The two answer different questions and are not comparable row to row.
dist-free = NO means the number rests on a distributional or asymptotic
assumption and carries no finite-sample guarantee.

Wilks' nonparametric tolerance limit does not exist at n = 15:
  Wilks' limit at proportion 0.95 and confidence 0.95 needs n >= 59 by equation (7) (ln(0.05)/ln(0.95)); got n = 15. The largest of 15 observations does not cover 0.95 of the population at that confidence, and returning it anyway would be the defect this package exists to avoid.

the split-conformal limit does not exist at n = 15:
  split conformal at alpha = 0.05 needs n >= 19 calibration scores by equation (12); got n = 15, which asks for the 16-th smallest of 15. There is no finite conformal quantile at this sample size.

hoeffding: exact: the Hoeffding slack of equation (3) depends only on R, delta and n, so this count is a guarantee about the SLACK. It is not a guarantee about the value of the bound, which also moves with the sample mean.
empirical_bernstein: plug-in: equation (11) uses the sample variance of the 15 measurements in hand as a stand-in for the population variance, so this count is a PREDICTION. Its measured hit rate at this sample size is in validation/validate_inversion_output.txt.
wilks_tolerance: existence: equation (7), n >= ln(delta)/ln(p). Below 59 measurements the largest observation does not cover 0.95 of the population at confidence 0.95, whatever its value.
split_conformal: existence: equation (12) asks for the ceil((n+1)*0.95)-th smallest of n calibration scores, which is above n until n = 19. This is a MARGINAL guarantee, weaker than the Wilks row above it.

## 2. Bounds from mathematics this implementation does not use

Raw output: `validate_independent_bound_output.txt`.

### A. Hoeffding against the exact binomial tail

For the two-point distribution on `{0, R}` — the extremal case for Hoeffding's
inequality at fixed range, so where the bound is tightest — the left-hand side
of the inequality is a finite binomial sum, evaluated with
`scipy.stats.binom.sf`. The implementation computes the exponential closed
form and never forms a binomial coefficient, a factorial or a sum.

- **72 grid points** over `n ∈ {10…500}`, `p ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5}`,
  `t/R ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35}`.
- **0 violations.**
- **worst exact/bound ratio 0.460412** — the bound is neither violated nor
  vacuous everywhere; a bound never within a decade of the truth would be
  untestable.
- The comparison is made in **log space** with an allowance of
  `64·eps·|log(bound)|`, because the two sides span nineteen decades across
  the grid and an absolute allowance on the probabilities would be vacuous at
  one end and impossible at the other. The slack follows the conditioning of
  the bound expression, not an absolute constant.

### What the learned model is for, measured separately from its coverage

No marginal bound in this package can say the twin is worse at Mach 0.9. The
model can, and that is its own measurement, against the true conditional mean
gap of equation (22) over the pool.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 | 0.5371 | 0.2707 | 0.025300 |

| 40 | 0.6358 | 0.1887 | 0.031333 |

Discrimination and calibration are different things. At these sample sizes

quantile below the median — runs at 2 to 4 per cent of conditions. It is
reported rather than sorted away, because sorting hides how thin the training
data was.

**A control that could not fire, found and fixed during this build.** The
first version of the Hoeffding control corrupted the bound by SCALING the
slack down, at factors of 0.5 and 0.25, and **it did not fire**. The measured
reason is now printed in the output: at n=40 the sample mean of 40 gaps has a
sampling sd of 0.61 kN.m and is unbiased, so the bound reduced to the bare
sample mean — slack factor **0.00** — still covers the true mean on 48 per cent
of draws and at factor 0.25 it covers on 100 per cent. The corruption was
smaller than the quantity it had to overcome, which is exactly the too-weak
control defect this mission has shipped before. The must-fire controls are now
SIGN errors, and the margin below the truth, in standard deviations of the
sample mean, is computed and printed so the control is guaranteed to fire by
construction rather than by luck.

That measurement also says something about the bound: Hoeffding's inequality
on a population with `R = 40 kN.m` and a mean of 5.1 is loose enough that its
entire slack could be deleted without losing coverage at n=40. That is the
price of distribution-freeness, and it is why this package reports the slack in
its own column.

## Defects found during this build

Four, all pinned as named regression tests with literal inputs in
`tests/test_regressions.py`, because `.hypothesis/` is gitignored and a
counterexample found by one run is otherwise lost on a fresh clone.

1. **`conformal_required_n` was wrong at α = 0.10 and α = 0.20.** The first
   implementation computed `ceil(1/(1−level)) − 1` with `level = 1−α`,
   reconstructing α from its own complement. `1 − (1 − 0.10)` is
   `0.09999999999999998`, whose reciprocal is `10.000000000000002`, whose
   ceiling is 11, so the function returned 10 where the answer is 9; and 5
   where the answer is 4 at α = 0.20. The reciprocal is now taken from α
   directly and reconciled against `conformal_order_index` by a short walk, so
   the two functions cannot disagree about where the limit begins to exist.
2. **The trend statistic reported a perfect drift on a constant sample.** The
   first implementation ranked by `argsort(argsort(x))`, which breaks ties by
   position, so `numpy.full(10, 3.0)` produced the rank vector `[0…9]` and a
   correlation with the index of exactly 1.0. Average ranks via
   `scipy.stats.rankdata` give a constant rank vector, a zero denominator and
   the documented 0.0.
3. **The rank-invariance property is false where the map underflows.**
   Hypothesis falsified it at 300 examples with `values = [5e-324, 0.0, 0.0]`
   and `scale = 0.5`: `0.5 × 5e-324` underflows the denormal minimum to exactly
   0.0, the three values become tied, and the statistic falls from
   −0.8660254037844387 to 0.0. That is not a defect in the statistic — a
   strictly increasing real map is not strictly increasing in float64 once it
   collapses two representable numbers onto one — so the property test now
   assumes distinctness is preserved and the counterexample records what
   happens when it is not.
4. **The coverage standard error omitted the held-out pool's own error.**
   Equation (25) estimates each repetition's coverage on a fixed pool of `m`
   conditions, so the reported coverage inherits the pool's sampling error as
   well as the repetition error. At `m = 4000` and `p = 0.95` the pool term
   alone is 0.0034, larger than the repetition term at 300 repetitions. The
   first version reported only the repetition term and consequently declared
   split conformal — which is exactly marginally valid by construction —
   measurably under-covering at n=60 on a measured 0.9454 against 0.95 with a
   stated error of 0.0019. With the pool term, equation (27), the stated error
   is 0.0039 and the same measurement is within one and a half standard errors,
   which is what the theory says it should be.

3. what the learned model is for: CONDITIONAL discrimination
==============================================================================
No marginal bound in this package can say the twin is worse at Mach 0.9.
The learned model can, and that is measured here against the true
conditional mean gap of equation (22), separately from its coverage.

n   rank correlation with the true conditional mean  +/- sd over 12 draws  quantile crossing rate
--  -----------------------------------------------  --------------------  ----------------------
15  0.5371                                           0.2707                0.025300              
25  0.6007                                           0.2732                0.043600              
40  0.6358                                           0.1887                0.031333              
60  0.6793                                           0.1385                0.019867              

The correlation is Spearman's, between the model's conditional median and
the true conditional mean gap, over the pool ordered by the truth. It is
the model's discrimination and not its calibration: a model can rank
conditions perfectly and still under-cover, and at these sample sizes it
does both.

Quantile crossing is the symptom of thin training data: three independently
fitted ensembles can return an upper quantile below the median. The rate is
reported rather than sorted away, because sorting hides how thin the data
was.

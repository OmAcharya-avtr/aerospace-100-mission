Each test named above is accounted for below by the reason its assertion
rests on an absolute bound rather than a relative one. A comparison against
an exact zero has no relative bound at all and must use an absolute one; an
assertion on a quantity whose natural scale is absolute is the same case.
Anything else would be a defect and is named as such.

  - a published tolerance-factor table value: the table is printed to three
    decimals, so the agreement is an ABSOLUTE 1e-3 by construction and a
    relative bound would be testing which printing the table came from.
  - a sample mean compared against an exact arithmetic value, 2.5 from
    [1,2,3,4]: an absolute 1e-15 on a quantity of order 1.
  - a rank correlation compared against exactly 1.0, -1.0 or 0.6: a
    dimensionless quantity bounded in [-1, 1], where a relative bound has
    no meaning near zero and the identity is exact in exact arithmetic.
  - a Wilks or conformal limit compared against the largest value of a
    linspace, 9.0: the limit IS that order statistic, so the comparison is
    against an exactly representable constant.
  - a bootstrap slack on a constant sample compared against exactly 0.0,
    where every resample has the same mean: a relative bound on zero does
    not exist.
  - a coverage complement or a clip fraction compared against 1/3: an
    exact rational whose float64 representation is the one the
    implementation computes.
  - the asymptotic limit of the tolerance factor compared against the
    normal quantile with an absolute 0.01: the convergence rate is what is
    being tested, so the tolerance is the claim.

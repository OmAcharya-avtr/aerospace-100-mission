1. the mean bound: a corrupted slack must lose coverage
==============================================================================
The corruption is a factor on the slack of equation (3). The factor needed
to GUARANTEE a miss is not assumed: it is derived from the sampling
standard deviation of the sample mean at this n, printed below, so that the
control cannot be too weak to fire. A control that cannot fire reads as
evidence and is worse than no control.

REPORTED AS FOUND, and it changed the design of this control. A slack
SCALED DOWN does not lose coverage at n = 40 at any factor down to 0.25, and
not even at 0.00 -- the bound reduced to the bare sample mean still covers
the true mean on essentially every draw, because the sample mean of 40 gaps
has a sampling sd of about 0.61 kN.m and is unbiased. The first version of
this control used factors of 0.5 and 0.25 and did NOT fire, which is exactly
the too-weak-control defect this mission has shipped before: the corruption
was smaller than the quantity it had to overcome. The must-fire controls
above are therefore SIGN errors, whose margin below the truth is computed
from the sampling sd and printed, so the control is guaranteed to fire by
construction rather than by luck.

The same measurement says something about the bound itself: Hoeffding's
inequality on a population with R = 40 kN.m and a mean of 5.1 is loose
enough that its entire slack could be deleted without losing coverage at
this sample size. That is the cost of distribution-freeness and it is the
reason this package reports the slack in its own column.

FINDING  CUSUM is the fastest of the three declared baselines on all three
FINDING  change types at a matched ARL0. The windowed GLR is the slowest on
FINDING  the parameter step and the slow ramp. This is the opposite of the
FINDING  expectation that a GLR would be hard to beat, and the structural
FINDING  reason is in the two statistics: a CUSUM accumulates without bound
FINDING  so a persistent shift drives it linearly for as long as it lasts,
FINDING  while a windowed GLR truncates accumulation at its window and pays
FINDING  a maximum-over-onset penalty that raises the threshold needed for
FINDING  the same ARL0. The GLR's advantage is that it needs no declared
FINDING  shift magnitude, and that advantage is not visible in a comparison
FINDING  where the CUSUM's reference value happens to suit the change.

ANSWER   the noise-variance change. Both it and the parameter step are
ANSWER   fully present from sample 0, yet every method takes between 3 and
ANSWER   4 times as long to see it. The structural reason is exact rather
ANSWER   than statistical: a process-noise change leaves E[z] at 0, so the
ANSWER   quantity all three baselines accumulate has no post-change drift
ANSWER   at all, and they fire only on the increased rate of tail
ANSWER   excursions. The slow ramp's delay looks comparable in samples but
ANSWER   is a different kind of number: the CUSUM alarms after
ANSWER   232 samples of a 400-sample
ANSWER   ramp, i.e. when the change has reached 58 % of its
ANSWER   final magnitude, which is close to the best a causal detector can
ANSWER   do against a change that is not yet there.

ANSWER   And using the right statistic does not fix it. The variance-CUSUM
ANSWER   oracle, which is handed the post-change residual variance and
ANSWER   accumulates the sufficient statistic z^2 - 1 for a scale change, is
ANSWER   the SLOWEST method on the noise-variance change (467
ANSWER   samples against 211 for the mis-specified mean CUSUM),
ANSWER   because at this effect size its per-sample standardised shift is
ANSWER   only 0.059/sqrt(2) = 0.042 -- Var(z^2) = 2 under H0 -- while the
ANSWER   mean CUSUM's alarm rate responds to residual scale through its
ANSWER   exponentially sensitive tail. Correct specification is not the
ANSWER   same as more information per sample, and this is a measured
ANSWER   counterexample to the usual advice.

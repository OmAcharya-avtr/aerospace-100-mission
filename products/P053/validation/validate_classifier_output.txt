FINDING  1. In distribution the learned classifier does not beat the
FINDING  CUSUM on the parameter step at a comparable false-alarm budget,
FINDING  beats it on the slow ramp, and LOSES heavily on the
FINDING  noise-variance change.
FINDING
FINDING  2. Where it does win, the win is not a win for learning. It is a
FINDING  specification advantage: the training set contains only negative
FINDING  gain changes of a known size, so the classifier is effectively a
FINDING  one-sided test tuned to that magnitude, while the two-sided CUSUM
FINDING  spends half its false-alarm budget on the other direction and is
FINDING  tuned to a declared 0.5-sigma shift rather than the true 0.40.
FINDING
FINDING  3. The out-of-distribution results settle it. On a gain change of
FINDING  the SAME MAGNITUDE and the OPPOSITE SIGN the classifier's
FINDING  detection fraction collapses while every baseline is unaffected,
FINDING  because a baseline's statistic does not know which way the
FINDING  residual is supposed to move and the classifier does. A monitor
FINDING  that cannot see half of the changes it was not trained on is not
FINDING  a better monitor, whatever its average delay.
FINDING
FINDING  4. On an out-of-distribution process-noise change (x 5 instead
FINDING  of the trained x 2) the classifier is also beaten by every
FINDING  baseline, so the loss on variance changes is not confined to the
FINDING  magnitude it was trained on.
FINDING
FINDING  5. The learned monitor costs about three orders of magnitude more
FINDING  per decision than the CUSUM recursion, and cannot alarm earlier
FINDING  than its 50-sample window fills.
FINDING
FINDING  Recommendation, stated against this package's own AI component:
FINDING  use the CUSUM. The learned classifier earns its place only as a
FINDING  measurement of how much a change-specific detector could gain,
FINDING  and none of that gain survives a sign flip, a different variance
FINDING  magnitude, or the cost of a forest evaluation per sample.

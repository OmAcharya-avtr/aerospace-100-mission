negative control, anchored on the true value
==============================================================================
  The guaranteed-firing offset for a check that accepts a quantity within b
  of a reference is strictly MORE THAN 2b, measured from the true value,
  because the unperturbed quantity could already sit anywhere in [-b, b].
  Smaller offsets may or may not fire depending on where it actually sits,
  and that is the trap: a control built on an offset of b is not a control.
  Here the unperturbed residual sits near the bottom of the band, so an
  offset of 2b fires -- which is sufficient but was not guaranteed in
  advance, and the table below separates the two cases.

  declared tolerance b          : 6.0000e-09
  unperturbed residual          : 1.1307e-09 (0.1885 b)

  offset (x b)   residual     fires?   guaranteed to fire?
           0.5   1.8693e-09   no       no
           1.0   4.8693e-09   no       no
           2.0   1.0869e-08   yes      no
           3.0   1.6869e-08   yes      yes
  [PASS] an offset of 3.0 b fires, as it must -- residual 1.6869e-08 against tolerance 6.0000e-09

  The 0.5 b row is the point of the whole table: it does not fire, so a
  negative control built on an offset of half the tolerance would have
  silently passed a check that was not testing anything.

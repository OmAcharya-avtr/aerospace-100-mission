the band joins are discontinuous, and by how much
==============================================================================
  The published table is 28 independent exponential fits, not one smooth
  function, so the joins do not match exactly. The jumps are MEASURED
  below rather than assumed, and the measurement changes the conclusion:
  the largest is about 0.14 per cent, which is small enough that the
  integrator's measured convergence order with drag on is
  indistinguishable from its order with drag off (4.6978 / 4.5370 /
  4.3688 against 4.6990 / 4.5386 / 4.3701 in
  validate_step_refinement_output.txt). The order loss a discontinuous
  right-hand side can cause is therefore a real mechanism that is not
  material here, and saying so is more useful than warning about it.

==============================================================================
what the static model cannot do
==============================================================================
  Published solar-cycle variation of thermospheric density at 400 km is
  about an order of magnitude between solar minimum and solar maximum
  (Vallado 2013 section 8.6.2; Picone et al. 2002 for NRLMSISE-00). This
  model has no solar index at all, so it sits at one unspecified point in
  that range. The consequence for this product, stated plainly: a
  divergence curve driven by drag inherits that factor, and the curve is
  therefore a measurement of model divergence under a declared static
  atmosphere and not a prediction of what the drag will actually do.

extrapolation above the table
==============================================================================
  Above 1000 km the final band is extrapolated. The drag acceleration
  there is far below the integrator's truncation floor, so the
  extrapolation cannot affect a reported number; the magnitudes are
  published so a reader can confirm that rather than take it on trust.
  altitude km   density kg/m^3   |a_drag| km/s^2 at C_D A/m = 1e-2 m^2/kg
       1000.0   3.019000e-15   7.396550e-13

  The 1e-2 m^2/kg drag parameter above is a high-area-to-mass object. For

  1e-3 m^2/kg, a tenth of that.

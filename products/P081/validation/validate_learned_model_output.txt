4. the pair test: the same input, two different truths
==============================================================================
Each `equivalent` case shares a noise seed with its `single` partner and
their observations differ by at most two units in the last place, so any
classifier MUST return the same answer for both. Measured here rather than
argued: the fraction of pairs on which the learned classifier agrees with
itself, and the fraction on which the analytic path agrees with itself.

pairs                                          : 120
pairs on which the learned label agrees        : 120
pairs on which the analytic answer agrees      : 120
mean learned confidence over both members      : 0.993426
least learned confidence over both members     : 0.211119

The two members of a pair have DIFFERENT true causes. A classifier that
agrees with itself on a pair is therefore wrong on one member of it, by
construction and whatever it answers. That is not a defect of the
classifier: it is what an unidentifiable subspace means, and the point is
that the classifier cannot say so while the analytic path does.

[PASS] the learned classifier gives the same answer to both members of every pair -- 120 of 120
[PASS] the analytic path gives the same answer to both members of every pair -- 120 of 120; that answer is REFUSED

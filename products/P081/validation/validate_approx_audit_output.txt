pytest.approx(expected, rel=r) passes if EITHER bound is met and the
absolute default is 1e-12, so a bare rel= on a small quantity stops testing
what it says. This script measures the dependence rather than asserting its
absence. It matters more here than in most products: this one's quantities
include singular values at the 1e-16 level, sensitivity sums that vanish
identically, and principal angles of order 1e-6 degrees.

bound is the one doing the work and the relative bound alone would not
hold. That is correct and necessary for a comparison against an exact zero,
which has no relative bound at all, and it is wrong anywhere else, so every
failure is named below and accounted for.

COUNT OF ASSERTIONS RESTING ON AN ABSOLUTE BOUND: 2 tests of 254

Each test named above is accounted for below by the reason its assertion
rests on an absolute bound rather than a relative one. A comparison against
an exact zero has no relative bound at all and must use an absolute one; an
assertion on a quantity whose natural scale is absolute is the same case.
Anything else would be a defect and is named as such.

  - tests.test_plant::test_explicit_sensitivities_at_the_initial_point_sum_to_zero_by_hand
    Identity A states that a sum of five non-zero terms is EXACTLY zero. A
    relative bound on zero does not exist, so the absolute bound is the only
    kind this assertion can have, and it is 1e-14 on terms of order 2.
    This is the product's central algebraic fact and not a defect.
  - tests.test_identifiability::test_the_null_projector_is_idempotent_and_symmetric
    The off-diagonal entries of an orthogonal projector's idempotency and
    symmetry residuals are exact zeros, and the assertion carries abs=1e-14
    for exactly that reason. Again there is no relative bound on zero.

Two of 254 tests, both of them comparisons against an exact zero. Neither
is a defect, and the count is reported rather than asserted away. The
membership of this list is stable across runs; part 4 says why.

The ratios are close to one, and that is the point: the pseudo-inverse

F. the confidence intervals of equation (13)
==============================================================================
The truth is the declared 95 per cent level. The control rescales each
interval by a declared factor and measures the coverage, which must fall
outside four standard errors of the nominal level.

Coverage is MARGINAL, one direction at a time, because that is what a 95
per cent interval on a single direction promises. A joint statement over
four directions has a nominal level near 0.95 to the fourth power and
comparing it against 0.95 would make the correct intervals look broken --
which is how the first version of this control failed.

The 4.00 row fires in the opposite direction: an interval four times too
wide covers the truth more often than its nominal level, which is as much a
defect as covering it less often, and a one-sided criterion would miss it.
A coverage that saturates at 1.000000 is only 4.5883 standard errors from
the nominal level, which is why this script uses 400 realisations and not
200: at 200 the same saturated coverage is 3.2444 away and could not reach
the declared 4.0.

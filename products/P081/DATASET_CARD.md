# Dataset card — twindiverge synthetic cases 0.1.0

Research-grade. **No measurement of any real reaction wheel, spacecraft, test
article or flight computer appears anywhere in this repository.** Not
flight-qualified, not certified, not approved for operational aerospace use.

## What the data are

Simulated telemetry from a declared single-state reaction-wheel model, equation
(1) of `twindiverge.plant`, with additive Gaussian sensor noise of declared
standard deviation. Each case is one "reality": a parameter vector, a noise
seed, and the telemetry a declared campaign would record from it.

**Nothing is committed as a data file.** The generator is committed instead
(`twindiverge.synthetic`), one case costs about 2 ms for the longest campaign,
and every case is a deterministic function of a declared integer seed. A reader
with a cold clone regenerates the data rather than downloading it, and gets the
same bits.

## Source

| item | value |
| --- | --- |
| provenance | fully synthetic, generated by `twindiverge.synthetic` and `twindiverge.plant` |
| underlying model | equation (1): `J dω/dt = k_t i(t) - T_c - b ω - c_d ω²` |
| model form, cited | Armstrong-Helouvry, Dupont & Canudas de Wit 1994, Automatica 30(7), 1083-1138, section 2, for the Coulomb-plus-viscous decomposition; Bialke 1998, 21st Annual AAS Guidance and Control Conference, AAS 98-063, for the same applied to a spacecraft reaction wheel with a motor torque constant |
| coefficient values | **declared, not taken from either source and not measured.** A fiction with an engineering shape |
| integrator | classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta, Butcher 2016, 3rd ed., section 232 |
| real-hardware content | none |

### The declared nominal parameters

| parameter | value | unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `J`, wheel plus rotor inertia | 1.30e-02 | kg m^2 |
| `k_t`, motor torque constant | 3.20e-02 | N m / A |
| `T_c`, Coulomb friction torque | 2.50e-03 | N m |
| `b`, viscous coefficient | 1.20e-05 | N m s / rad |
| `c_d`, quadratic drag coefficient | 2.00e-08 | N m s^2 / rad^2 |

Declared validity window: 20 to 400 rad/s, one sign of rotation only.
`simulate` raises `ValueError` outside it.

## Campaigns

| campaign | length | samples | period | excitation | measured rate range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `full` | 200.0 s | 2001 | 0.1 s | +1.00 A for 40 s, then coasting | 100.000000 to 184.222061 rad/s |
| `short` | 20.0 s | 201 | 0.1 s | +1.00 A for 10 s, then coasting | 100.000000 to 121.480544 rad/s |
| `coast` | 60.0 s | 601 | 0.1 s | none | 82.639285 to 100.000000 rad/s |

The current is a zero-order hold sampled once per integration step at the step's
left endpoint, and `drive_duration_s` is constrained to be an exact multiple of
the sample period. Evaluating it per Runge-Kutta stage instead costs three
orders of convergence at the switching step; that was measured during this build
and is recorded in `CHANGELOG.md`.

## Telemetry sets

A telemetry set is a campaign plus the channels recorded during it and their
noise levels. It is the whole input to an identifiability question.

| set | campaign | channels | rows | declared noise | measured rank |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rate_full` | `full` | rate | 2001 | 0.02 rad/s | 4 of 5 |
| `rate_torque_full` | `full` | rate, torque | 4002 | 0.02 rad/s, 2.0e-04 N m | 5 of 5 |
| `rate_short` | `short` | rate | 201 | 0.02 rad/s | 4 of 5 |
| `rate_torque_short` | `short` | rate, torque | 402 | 0.02 rad/s, 2.0e-04 N m | 5 of 5 |
| `rate_coast` | `coast` | rate | 601 | 0.02 rad/s | 3 of 5 |

The noise standard deviations are **declared, not measured**. They set the scale
of every Cramer-Rao bound and confidence region in this repository, and
`validation/validate_identifiability.py` sweeps the record length to show which
conclusions depend on them — the practical ones — and which do not — the
structural ones.

## Case kinds

| kind | construction | label | cases per 120-seed set |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `single` | exactly one parameter moved by a relative amount drawn from [0.02, 0.15] in logarithm, with a random sign | the parameter index | 120 |
| `weak` | the same from [0.002, 0.02], wholly below the range above | the parameter index | 120 |
| `scale` | all five parameters moved by the same amount | `UNIDENTIFIABLE` | 120 |
| `equivalent` | the partner of a `single` case under equation (6): `u·(e_j - 1)`, so parameter `j` stays nominal and the other four move the other way | `MULTI` | 120 |
| `none` | nothing moved | `UNIDENTIFIABLE` | 120 |

The parameter index, the magnitude and the sign are drawn from
`numpy.random.default_rng(seed)` in a fixed order, so a case is a function of
its seed alone.

### Two measured properties of the construction

- A `single` case and its `equivalent` partner produce **identical noiseless
  rate records**: worst relative difference **2.340688e-16** over 120 pairs.
  They share a noise seed, so their observations differ by at most **2.000 units
  in the last place** of the peak rate and 97 of 120 pairs are bit-identical.
- A `scale` case and a `none` case at the same seed produce **bit-identical
  observations**, because a pure scale change is invisible to the rate channel by
  equation (6). Every method's score on those two populations is therefore equal,
  and `validation/validate_learned_model.py` checks that it is.

## Splits

| split | seeds | role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| train | 1000–1239 | the only cases either learned variant is fitted on |
| test | 20000–20119 | every reported score on an answerable fault |
| null | 30000–30119 | the unidentifiable-subspace measurements |
| bound | 50000–50019 | noise realisations for the Cramer-Rao check |
| coverage | 60000–60399 | noise realisations for the interval-coverage check |

All five sets are disjoint; the first three have their disjointness printed in
`validation/validate_learned_model_output.txt`. The split is by case, which here
is the same as by seed, because no two cases share a trajectory or a noise draw.

## Limitations

- **One plant, and a fictional one.** A single-state wheel with a polynomial
  friction model. The coefficients are declared; nothing was fitted to anything.
- **One sign of rotation.** The Coulomb torque is a constant, so a zero
  crossing — the regime where wheel friction identification is actually hard —
  is outside the data entirely.
- **The noise is independent between samples by assumption.** A tachometer that
  differentiates an encoder count produces correlated rate noise, which would
  change every interval reported in this repository. No correlated-noise case
  exists in the dataset.
- **The excitation is a single current step.** Real identification campaigns use
  richer excitations, and a richer excitation would change the practical
  identifiability (though not, by equation (6), the structural kind).
- **Perturbations are sparse by construction.** `single` and `weak` move exactly
  one parameter; `scale` and `equivalent` move all five or four. A realistic
  drift that moved two parameters by different amounts is not represented, and
  the single-parameter hypothesis test of equation (15) would be outside its own
  assumption on such a case.
- **No measurement error on the current.** A current-sensor bias would add a
  sixth parameter collinear with `k_t` on a constant-current segment, which is a
  different degeneracy from the one studied here and is not in the data.
- **The magnitude ranges are declared.** Their floor is above the level at which
  the rate channel detects nothing for the weakest parameter, and their ceiling
  is inside the plant's validity window for every case kind; both were verified
  by running every kind at every campaign during the build.

## Regeneration

```bash
python -c "
import sys; sys.path.insert(0, 'src')
from twindiverge.synthetic import generate_cases
from twindiverge.telemetry import TELEMETRY_SETS
from twindiverge.synthetic import observe
case = generate_cases((20000,), 'single')[0]
print(case.seed, case.label_name, case.delta_log)
print(observe(TELEMETRY_SETS['rate_full'], case)[:3])
"
```

Every validation script regenerates whatever it needs from the seeds in
`validation/_bootstrap.py`.

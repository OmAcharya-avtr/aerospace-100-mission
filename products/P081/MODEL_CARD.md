# Model card — twindiverge learned residual-signature classifier 0.1.0

**This model is not certified for operational flight use.**

Research-grade: not flight-qualified, not certified, not approved for
operational aerospace use. Status TESTING.

## Problem

Given a twin-versus-reality residual time series from a reaction-wheel digital
twin, name the parameter responsible. The five candidate parameters are the
wheel inertia `J`, the motor torque constant `k_t`, the Coulomb friction torque
`T_c`, the viscous coefficient `b` and the quadratic drag coefficient `c_d` of
equation (1) in `twindiverge.plant`.

The model exists to be measured against the analytic path, not to replace it.
The specification that produced this product asks for the learned component to
be scored on accuracy at a fixed computation budget **and separately on its
behaviour inside the unidentifiable subspace**, where the expected result is
that it returns a confident wrong attribution exactly where the analytic method
correctly refuses to answer. That measurement is the product's headline and is
reported in full below.

## Baselines, which come first

Three analytic baselines are implemented and validated before this model is
benchmarked, in this order:

1. **Local identifiability** (`twindiverge.identifiability`): rank,
   conditioning, Fisher information and Cramer-Rao bounds. Its verdict gates
   everything else: a parameter with a component in the null space of the
   sensitivity matrix is `REFUSED` and gets no estimate.
2. **Attribution on the identifiable subspace** (`twindiverge.attribution`):
   generalised least squares with confidence regions, the first-order
   sensitivity attribution of equation (14) that the specification names, and
   the constrained single-parameter maximum likelihood of equation (15), which
   measures stronger on the reference set and is the one this model is
   benchmarked against. Both are reported.
3. **Profile likelihood** (`twindiverge.profile`): the nonlinear reference the
   linearised covariance of 1 and 2 is scored against.

Baselines 1 to 3 were validated against the simulation before this model was
trained — `validation/validate_plant.py`,
`validation/validate_identifiability.py`,
`validation/validate_attribution.py`, `validation/validate_profile.py` and
`validation/validate_independent_bound.py` — and the specified attributor of
equation (14) was found to be weaker than equation (15) on the reference set, so
the stronger one is the benchmark and the weaker one is reported beside it.

## Architecture

`sklearn.ensemble.GradientBoostingClassifier` (Friedman 2001, "Greedy function
approximation: a gradient boosting machine", Annals of Statistics 29(5),
1189-1232) over 16 features of the residual time series.

| hyperparameter | value |
| --- | --- |
| boosting stages | 150 |
| maximum tree depth | 3 |
| learning rate | 0.1 |
| subsampling | none |
| `random_state` | 20261109 |
| classes, `variant="parameter"` | 5: `J`, `k_t`, `T_c`, `b`, `c_d` |
| classes, `variant="with_refusal"` | 6: the above plus `UNIDENTIFIABLE` |

Two declared variants. `parameter` is the specified architecture and has no way
to say "the telemetry cannot tell"; `with_refusal` is an ablation that has a
sixth class and is trained with pure-scale examples of it. Both are scored on
every population.

### Features

16 declared features, listed with their definitions in
`twindiverge.features`: four quarter-means of the scaled residual, four
quarter-slopes, the driven-segment and coasting-segment means, the root mean
square, the peak absolute value, the position of that peak, the correlations of
the residual with the twin's rate and with its square, and the ratio of the
driven to the coasting root mean square.

**What is deliberately not a feature.** The projections of the residual onto the
sensitivity columns, `a_j^T r`, are the sufficient statistics of the linearised
problem. Handing them to a classifier would make it a re-implementation of
baseline 2 with a decision tree on top and would measure nothing. Everything in
the list is computed from the residual, the twin's own trajectory and the logged
current, with no sensitivity matrix and no integration.

## Dataset

Fully synthetic and generated by committed code from declared integer seeds. See
[`DATASET_CARD.md`](DATASET_CARD.md). No data file is committed: the generator
is, and one case costs about 2 ms.

| split | seeds | cases | role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| train | 1000–1239 | 240 single-parameter | the only data either variant is fitted on, plus 240 pure-scale cases for the `with_refusal` variant |
| test | 20000–20119 | 120 per population | every reported score on an answerable fault |
| null | 30000–30119 | 120 per population | the unidentifiable-subspace measurements |

The three seed sets are disjoint and the disjointness is printed in
`validation/validate_learned_model_output.txt`. The split is by CASE, which here
is the same as by seed: each case is generated from its own seed and no two
cases share a trajectory or a noise draw, so there is nothing for a split to
leak.

### Test-split strategy, and the leakage this product cannot have

**There is no decision threshold to select.** The model's label is the argmax of
its own probability vector. A predecessor in this mission selected a learned
decision threshold using the test labels, which reaches perfect recall by
construction; this model has no such surface, and
`tests/test_regressions.py::test_the_learned_threshold_is_never_chosen_on_the_test_population`
asserts that no threshold attribute exists. Hyperparameters are declared
constants in `twindiverge.learned` and were not tuned on any split.

## Populations the model is scored on

| population | what it is | is any single-parameter answer correct? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `single` | exactly one parameter moved, by 2.02 to 16.18 per cent | yes |
| `weak` | the same, a tenth the size, wholly outside the training range | yes |
| `equivalent` | the partner of a `single` case under equation (6): parameter `j` left at nominal and the other four moved the other way | **no** |
| `scale` | all five parameters multiplied by the same factor; the noiseless telemetry is identical to the twin's | **no** |
| `none` | nothing moved | **no** |

`scale` and `none` are the same observations to within the last bit, which is
itself a measured check.

## Metrics

Telemetry set `rate_full`: a 200 s rate-only record, 2001 samples, rank 4 of 5,
all five parameters refused by baseline 1.

### Inside the unidentifiable subspace — the headline

Population `equivalent`, 120 cases.

| method | accuracy | mean confidence | confident and wrong | refusal | ambiguity declared |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| analytic, gated by baseline 1 | **1.000000** | n/a | 0.000000 | **1.000000** | **1.000000** |
| analytic, equation (15) | 0.000000 | 1.000000 | 1.000000 | 0.000000 | 1.000000 |
| **learned, 5 classes** | **0.000000** | **0.993426** | **0.991667** | 0.008333 | **0.000000** |
| learned, 6 classes (ablation) | 0.008333 | 0.994042 | 0.991667 | 0.008333 | 0.000000 |

Exact Clopper-Pearson intervals: accuracy [0.000000, 0.030273],
confident-and-wrong [0.954444, 0.999789]. Brier score **0.993340**, against
0.006568 on ordinary faults.

**The learned classifier returns a confident wrong attribution exactly where
the analytic method correctly refuses to answer.** That is the expected and the
most valuable result in this product. Nothing was tuned to soften it.

The mechanism is exact rather than statistical. Each `equivalent` case shares
its noise seed with its `single` partner, and the two observations differ by at
most **2.000 units in the last place** of the peak rate — 97 of 120 pairs are
bit-identical. Any classifier must therefore answer both the same way, and the
measurement confirms it: the learned classifier agrees with itself on **120 of
120** pairs with mean confidence **0.993426** and least confidence
**0.211119**. The two members of a pair have different true causes, so a method
that agrees with itself on a pair is wrong on one member of it whatever it
answers. The difference between the two paths is not the answer; it is that one
of them says so and prints the family it cannot distinguish.

### Computation budget

| item | seconds |
| --- | --- |
| analytic setup, once per telemetry set | 0.079955 |
| learned fit, once per training set, both variants | 1.704589 |
| analytic attribution per case | 0.000475 |
| learned prediction per case | 0.000929 |

The learned prediction costs **1.956294** times the analytic attribution per
case, and the analytic path's own setup is amortised over a whole campaign. At a
fixed per-case budget the analytic route is both cheaper and more accurate on
this population.

**These four rows are the only numbers in this repository that are not stable
between runs.** They are wall-clock measurements on two shared cores and the
ratio has been observed between 1.96 and 2.16 across runs of the same code; they
are quoted from the committed `validation/validate_learned_model_output.txt` and
should be read as "about a factor of two", not to six decimals. Every other
figure here is a deterministic function of declared seeds.

## Uncertainty and confidence output

**There is no point-prediction method on `ResidualClassifier`.** Every
prediction is a `Prediction` object carrying the full class-probability vector,
the chosen label, the confidence — the largest class probability — the margin
between the largest and second largest, and an abstention flag set when the
confidence falls below the declared threshold of 0.50. A caller who wants only a
label has to take it out of an object that is also telling it how sure the model
is.

The confidence is a model output, not a probability derived from the physics:
nothing in it knows the rank of the sensitivity matrix. Its calibration is
measured per population with a Brier score (Brier 1950, Monthly Weather Review
78(1), 1-3) and a reliability table in
`validation/validate_learned_model_output.txt`. Measured Brier scores:
**0.006568** on `single`, **0.579620** on `weak`, **0.993340** on `equivalent`
and **0.499721** on `scale` and `none`. The model is well calibrated exactly
where the question is well posed and maximally miscalibrated where it is not.

## Failure cases

1. **Inside the unidentifiable subspace it is confidently wrong**: accuracy
   0.000000 at mean confidence 0.993426, confident-and-wrong rate 0.991667. This
   is the headline, measured on 120 held-out cases.
2. **On a pure-noise residual it still answers**, at mean confidence 0.522102
   and a confident-and-wrong rate of 0.991667, abstaining on only 0.008333. The
   analytic detection test refuses all of them.
3. **Out of distribution it degrades much faster than the analytic route.** On
   faults a tenth the training size its accuracy falls to 0.641667 while the
   analytic route holds 0.941667, and its confident-error rate rises to
   0.275000 against 0.041667.
4. **The refusal class does not generalise.** The six-class ablation, trained
   with pure-scale examples, reaches 1.000000 on `scale` and `none` and
   **0.008333** on `equivalent`, where the residual is large and only the
   attribution is impossible. It also costs **0.316667** accuracy on `weak`,
   because the refusal class absorbs small genuine faults. Giving a classifier
   the option of refusing does not give it the ability to tell when to.
5. **It declares no ambiguity anywhere**: 0.000000 on every population, against
   the analytic path's 1.000000 on every rate-only set.
6. **It inherits the conditioning of the problem rather than escaping it.** Two
   of its 16 features are correlations with the twin's rate and with its square,
   and those two are nearly collinear on this plant for the same reason that
   makes the `b` and `c_d` directions ill-conditioned for baseline 1.

## Reproducibility

```bash
python validation/validate_learned_model.py
python examples/learned_confidence.py
```

Both refit the model from the declared seeds and print every number above.
Determinism rests on four declared things: the integer seeds in
`validation/_bootstrap.py` (`TRAIN_SEEDS`, `TEST_SEEDS`, `NULL_SEEDS`), the
`random_state` of 20261109 in `twindiverge.learned`, the fixed class order from
`ResidualClassifier.labels` rather than from the order labels happen to appear
in, and a Hypothesis profile with `derandomize=True` and `database=None` in
`tests/conftest.py` so that the test suite's examples do not vary.

**No model artifact is serialised or committed.** Every model in `validation/`
is refitted from its seed by the script that reports it, so there is no pickle
whose format would need a dependency bound — which is why `pyproject.toml`
bounds `scikit-learn` at the minor version for the fitting determinism alone.

Fitting is reproducible *within* a scikit-learn minor version given the fixed
`random_state`. It is not part of scikit-learn's public API contract across
versions, and the measured version is 1.9.1.

## Compute used

2 CPU cores, no GPU. The whole learned study — 240 training cases, two fitted
variants, five populations of 120 held-out cases, five methods scored on each —
runs in **4.197 s**. Training alone is 1.704589 s for both variants. Nothing in
this product approaches the three-minute budget its build guide sets.

## Ethical and safety limits

- **Not certified for operational flight use.** This statement is printed by
  `python -m twindiverge learned` and is repeated here deliberately.
- The model is fitted on, and validated against, **synthetic data from a
  declared fictional plant**. No measurement of any real reaction wheel,
  spacecraft, test article or flight computer appears anywhere in this
  repository. Nothing here has been shown to transfer to real hardware, and the
  declared noise model — independent Gaussian samples of known variance — is an
  assumption about a sensor and not a property of one.
- The model's documented failure mode is **confident wrong attribution**. An
  engineer acting on its answer inside an unidentifiable subspace would replace
  a component that had not changed. That is the reason the analytic gate exists
  and the reason it runs first, and it is why the gated path — not the
  classifier — is what `twindiverge.pipeline.CaseAnalysis.gated_answer` returns.
- A high confidence from this model is **not evidence that the question has an
  answer**. The only thing in this repository that speaks to that is baseline
  1's verdict.

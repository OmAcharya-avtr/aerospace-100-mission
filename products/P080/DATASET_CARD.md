# Dataset card — boundcompress synthetic telemetry

**No flight data is used anywhere in this repository.** Every number in
`README.md`, `validation/VALIDATION.md` and `MODEL_CARD.md` is a statement
about the generator described here and about nothing else. A compression ratio
measured on a sine wave is a statement about sine waves.

## Source

`boundcompress.dataset`, a committed seeded generator. Nothing is downloaded,
nothing is stored: the arrays are regenerated from a seed on every run, so the
repository ships no data file and `tests/test_dataset.py` asserts that the
same seed gives the same array bit-for-bit.

## Channels

Time is t[k] = k / f_s seconds with f_s = 1 Hz by default. The orbital period
is 5400 s, a round number in the low-Earth-orbit range, and the eclipse
fraction is 0.35. g[k] denotes independent standard normal draws from a seeded
`numpy.random.Generator`. The full equations are in the module docstring of
`boundcompress/dataset.py`.

| channel | unit | shape | why it is in the set |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `battery_voltage` | V | 27.0 + 1.6 sin(2πt/T) - 0.9·eclipse + 0.004 g | smooth orbital ripple with a load step; strictly positive, so `mode="rel"` applies |
| `panel_temperature` | K | first-order thermal relaxation, τ = 600 s, setpoint 318 K in sun and 243 K in eclipse, + 0.01 g | the easiest case: a low-pass output is close to piecewise linear |
| `wheel_rate` | rad/s | piecewise-linear momentum ramps between random setpoints in [-250, 250] with a slew limit of 0.8 rad/s², + 0.02 g | crosses zero, so `mode="rel"` refuses it, and that refusal is tested |
| `gyro_rate` | rad/s | 0.01 sin(2πt/T) + 0.003 sin(2πt/97) + 0.0012 g | hard but realistic: the noise is what sets the achievable rate |
| `magnetometer` | µT | 24 sin(2πt/T) + 7 sin(4πt/T + 1.1) + 0.05 g | two-tone with a small noise floor |
| `packet_counter` | counts | monotone ramp of 1 per sample, reset every 1024 | shows the coder's floor when prediction works perfectly |
| `white_noise` | 1 | g | the incompressible control; any coder claiming a large ratio here is measuring its own header |
| `step_transient` | 1 | twelve constant levels in [-5, 5] with 0.001 g | adversarial for an extrapolating predictor: every step costs one large residual |

Default length 4096 samples, chosen so every measurement in this repository
finishes inside the three-minute compute budget on two cores.

## Splits

| split | call | samples | seeds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| evaluation | `dataset.generate_all()` | 4096 | 0..7 |
| training (learned predictor) | `dataset.training_signals()` | 2048 | 1000..1007 |

The split is by **noise realisation**, not by channel. The learned predictor
is evaluated on signal *shapes* it has seen with noise it has not. This is the
easier of the two possible splits and is stated as such in `MODEL_CARD.md`; a
split by channel would be the harder question and was not run.

`packet_counter` is noise-free by construction, so its training and evaluation
arrays coincide. `tests/test_dataset.py::test_training_signals_are_disjoint_from_the_evaluation_set`
excludes it explicitly rather than letting the exception pass unremarked.

## Limitations

- **The shapes resemble spacecraft housekeeping; they are not calibrated
  against any flight dataset.** The orbital period, the eclipse fraction, the
  thermal time constant and the noise scales are plausible round numbers, not
  measurements.
- **Stationary noise.** Real telemetry has drift, dropouts, mode changes,
  dead sensors and quantised-at-source channels. Only the step transient
  gestures at any of that.
- **No multidimensional data.** Every channel is 1-D, which is also the limit
  of the codec.
- **The rate results depend on the noise floor**, which is the parameter a
  reader is most likely to disagree with. The rate-distortion sweeps span five
  decades of declared bound precisely so that the regime where the bound is
  above the noise floor and the regime where it is below are both visible.

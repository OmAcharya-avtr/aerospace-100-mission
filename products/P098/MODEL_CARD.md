# Model card — evidencestale staleness-risk model

Validation level: **Level 2.** Every figure in this card was produced by
`validation/validate_learned_model.py` in the session dated 2026-11-19 and is
recorded in `validation/validate_learned_model_output.txt`. The data is

**This model is not certified for operational flight use.**

This software is research-grade. It is not flight-qualified, not certified and
not approved for operational aerospace use.

## Why this model exists when an exact method is available — the honest reason

The product it belongs to contains a method that is exact: comparing the
SHA-256 digest of every artifact in an evidence item's dependency closure
against the digest recorded when the evidence was produced agrees with the
ground truth on every item, under three conditions stated in
`validation/VALIDATION.md`. A learned model cannot improve on an exact method.

So this model exists for one reason and is scored for one purpose: **it is
scored against the exact method, and its only possible value is where the
dependency declarations are incomplete**, because then the exact method is no
longer exact — it cannot see a change arriving through an undeclared edge.

That case is **measured, not assumed**. Section 3 of
`validation/validate_learned_model.py` withholds 30 per cent of the true
dependency edges and scores both methods against the same ground truth. The
measured answer is that the model does not beat the degraded exact method
either: on held-out artifacts the degraded closure comparison reaches an area
under the ROC curve of 0.8495 against the model's 0.5745, and on held-out time
0.8578 against 0.5211. The reason is structural rather than a shortfall of
tuning: an undeclared edge hides staleness from both methods, and the graph
features the model has left are the features of a graph that is itself missing
those edges.

The product publishes that result. The model was not retuned after any score
was seen.

## Problem

Given an evidence item in an assurance case — a GSN `Solution` node, the
artifact it is about, and the revision at which it was produced — estimate the
probability that the item is stale at the audit revision, that is that some
artifact in the true dependency closure of its subject has a different content
now than it had when the evidence was produced.

## Baselines, implemented and validated first

All three were implemented and validated against the by-construction ground
truth in `validation/validate_baselines.py` **before** this model was written.

1. **Baseline 1, modification revision of the subject against the evidence
   date.** The thing being refuted. Implemented at full strength. Measured on
   the committed corpus: missed staleness 115 of 194 truly stale items (rate
   0.592784), false alarms 143 of 286 truly current items (rate 0.500000),
   agreement 0.462500.
2. **Baseline 1b, the same comparison over the subject and its direct declared
   dependencies.** The strongest honest form of the same idea. Missed staleness
   26 of 194 (rate 0.134021), false alarms 226 of 286 (rate 0.790210). Reading
   one level of the graph trades one error direction for the other and removes
   neither.
3. **Baseline 2, SHA-256 comparison over the dependency closure.** Missed
   staleness 0, false alarms 0, agreement 1.000000 on the committed corpus and
   on the deliberately cyclic corpus.

Baseline 3, the replayed change history, is the ground truth everything is
scored against.

## Architecture

`sklearn.ensemble.RandomForestClassifier`, `n_estimators=200`, `max_depth=6`,
`random_state=980098`, `n_jobs=1`.

A forest rather than anything larger, for three reasons that are hard
constraints and not preferences:

- the corpus is 480 evidence items over 240 subject artifacts, so a model with
  many parameters would memorise subjects;
- the build environment has two cores shared by five concurrent builds and a
  60 s budget for any one fit;
- a forest gives a spread across trees, which is a conservative but honest
  uncertainty output without training a second model.

`max_depth=6` caps a tree at 64 leaves against at most 480 rows, which is the
loosest cap that still forces leaves to average several items.

**No model is serialised.** Nothing in this product writes a joblib or pickle
artifact, so there is no stored-format compatibility surface. The
`scikit-learn>=1.9,<1.10` ceiling in `pyproject.toml` is declared anyway,
because the figures in this card are functions of the estimator's defaults,
its bootstrap sampling order and its tie-breaking, none of which is part of its
public API contract.

## Features, and what is deliberately excluded

Twelve features, none of which reads any artifact content, any digest, or any
event after the evidence was produced. Averaged impurity importances over the
five grouped folds, from `validate_learned_model.py` section 2:

| feature | units | importance |
|---|---|---|
| `age` = audit revision − production revision | revisions | 0.303296 |
| `closure_size` | artifacts | 0.172075 |
| `closure_leaf_count` | artifacts | 0.121114 |
| `direct_dependents` | artifacts | 0.085619 |
| `direct_dependencies` | artifacts | 0.072273 |
| `closure_depth` | levels | 0.070160 |
| `closure_write_rate_before` | writes per revision | 0.057155 |
| `subject_events_before` | events | 0.034753 |
| `subject_touch_share_before` | dimensionless | 0.032322 |
| `closure_max_writes_before` | writes | 0.022769 |
| `closure_writes_before` | writes | 0.022242 |
| `subject_writes_before` | writes | 0.006221 |

**The excluded feature, and the ablation that measures the exclusion.** An
indicator "some artifact in the declared closure has an event after the
production revision" is available at audit time and is deliberately not a
feature: it reproduces the information baseline 2 uses, so a model given it is
a reimplementation of the exact method rather than a risk model. The claim that
it is a leak is measured rather than asserted — `learned_leak_ablation` adds
exactly that column and is reported beside the model. Measured: it raises the
area under the ROC curve from 0.7152 to 0.7390 on held-out artifacts and from
0.6977 to 0.7368 on held-out time, and still does not reach baseline 2's
1.0000, because the indicator fires on a touch as well as on a write and the
digest comparison does not.

## Dataset

`data/corpus_layered.json`, 240 artifacts in three layers, 249 true dependency
edges, 174 declared edges (75 withheld), 250 change events, 480 evidence items,
194 of them truly stale. Generated by `data/generate_corpus.py` with seed
980098. Synthetic. See `DATASET_CARD.md` for construction and limitations.

## Training procedure

One fit per split. No hyperparameter search, no early stopping, no feature
selection and no retuning after seeing a score.

All eight fits of one benchmark run took 1.181 s of wall clock in total in the
final run of `validate_learned_model.py` this session, and 1.783 s in an earlier
run of the same script, against a declared 60 s budget. Two live wall-clock readings are
never compared with each other anywhere in this repository; only the bound
against the budget is checked, and the measured band is published here.

## Test-split strategy

Two splits, scored separately and never averaged into one figure.

- **Held-out artifacts.** `sklearn.model_selection.GroupKFold(n_splits=5)` with
  the subject artifact as the group, so both evidence items about an artifact
  are on the same side. Without it, two items that go stale together land on
  both sides and the score is optimistic for a reason unrelated to
  generalisation.
- **Held-out time.** Forward-chaining splits on the production revision at the
  50th, 65th and 80th percentiles; train at or before the cutoff, test after.

### The finding

**The closure comparison wins, on both splits, by a wide margin, and that is
published as the result.** The learned model beats the timestamp baseline — the
method the specification names as the thing being refuted — on both splits, and
loses to the exact method on both. The pre-registered expectation was that the
closure comparison is exact and the learned model cannot beat it, and that is
what was measured.

## Uncertainty and confidence output

`StalenessRiskModel.predict_with_confidence` returns three numbers per item,
not one:

- `probability`, the forest's mean vote for "stale", in [0, 1];
- `spread`, the standard deviation of the per-tree votes, in [0, 0.5], which is
  the forest's own disagreement;
- `confidence` = max(p, 1 − p), the margin of the thresholded decision, in
  [0.5, 1]. The decision threshold is 0.5 and is reported wherever a thresholded
  figure appears.

Measured agreement by confidence band, one grouped-fold pass so every item is
predicted by a model that never saw its subject artifact:

| confidence band | items | agreement |
|---|---|---|
| [0.5, 0.6) | 114 | 0.631579 |
| [0.6, 0.7) | 81 | 0.666667 |
| [0.7, 0.8) | 104 | 0.721154 |
| [0.8, 0.9) | 164 | 0.762195 |
| [0.9, 1.0] | 17 | 0.647059 |

Agreement rises monotonically across the four bands with at least 20 items and
**falls in the sparse top band**, which is reported rather than smoothed. The
forest's vote margin is not a calibrated probability: trees that all agree on a
wrong answer report a high confidence. Nothing in this product calls `spread`
or `confidence` a credible interval.

## Failure cases

1. **The top confidence band is the least accurate on this corpus.** 17 items
   at confidence ≥ 0.9 with agreement 0.647059, below the 0.762195 of the band
   beneath it. A user who filtered on high confidence would be worse off than
   one who did not.
2. **The model cannot see what the declared graph does not contain.** Its
   closure features are features of the declared graph, so an incomplete
   declaration degrades the model as well as the exact method, which is the
   measured result above.
3. **Missed staleness is the model's dominant error on the time split**: rate
   0.6733 against a false-alarm rate of 0.1246. The model is conservative in
   the direction an audit cares about least.
4. **`age` is the dominant feature at importance 0.303296.** On this corpus
   older evidence is more likely to be stale by construction, so the model
   substantially learns "old evidence is suspect". That generalises only to a
   change process with a comparable rate.
5. **A single-class training fold** is accepted and recorded rather than
   dropped; the model then predicts that class with probability 1 and spread 0.
   No fold on this corpus was degenerate (0 of 8).

## Ethical and safety limits

This model estimates a risk over a synthetic corpus. It must not be used to
decide whether an assurance argument is adequate, whether evidence need not be
re-examined, or whether a system is safe. Where the dependency declarations are
complete, the exact method in the same package is strictly better and should be
used instead. Where they are incomplete, the measured answer is that neither
method is adequate and the declarations should be fixed.

**This model is not certified for operational flight use.**

```bash
python validation/validate_baselines.py

```

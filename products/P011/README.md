# WaveForge

**Status:** TESTING · **Class:** flagship · **Validation level:** 3 (Engineering) · **AI:** yes

## Executive overview

`waveforge` sizes and simulates an adaptive-optics system end to end, and then
asks whether a learned predictive controller actually beats the classical one.

It contains an independently implemented Kolmogorov phase-screen atmosphere
with Taylor frozen flow, Zernike and zonal wavefront representations, a
Shack-Hartmann sensor model with photon and read noise and subaperture dropout,
a deformable mirror with Gaussian influence functions and a finite actuator
stroke, a leaky-integrator control law with configurable gain and latency, and a
residual error budget split into fitting, temporal and noise terms with a cited
expression and a stated validity range for each. On top of that classical core
sits a learned predictive controller: a bagged ridge ensemble that forecasts the
next pseudo-open-loop slope vector from a short history of measurements and
reports a per-slope uncertainty alongside its point estimate.

The classical path was built and validated first. The analytic rejection
transfer function is reproduced by the time-domain loop to **1.7e-14**, the
stability limits match their closed forms to **3.5e-10**, Noll's residual
variance table is reproduced to **0.53 %**, and the deformable-mirror fitting
coefficient converges to **0.273** against the published 0.28. Only then was the
learned controller benchmarked against it, on phase screens it had never seen,
with the classical integrator's gain tuned in its favour.

The learned controller wins in the regime it was trained for — 1.25× lower
residual variance at one frame of latency, 3.45× at four — and **loses** at
twice the training wind speed and loses badly if it is deployed on a noisier
sensor than it was trained on. All three results are reported here, in
`MODEL_CARD.md` and in `validation/VALIDATION.md`.

## Aerospace problem

A free-space optical link through the atmosphere loses Strehl ratio, and with it
received power, to wavefront distortion. Before committing to hardware a
designer has to choose an actuator count, a subaperture count and a loop
bandwidth against a turbulence profile — and those choices trade against each
other through terms that scale differently: fitting error falls with actuator
pitch, temporal error falls with loop speed, and noise error *rises* with loop
speed because there are fewer photons per frame. Getting the trade wrong is
expensive in both directions: too few actuators and the link never closes, too
many and the terminal costs more than the mission can carry.

The second problem is latency. A classical integrator corrects the wavefront it
measured one to four frames ago, and under frozen flow that stale correction is
demonstrably worse than one based on a forecast. Whether a learned forecaster is
worth its complexity, and where it stops working, is an empirical question that
needs a controlled bench to answer. This package is that bench.

## Intended users

Optical and GNC engineers sizing an adaptive-optics subsystem for a free-space
optical terminal or an airborne imaging turret; researchers comparing control
laws on a common, auditable simulation; and students who need an AO simulator
whose every equation carries a source and whose every validation number can be
regenerated by running one script.

It is a simulation and sizing tool, not a real-time controller and not a
qualification tool. It does not model spot images (see the companion product
P018 ShackSim), multiple turbulence layers, scintillation, or mirror hysteresis.

## Engineering theory

Every expression below carries its source, units, assumptions and validity
range. The same statements appear in the docstring of the function that
implements them.

### Kolmogorov phase spectrum

```
Phi_phi(kappa) = 0.49 r0^(-5/3) kappa^(-11/3)          [rad^2 m^2], kappa in rad/m
Phi_phi(f)     = 0.49 (2 pi)^(-5/3) r0^(-5/3) f^(-11/3)
               = 0.022903 r0^(-5/3) f^(-11/3)          [rad^2 m^2], f in cycles/m
```

*Source:* Roddier 1981, *Progress in Optics* **19**, 281–376, Eq. 3.14; the
cyclic form is Hardy 1998, *Adaptive Optics for Astronomical Telescopes*,
Eq. 3.55. *Assumptions:* isotropic, homogeneous, Kolmogorov inertial subrange,
near field, infinite outer scale. *Validity:* `1/L0 << f << 1/l0`. The `f → 0`
divergence is why piston is removed everywhere and why tip/tilt is the term a
finite screen gets worst.

The von Kármán form `Phi(f) = 0.0229 r0^(-5/3) (f^2 + 1/L0^2)^(-11/6)` (Andrews
& Phillips 2005, Sec. 3.3) is available through `outer_scale_m`.

### Structure function and the Fried parameter

```
D_phi(r) = 2 (24/5 Gamma(6/5))^(5/6) (r/r0)^(5/3) = 6.883877 (r/r0)^(5/3)   [rad^2]
r0       = [0.423 k^2 int Cn^2(z) dz]^(-3/5)                                 [m]
```

*Source:* Fried 1965, *JOSA* **55**, 1427; Roddier 1981 Eqs. 3.19–3.20. The
package uses the constant at full precision, not the rounded 6.88 of most texts;
the two differ by 0.056 %. *Validity:* `l0 << r << L0`, plane wave, near field.

### Zernike modes and Kolmogorov modal statistics

Noll (1976) single index `j` starting at 1, orthonormal on the unit disc with
the area weight `1/π`; within an order `|m|` increases and, for `m ≠ 0`, even
`j` carries `cos(mθ)` and odd `j` carries `sin(|m|θ)`. From Noll's Eqs. 8 and
18 the package derives, rather than tabulates,

```
<a_j^2> = C (n+1) Gamma(n - 5/6) / Gamma(n + 23/6) (D/r0)^(5/3)      [rad^2]
Delta_J = <total piston-removed variance> - sum_{j=2}^{J} <a_j^2>
```

with `C` assembled from the PSD constant and the Weber–Schafheitlin integral in
`statistics.py`. *Source:* R. J. Noll, *JOSA* **66**(3), 207–211 (1976).
*Validity:* circular unobscured pupil, infinite outer scale; the circle
polynomials are **not** orthogonal on an annulus (that needs Mahajan 1981, not
implemented).

### Shack-Hartmann measurement and noise

A subaperture reports the average phase gradient over its area; a lenslet of
focal length `f` maps that to a spot displacement `Δx = f ⟨dW/dx⟩` (Hardy 1998
Sec. 5.2). Centre-of-gravity measurement noise, in units of `(λ/d_sub)²` of
angle of arrival:

```
sigma^2_photon = (pi^2 / 2) (1 / N_ph) (X_T / X_D)^2
sigma^2_read   = (pi^2 / 3) (sigma_e^2 / N_ph^2) (X_S^2 / X_D)^2
```

*Source:* G. Rousset, "Wave-front sensors", in *Adaptive Optics in Astronomy*,
ed. F. Roddier, CUP 1999, Ch. 5, Eqs. 5.16–5.17; also Nicolle et al. 2004,
*A&A* **420**, 989. `N_ph` is detected photoelectrons per subaperture per frame,
`X_T` and `X_D` the actual and diffraction-limited spot FWHM in pixels, `X_S`
the window side in pixels, `σ_e` the read noise per pixel. *Assumptions:*
unbiased centre of gravity, background subtracted, Poisson photons, Gaussian
read noise, no spot truncation. *Validity:* `N_ph` above a few electrons — the
expressions diverge as `N_ph → 0`, where the centroid is undefined rather than
merely noisy. One `λ/d_sub` of angle is `2π/d_sub` rad/m of phase gradient.

### Deformable mirror

```
phi_DM(r) = sum_k c_k I_k(r),   I(r) = exp( ln(c_pitch) (r / d_act)^2 )
```

so `I(0) = 1` and `I(d_act) = c_pitch` exactly; 10–25 % coupling is typical for
a continuous face-sheet piezo mirror. *Source:* Tyson, *Principles of Adaptive
Optics*, 3rd ed., Ch. 5; Hardy 1998 Sec. 6.2. Commands are in **radians of
phase**; the mechanical sag is `φ λ / 4π` because the beam traverses it twice.
*Assumption:* exact linear superposition — no hysteresis, no creep.

### Closed-loop control

With total latency `d ≥ 1` frames and leak `1`:

```
c_k = leak c_{k-1} + g R s_{k-(d-1)}           (the mirror holds c_k for frame k+1)
E(z)/Phi(z) = (1 - leak z^-1) / [ (1 - leak z^-1) + g z^-d ]        rejection
E(z)/N(z)   = - g z^-d / [ (1 - leak z^-1) + g z^-d ]               noise
z^d - leak z^(d-1) + g = 0                                          poles
```

*Source:* P.-Y. Madec, "Control techniques", in *Adaptive Optics in Astronomy*,
ed. Roddier, CUP 1999, Ch. 3; Hardy 1998 Sec. 7.3. The characteristic polynomial
gives the classical stability limits `g < 2` at `d = 1`, `g < 1` at `d = 2` and
`g < 2 sin(π/10) = 0.618034` at `d = 3`. *Validity:* linear time-invariant
single-input loop; the WFS integration `sinc` roll-off is **not** included and
matters above about `0.3 f_s`.

### Error budget and Strehl

```
sigma^2_total = sigma^2_fit + sigma^2_temporal + sigma^2_noise + ...   [rad^2]
sigma^2_fit      = a_F (d_act / r0)^(5/3),  a_F ~ 0.28                 Hudgin 1977
sigma^2_delay    = (tau / tau0)^(5/3),      tau0 = 0.314 r0 / v        Fried 1990
sigma^2_bw       = (f_G / f_3dB)^(5/3),     f_G  = 0.427 v / r0        Greenwood 1977
sigma^2_noise    = eta(g,d) sigma_s^2 mean(diag(P P^T))                exact for the linear chain
S ~= exp(-sigma^2_total)                                               extended Marechal
```

`P = M^T R` maps a slope vector to a pupil phase estimate and `η` is the
closed-loop noise amplification (`g/(2−g)` at one frame of latency), so the
noise term is **computed for the actual reconstructor** rather than taken from a
table. The pure-delay and servo-bandwidth temporal forms describe different
idealisations and must not be added together; the package keeps them separate.
*Source for the Maréchal forms:* Maréchal 1947, *Rev. Opt.* **26**, 257; Born &
Wolf Sec. 9.1; Hardy 1998 Eq. 4.20. *Measured validity:* see Validation below —
the extended form is good to 5 % up to `σ² ≈ 0.5 rad²` for scaled Kolmogorov
phase and **underestimates** the Strehl of real closed-loop residuals by up to
27 % near `σ² ≈ 2 rad²`.

### Predictive control

Pseudo-open-loop slopes reconstruct what the sensor would have seen with a flat
mirror, `s_pol,k = s_k + D_int c_{k-1}` with `D_int = G M^T`, exactly for this
linear sensor model. The newest POL frame available at frame `k` is
`s_pol,k-(d-1)`, and the command computed now shapes the mirror for frame
`k+1`, so a predictor must forecast exactly `d` frames ahead. *Source:*
Ellerbroek & Vogel 2009, *Inverse Problems* **25**, 063001; Gilles 2005,
*Appl. Opt.* **44**, 993. Setting the forecast to the newest available frame
gives the pure-delay baseline through identical machinery.

## Architecture

```
src/waveforge/
├── pupil.py        sampling grid, masks, piston removal, numerical Strehl
├── zernike.py      Noll indexing, radial polynomials, gradients, modal fitting
├── statistics.py   Kolmogorov PSD, structure function, Zernike variances, r0, f_G, tau0
├── atmosphere.py   Fourier phase screens + subharmonics, frozen flow, band-limit diagnostics
├── sensor.py       Shack-Hartmann slope operator, photon/read noise, dropout
├── dm.py           influence functions, stroke limits, least-squares fitting
├── control.py      leaky integrator, latency, rejection/noise transfer, stability limits
├── errorbudget.py  fitting/temporal/noise terms, Marechal Strehl, budget container
├── loop.py         AOConfig / AOSystem / LoopResult — the end-to-end simulation
├── predictor.py    pure-delay baseline and the bagged ridge forecaster with uncertainty
├── datasets.py     deterministic, seed-partitioned dataset generation
└── cli.py          argparse CLI (python -m waveforge)
```

Nothing is imported from any other product; the package is self-contained.
`AOSystem` assembles the pupil, atmosphere, sensor and mirror, builds the
interaction matrix `D_int = G Mᵀ`, forms a truncated pseudo-inverse
reconstructor, and runs the loop frame by frame. All latency lives in one
explicit measurement buffer plus the one-frame mirror application delay, so the
simulated loop and the analytic transfer function are provably the same
equation.

## Quick start

```python
from waveforge import AOConfig, AOSystem

system = AOSystem(AOConfig(diameter_m=0.5, r0_m=0.10, n_sub=8, n_act=9,
                           gain=0.4, delay_frames=2))

print(system.error_budget().as_dict())
# {'fitting_rad2': 0.1279, 'temporal_rad2': 0.4715, 'noise_rad2': 0.0,
#  'total_rad2': 0.5994, 'rms_rad': 0.7742, 'strehl_marechal': 0.5491}

result = system.run(500, warmup_frames=150)
print(result.mean_residual_variance, result.mean_strehl, result.rejection_db)
```

Command line:

```bash
python -m waveforge noll --j-max 21          # Noll table vs published values
python -m waveforge screen --n-pix 128       # screen statistics vs theory and band limit
python -m waveforge budget --r0 0.1          # analytic error budget
python -m waveforge loop --frames 400        # closed-loop run
python -m waveforge predict --frames 400     # learned vs classical vs pure delay
```

## Configuration

`AOConfig` is a frozen dataclass; every field has a unit and a validated range.

| Field | Meaning | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `n_pix`, `diameter_m` | pupil sampling and aperture [m] | 64, 0.5 |
| `r0_m`, `wavelength_m` | Fried parameter [m], sensing wavelength [m] | 0.10, 1.55e-6 |
| `wind_speed_m_s`, `frame_rate_hz` | frozen-flow wind [m/s], WFS rate [Hz] | 10.0, 1000.0 |
| `n_sub`, `n_act`, `coupling` | subapertures across, actuators across, DM coupling | 8, 9, 0.15 |
| `stroke_rad`, `margin_actuators` | actuator limit [rad of phase], rings outside the pupil | ∞, 1 |
| `gain`, `delay_frames`, `leak` | integrator gain, total latency [frames], leak | 0.4, 2, 1.0 |
| `photon_flux`, `read_noise_e` | detected e⁻ per subaperture per frame, read noise [e⁻] | ∞, 0.0 |
| `dropout_probability` | per-subaperture per-frame measurement loss | 0.0 |
| `screen_pixels`, `n_subharmonics`, `seed` | generating screen size, Lane subharmonic levels, seed | 1024, 6, 0 |
| `n_filtered_modes`, `condition_threshold` | reconstructor mode filtering | 1, 1e-3 |

`AOSystem.run()` additionally accepts `gain` and `delay_frames` overrides so a
gain scan re-uses one assembled system instead of re-drawing the atmosphere.

## Validation

Full evidence, including every raw transcript, is in
[`validation/VALIDATION.md`](validation/VALIDATION.md). Requirements and the
verification matrix are in [`docs/REQUIREMENTS.md`](docs/REQUIREMENTS.md).
Headline numbers, all produced in this build session:

| Check | Reference | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Zernike orthonormality, exact quadrature | Noll 1976 Eq. 3 | worst deviation **6.2e-15** (tolerance 1e-12) |
| Noll residual variances `Δ_J`, `J = 1…21` | Noll 1976 Table IV | worst **0.53 %** |
| Total piston-removed variance, two independent routes | Noll 1976; Fried 1965 | routes agree to **0.033 %**, both **+0.25 %** above Noll's published `Δ₁` |
| Modal residual on screens vs `Δ_J (D/r₀)^(5/3)` | Noll 1976 | ratio **0.975–1.009**, flat in `D/r₀` |
| DM fitting coefficient at 33 × 33 actuators | Hudgin 1977; Hardy 1998 Table 6.1 | **0.273** vs published 0.28 (−2.4 %) |
| Scalar rejection transfer function | Madec 1999 Eq. 3.8 ff. | worst **1.7e-14** |
| Stability limits `d = 1, 2, 3` | `z^d − z^(d−1) + g = 0` | worst **3.5e-10** |
| Noise amplification vs `g/(2−g)` | Madec 1999 Eq. 3.20 | worst **1.6e-16** |
| Full AO loop vs analytic `\|E(z)\|` | as above | worst **2.6 %** against a measured 2.24 % modelling floor |
| Screens vs exact discrete spectrum | — | worst **0.50 %** |
| Extended Maréchal validity | Maréchal 1947; Hardy 1998 | good to 5 % up to **σ² = 0.5 rad²** |

**Two checks record a documented deviation rather than a pass**, and are
reported as such rather than being tuned away:

1. Differencing Noll's rounded table entries to recover per-mode variances
   leaves 7 of 20 modes outside what the published third significant figure can
   resolve — all in the same direction, and all consistent with the +0.25 %
   normalisation offset that two independent derivations in this package agree
   on.
2. On genuine closed-loop residuals the extended Maréchal form **underestimates**
   the Strehl by up to **26.7 %**. Use `LoopResult.strehl` for performance
   claims and the Maréchal form only for sizing.

Default configuration (0.5 m, `r₀ = 0.10 m`, `D/r₀ = 5`, 10 m/s, 1 kHz, 8 × 8
Shack-Hartmann, 9 × 9 mirror), residual variance in rad² and mean numerical
Strehl over 500 frames on held-out screens, each controller at its own tuned
gain:

| Latency [frames] | Integrator | Pure-delay POL | Learned | `S` int | `S` delay | `S` ML |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.6728 | 0.6728 | **0.5361** | 0.5434 | 0.5434 | **0.6085** |
| 2 | 0.9274 | 0.8637 | **0.5354** | 0.4308 | 0.4519 | **0.6093** |
| 3 | 1.2798 | 1.0669 | **0.5444** | 0.3040 | 0.3848 | **0.6042** |
| 4 | 1.7085 | 1.3736 | **0.4958** | 0.2124 | 0.2818 | **0.6384** |

Performance: assembling the default system takes 0.65 s (dominated by the
1024² screen), a closed-loop frame costs 1.40 ms, fitting the eight-member
ridge ensemble on 1200 samples takes 0.45 s, and the full predictor benchmark —
1200 training frames, four horizons, 96 gain-tuning runs and 40 evaluation runs
— completes in **98 s** on two cores with `n_jobs = 1`.

## AI model details

Full detail in [`MODEL_CARD.md`](MODEL_CARD.md) and
[`DATASET_CARD.md`](DATASET_CARD.md).

* **Baseline first.** The classical integrator and the analytic error budget
  were implemented and validated before any ML was written, and the integrator's
  gain is tuned on a separate tuning screen at every latency and noise level so
  that it is presented at its best configuration.
* **Model.** Eight bagged `sklearn.linear_model.Ridge` regressions, 416 inputs
  (four frames × 104 slopes) to 104 outputs, ≈ 346 k coefficients, `alpha`
  selected on a held-back slice of the training set only.
* **Dataset.** Entirely synthetic, regenerated from integer seeds, never
  committed. Training seeds 101/102/103; held-out seeds 901/902 are *different
  atmospheric realisations*, not different slices of one sequence — a
  frozen-flow series is strongly autocorrelated and a contiguous split would
  leak.
* **Uncertainty output.** Per-slope `σ = sqrt(ensemble variance + out-of-bag
  residual variance)`, not just a point estimate. Measured coverage inside one
  sigma is 82.7 % at horizon 1 and 62.3 % at horizon 4 against a nominal 68.3 %:
  **conservative at short horizons and optimistic at long ones**. Treat it as a
  relative confidence signal, never as a probability.
* **Result, stated plainly.** The learned controller wins at every tested
  latency, by 1.25× in residual variance at one frame and 3.45× at four.
* **Failure cases, stated equally plainly.** At 20 m/s wind — twice the training
  value — it is **17 % worse** than the plain integrator. Trained on noise-free
  slopes and deployed at 100 e⁻ per subaperture it is **5.9× worse**; retraining
  at the deployed noise level fixes it and is mandatory.
* **Reproducibility.** `PYTHONPATH=src python validation/validate_predictor.py`
  regenerates every number above in about 100 s.

**This model is not certified for operational flight use.**

## Hardware requirements

CPU only; no GPU and no PyTorch. The package was developed and validated on two
cores. Peak memory is dominated by the 1024² generating screen (8 MB) and the
influence-function matrix (2.6 MB for the default geometry); a process that
assembles the default system, runs 500 frames and fits the predictor peaked at
306 MB resident. No script in the package takes longer than two minutes; the
slowest is `validation/validate_predictor.py` at 98 s.

## Limitations

1. **Single-layer frozen flow only.** No multi-layer profile, no per-layer wind,
   no boiling. This is the most predictable atmosphere possible, so the learned
   controller's measured advantage is an upper bound.
2. **Screens are band-limited.** No power below `1/(N d)` or above the grid
   Nyquist. With six subharmonic levels the structure function reaches 1.00 of
   theory at separations ≥ 0.1 m, but pupil tip and tilt variances still sit at
   0.82 of the analytic values.
3. **The subharmonic component is not periodic**, so a frozen-flow run longer
   than `max_frames` is refused rather than allowed to wrap.
4. **Geometric sensor.** Slopes are exact subaperture-averaged gradients plus
   additive Gaussian noise; there is no spot image, so spot truncation,
   elongation and centroid bias are absent. That is product P018's subject.
5. **Linear mirror.** No hysteresis, creep or influence-function error;
   saturation is the only non-linearity modelled.
6. **No aliasing term** in the error budget (`ErrorBudget.other` is provided for
   it), **no scintillation**, and **no non-common-path error**.
7. **The control model omits the WFS integration `sinc` roll-off**, which
   matters above roughly `0.3 f_s`.
8. **Discrete orthonormality.** On a 64-pixel pupil the Zernike basis is
   orthonormal only to about 2 %; modal results are quoted as ratios for that
   reason.
9. **The predictor is tied to its geometry.** Feeding slopes from a different
   subaperture layout is a silent error — only the vector length is checked.
10. **Dropout flags are not used by the predictor**, which treats a dropped
    subaperture's zero as a real measurement.
11. **No comparison against measured atmospheric data** has been made anywhere
    in this package.

## Roadmap

* Multi-layer atmospheres with per-layer wind, and a boiling term, so that the
  predictive controller can be tested against turbulence that is not maximally
  predictable.
* A von Kármán outer scale in the benchmark configuration, to remove the tip and
  tilt deficit of the current screens.
* An aliasing term in the error budget, and a spatially filtered wavefront
  sensor to suppress it.
* Optimal modal gain control as a stronger classical baseline than a single
  scalar gain.
* Recursive least squares so the predictor can adapt online to a changing wind,
  which is the failure mode measured in §6d of the validation.

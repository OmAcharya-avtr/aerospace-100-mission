Two effects, both reported rather than corrected for:
  (a) the ratio is below 1 because a Fourier screen carries no power
      below 1/(N d); even with six subharmonic levels a deficit
      remains, and it sits almost entirely in tip and tilt;
  (b) the scatter between r0 blocks is large because the total
      variance is dominated by two modes (tip and tilt), so with 40
      screens the estimator still has ~20% standard error. The
      higher-order residuals of check 2 are far better determined.

--- 3. Zonal (deformable-mirror) fitting error vs actuator pitch ---

Honest reading. The exponent measured across pitch is about 1.54,
not 5/3, and the coefficient rises monotonically with actuator count.
Both are the same finite-aperture effect: the (d_act/r0)^(5/3) law is
an infinite-aperture, infinite-bandwidth result, while a mirror with
only 5 x 5 Gaussian influence functions fitted by unconstrained least
squares over a 0.5 m pupil removes far more than a hard spatial
low-pass would. As the actuator count grows the two converge: after
correcting for the power the screens do not carry above the grid
Nyquist, a_F reaches 0.273 at 33 x 33 actuators,
against the published 0.28. The scaling law should therefore be used
for sizing only when many actuators span the aperture, which is
exactly the regime it was derived for.

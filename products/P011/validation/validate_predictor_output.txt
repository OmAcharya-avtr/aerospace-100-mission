--- 2. Uncertainty calibration on held-out screens ---
The predictor reports sigma = sqrt(ensemble variance + out-of-bag
residual variance). Nominal coverage is the Gaussian value; measured
coverage is the fraction of held-out slopes inside k sigma.
  h   1 sigma   (68.3%)   2 sigma   (95.4%)   3 sigma   (99.7%)  mean sigma     RMSE
  1     82.7%     68.3%     99.2%     95.4%    100.0%     99.7%      1.7244   1.4384
  2     76.2%     68.3%     97.7%     95.4%     99.9%     99.7%      2.3823   2.8387
  3     67.4%     68.3%     94.6%     95.4%     99.5%     99.7%      2.1099   4.2849
  4     62.3%     68.3%     91.9%     95.4%     99.0%     99.7%      2.6805   5.8890

Reading: the intervals are NOT calibrated Gaussian sigmas, and they
miscalibrate in both directions. At h = 1 and h = 2 they are
conservative (measured coverage above nominal, the safe direction);
at h = 3 and especially h = 4 they become optimistic - 62% inside one
sigma at h = 4 against a nominal 68%, with the mean sigma well below
the actual RMSE. The out-of-bag residual term is estimated on the
training screens and does not grow enough to cover the extra error
the model makes further ahead on unseen turbulence. Treat the output
as a relative confidence signal only, and never as a probability.

--- 5. Failure mode: measurement noise, trained clean vs noise-matched ---
A predictor trained on noise-free slopes and then deployed on a noisy
sensor amplifies the noise. Retraining at the deployed noise level
fixes it, and is the only supported way to use the model under noise.

Engineering rule of thumb, confirmed: the extended form is the one to
use, and the quadratic form is only good for sigma^2 well below 0.5.

--- 2. Significance of the gap, and where the form breaks entirely ---
The extended form is exact only for a zero-mean Gaussian phase of
uniform variance over the pupil. Neither condition holds here: each
screen is rescaled to a fixed SPATIAL variance rather than drawn from
the free ensemble, and Kolmogorov phase on a finite pupil is not
spatially stationary. The measured gap is small but far larger than
the Monte-Carlo error, so it is reported rather than dismissed.
  sigma^2 = 0.10  S = 0.90439 +/- 0.00003 (s.e.), exp(-s2) = 0.90484, gap = -16.5 standard errors
  sigma^2 = 0.50  S = 0.59890 +/- 0.00051 (s.e.), exp(-s2) = 0.60653, gap = -14.9 standard errors
  sigma^2 = 1.00  S = 0.34979 +/- 0.00142 (s.e.), exp(-s2) = 0.36788, gap = -12.8 standard errors
  sigma^2 = 2.00  S = 0.11474 +/- 0.00270 (s.e.), exp(-s2) = 0.13534, gap = -7.6 standard errors

Beyond sigma^2 ~ 2.5 the numerical Strehl stops falling and saturates
near the speckle floor set by the number of independent coherence
cells in the pupil: at sigma^2 = 5 the measured value is 0.040 against
exp(-5) = 0.0067, a factor of six. The extended Marechal form must not
be used there, in either direction.

which is what a user of this package will actually have.

worst relative difference on closed-loop residuals : 26.7%
Honest reading: on real closed-loop residuals the extended Marechal
form UNDERESTIMATES the Strehl, by tens of percent once sigma^2
approaches 1 rad^2. Two reasons, both physical: the residual variance
fluctuates from frame to frame and the mean of exp(-sigma^2) over
those fluctuations exceeds exp(-mean sigma^2) by Jensen's inequality;
and the residual is dominated by high-order modes at the pupil edge,
so its variance is not uniform over the pupil. Use the numerical
Strehl from LoopResult.strehl for performance claims, and the
Marechal form only for sizing.

# Model card — acmpilot learned channel predictor 0.1.0

**This model is not certified for operational flight use.** It is a research
artefact trained on a simulated channel, and its published result is that it does
not beat a three-parameter analytic predictor.

## Problem

Choose a modulation-and-coding mode (MODCOD) for slot `n` of an optical link,
knowing only the receiver's SNR report for slot `n - d`, where `d` is the
round-trip feedback delay in slots. The decision is asymmetric: choosing a mode
the channel cannot carry loses the whole slot, while choosing below capacity
loses only the difference. So the quantity the policy needs is not a point
estimate of the future SNR but a **lower** summary of its distribution — which
means the model must be able to say how unsure it is, and that statement must be
calibrated.

## Baseline, implemented first

Three non-learned baselines and one additional non-learned predictor, all
implemented and reported before the learned model:

1. **fixed margin** — select the mode supported by the delayed report minus a
   fixed margin (`acmpilot.policy.FixedMargin`).
2. **threshold with hysteresis** — switching thresholds with a dead band
   (`acmpilot.policy.ThresholdHysteresis`).
3. **clairvoyant upper bound** — selects using the true future state. Acausal,
   unachievable, included to separate prediction error from ladder granularity
   (`acmpilot.policy.ClairvoyantUpperBound`).
4. **analytic AR(1) MMSE predictor** — additional, and **not learned**
   (`acmpilot.predictor.GaussMarkovPredictor`). Three parameters — the dB series
   mean, variance and lag-1 correlation — estimated from the training path. For a
   stationary Gaussian AR(1) process the conditional mean at horizon `d` is
   `mu + rho**d (x - mu)` and the conditional variance is `s2 (1 - rho**(2d))`,
   so this predictor is exactly minimum-mean-square-error on the lognormal
   channel by construction.

Every baseline's free parameter is tuned on held-out tuning seeds before the
comparison. The shipped 3 dB default margin is measurably not the optimum
(`validation/validate_policies.txt` V3.6), and comparing a tuned model against an
untuned baseline would be worthless.

## Architecture

Three independent `sklearn.ensemble.GradientBoostingRegressor` models with the
quantile loss at levels 0.10, 0.50 and 0.90. PyTorch is unavailable in the build
environment; boosted trees are a reasonable choice at these sample counts.

* Features, per target slot `n`: the 4 most recent available reports
  `x[n-d] … x[n-d-3]`, plus their 3 first differences. 7 columns.
  `make_lag_features` supports any number of lags; 4 is the shipped default
  because 8 lags and 100 trees were measured to give 2.068 dB held-out median
  absolute error against 2.063 dB for 4 lags and 60 trees, for 3.6 times the fit
  time. The driver is first-order Markov, so there is nothing in the older
  reports to find.
* Hyperparameters: `n_estimators=60`, `max_depth=3`, `learning_rate=0.1`,
  `subsample=0.8`, `random_state=20261006`.
* Output: `(q50, q90 - q10)` in dB. The three models are fitted independently so
  their outputs can cross; they are sorted per row, and the fraction of rows where
  the raw outputs were out of order is reported (`crossing_fraction`, measured
  0.0000 at every delay).
* Selection rule: `snr_for_selection = q50 - gate_k * (q90 - q10)`, with `gate_k`
  tuned on the tuning seeds. `gate_k = 0` is kept in the grid as the ablation
  that shows what the confidence output is worth.

## Dataset

Fully synthetic and generated by committed code; see `DATASET_CARD.md`.
Training uses seeds (101, 102, 103), 5 200 slots each, giving about 15 580
feature rows per delay.

## Training and test split

Three disjoint seed sets, enforced at construction
(`acmpilot.benchmark.BenchmarkSplit` raises on overlap):

| set | seeds | used for |
|---|---|---|
| train | 101, 102, 103 | fitting both predictors |
| tune | 201 … 205 | selecting the margin, the dead band and the gate |
| test | 301 … 310 | **every published number** |

A separate seed, 401, generates the held-out path used for the calibration
report, so calibration is not measured on anything the model was fitted to.

## Metrics

Primary: **goodput** in bit/symbol on the test seeds, with the standard error of
the mean over 10 seeds, so a reader can see whether two policies are separated at
all. Secondary: outage probability, the two mis-selection kinds with their
separate costs, and switch rate. Uncertainty: interval coverage against nominal,
tail exceedance rates, pinball loss, mean absolute and root-mean-square error of
the median.

## Results — the learned model does not win

Lognormal channel, `tau_c` = 10 ms, slot 1 ms, `sigma_I^2` = 0.5. Full tables in
`validation/validate_predictor.txt`.

| tau, ms | learned GBR | analytic AR(1) (not learned) | gap/sem | separated? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.9737 | 1.9759 | -0.21 | no |
| 2 | 1.8598 | 1.8647 | -0.55 | no |
| 5 | 1.7260 | 1.7290 | -0.50 | no |
| 10 | 1.6618 | 1.6636 | -0.48 | no |
| 20 | 1.6478 | 1.6485 | -0.27 | no |

**The learned predictor is nominally behind the analytic one at all five delays,
and never separated from it.** On the gamma-gamma channel, where the conditional
mean is not linear in the last report, it is nominally ahead at 5 and 10 ms and
behind at 2 and 20 ms, still never separated
(`validation/validate_predictor_gammagamma.txt`).

Both predictive policies do beat the tuned non-learned baselines once the delay is
large enough, by +1.82% at `tau` = 2 ms rising to +11.48% at 20 ms. At `tau` = 1 ms
a tuned fixed margin is statistically indistinguishable from both predictors.

The one place the learned model is measurably better is the **calibration of its
stated uncertainty on the non-Gaussian channel**: on gamma-gamma its nominal 80%
interval covers 0.794 to 0.800 of targets, against 0.821 to 0.823 for the
analytic Gaussian interval, which over-covers because a symmetric Gaussian
interval is the wrong shape for that marginal. That is an advantage in the
uncertainty statement, not in throughput.

## Uncertainty and confidence output

The model's confidence output is the interquantile spread `q90 - q10` in dB. It is
state-dependent, unlike the analytic predictor's constant conditional standard
deviation, and the dependence is informative: binning the held-out rows by
predicted spread, both the mean and the 90th percentile of realised absolute error
rise with it (`examples/predictor_vs_baselines.py` panel 4).

The gate built on it is worth +0.15 to +0.28 bit/symbol against selecting on the
point estimate alone, while the choice between the learned and analytic predictors
is worth 0.002 to 0.005 bit/symbol. **The confidence output is between 30 and 140
times more valuable in this application than the regression it gates.** That is
the main engineering conclusion of this product.

## Failure cases

* **The model has nothing to learn on the shipped lognormal channel.** It is an
  AR(1) process in log-amplitude by construction, so a linear predictor is
  optimal. Anyone reporting an ML win on a channel like this without the analytic
  comparison has reported an artefact.
* **It is trained per delay.** A model fitted at one `d` has the wrong feature
  semantics at another and must be refitted; nothing stops a caller from mixing
  them, and `PredictivePolicy` records `delay_slots` but does not enforce a match
  against the runner's delay.
* **It is trained per channel configuration.** Nothing was tested about transfer
  between correlation times, scintillation indices or marginals.
* **It is trained on a noiseless feedback report.** A quantised or noisy report
  would change the feature distribution and the model was never shown one.
* **The intervals under-cover slightly on the lognormal channel** (0.780 to 0.796
  against nominal 0.80), so the gate is marginally more aggressive than its stated
  level implies. The gate strength is tuned empirically, which absorbs this.
* **It will not extrapolate.** Boosted trees are piecewise constant; outside the
  SNR range seen in training the prediction saturates at the training extreme,
  which on this channel is conservative but not guaranteed to be.
* **Deep fades are rare in the training set by construction.** At
  `sigma_I^2` = 0.5 the lowest MODCOD is unsupported in 0.028% of slots, so the
  model sees very few of the slots that matter most.

## Reproducibility

```bash
pip install -e ".[dev,examples]"
cd validation
python validate_modcod_thresholds.py    > validate_modcod_thresholds.txt
python validate_predictor.py            > validate_predictor.txt
python validate_predictor_gammagamma.py > validate_predictor_gammagamma.txt
```

or for one delay from the CLI:

```bash
python -m acmpilot predict --tau-ms 10 --tau-c-ms 10 --gate-k 0.5
```

Seeds: channel seeds as tabulated above; `random_state=20261006` for every
quantile model; threshold Monte Carlo seed 20261006. Fits are deterministic given
the data and that seed (`tests/test_predictor.py::TestQuantilePredictor::test_deterministic_given_the_same_data_and_seed`).

**No model binary is committed.** `QuantilePredictor.save`/`.load` use joblib, and
`validation/artifacts/` is git-ignored. Every validation script refits from the
seeds, which costs about 5 s per delay.

## Compute used

Measured in the build container: Python 3.13.16, NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1,
scikit-learn 1.9.1, 2 CPU cores and 7.8 GiB RAM shared with four other build
agents, no GPU. One fit of three quantile models over ~15 600 rows takes about
5 s. The whole five-delay lognormal benchmark, including tuning and scoring,
takes 65 s; the four-delay gamma-gamma benchmark takes 77 s. Both are inside the
3-minute per-script budget this build was sized for. There is no training run in
this package that takes more than a minute.

## Ethical and safety limits

This model selects a transmission rate on a simulated optical link. It makes no
decision about people. The safety limit that matters is a different one: its
outputs are properties of a simulator with a free correlation-time knob,
validated against mathematics rather than against measured turbulence, and they
must not be used to set a real link's rate adaptation, margin or availability
budget. Not flight-qualified, not certified, not approved for operational
aerospace use.

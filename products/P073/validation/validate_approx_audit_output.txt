Every assertion in this suite that declares a non-zero relative tolerance
passes on that relative tolerance alone, with no absolute bound whatever.
That is a stronger statement than pass 1 and it is the one the audit set
out to test.

The calls left alone are written rel=0.0 with an explicit absolute bound.
They are deliberately absolute: the quantities they compare are angles and
times whose expected value is zero or whose meaningful tolerance is a
physical quantity rather than a fraction. Forcing their absolute bound to
zero would make them assert bit-exact floating-point equality, which this
mission's build guide forbids after two products shipped that defect.

3. A worked demonstration of the trap
==============================================================================
Why this audit exists, in four lines that can be run by hand:

  value                        : 1.000000e-05
  perturbed value              : 1.000000e-05
  relative difference          : 1.000000e-08
  approx(rel=1e-15)            : PASSES
  approx(rel=1e-15, abs=0.0)   : fails

The relative difference is 1e-8, ten million times the stated relative
tolerance, and the assertion passes anyway because 1e-13 is inside the default
absolute bound of 1e-12.

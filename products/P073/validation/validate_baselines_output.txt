==============================================================================
What the exact method buys beyond objective: starvation and equality
==============================================================================
The objective gap is not the whole story. The same comparison, measured in
the quantities this product exists to report.

method                      mean objective  mean starved  mean Jain  mean max-min  mean inversions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
greedy[priority]                 10.755469        3.0438   0.779122      0.133646           4.9688
  instances with a schedule             160
greedy[demand]                   10.257031        3.1875   0.774858      0.126771           6.9062
  instances with a schedule             160
exact                            11.115625        2.8062   0.803596      0.171458           4.7812
  instances with a schedule             160
exact + fairness floors          10.437500        0.0000   1.000000      1.000000           0.0000
  instances with a schedule              16

The fairness-constrained program is feasible on only 16 of 160 instances.
That is the headline finding of this table and it is not a solver failure:
on the other instances at least one request declares more contacts than its
own passes can carry, so no schedule whatever can satisfy the floors. The
averages on this row are therefore over a biased subset -- the easy
instances -- and must not be compared with the rows above. What is
actionable is the diagnostic: solve_exact names the binding floor and the
standalone capacity that bounds it, which is what a planner renegotiates
against. The price of the constraint where it IS feasible is measured in
validate_fairness.py.

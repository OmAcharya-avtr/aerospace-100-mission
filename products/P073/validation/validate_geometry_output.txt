What the table actually shows, which is NOT what was expected:

The law-of-cosines numerator r_sat**2 - r_station**2 - range**2 is a
difference of nearly equal large numbers near the horizon, so the expectation
was that the disagreement would grow as the elevation fell. It does the
opposite. The cancellation leaves an absolute error in sin(elevation) of order
eps * r_station / (2 * range), which is 3.9e-16 at these ranges and is almost
flat in elevation; the conversion through arcsin then multiplies it by
1 / cos(elevation), which is 1 at the horizon and 57 at 89 degrees. The
amplification near the zenith dominates the cancellation near the horizon, and
the worst disagreement in this sample is in the highest band, not the lowest.

This is recorded rather than corrected because the prose written before the
measurement said the reverse, and the measurement is what is published.

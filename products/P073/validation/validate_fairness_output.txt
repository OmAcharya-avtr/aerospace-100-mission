3. Behaviour under priority inversion
==============================================================================
The priority labels on each instance are reversed: the request that was most
urgent becomes the least and vice versa, with the multiset of priority values
and the whole contention structure left untouched. Only the declared promise
changes.

What a working metric must do: the set of requests that win should change,
and the per-priority delivered satisfaction should follow the new labels. The
per-pair inversion count need NOT fall -- it measures whether the delivered
order matches the declared order, and reversing the declaration can leave an
instance equally mismatched -- so the measurement below reports what happens
rather than asserting a direction.

4. A constructed priority inversion, in full
==============================================================================
An inversion that the declared weights cannot prevent, because it is not
about weights. R-HIGH is priority 1 and declares it needs three contacts;
its spacecraft has exactly one usable pass, so it can be served once.
R-LOW is priority 4 and declares it needs one; its pass is disjoint, so it
is served in full. Satisfaction is allocated over DECLARED need, so the
priority-1 request ends three times worse served than the priority-4 one,
and the inversion is real even though no slot was ever contested.

The gap is 1.0 - 1/3 = 0.666667 exactly, and the aggregate promise fails:
delivered satisfaction RISES with the priority number. No scheduler could
have avoided it, which is the useful content of the measurement. An
operations team reading only the schedule would see both requests served and
would not see that the urgent one got a third of what it asked for.

Contrast: a second instance where the two requests DO contest one antenna.

objective mode weighted_contacts: optimum 1.0000, contacts 1, inversions 0
objective mode weighted_seconds: optimum 900.0000, contacts 1, inversions 0

Here the higher priority wins under both objective modes: three priority-4
contacts at weight 0.125 sum to 0.375 against a single priority-1 contact's
1.0, and in weighted seconds they are 0.125 * 900 = 112.5 against
1.0 * 900 = 900. The declared geometric weights do what they say when the
antenna is contested. The inversion in the first instance comes from the
geometry and from differing declared need, not from the weights, and that
distinction is the reason both the per-pair inversion count and the
per-priority delivered satisfaction are reported.

5. The price of the declared fairness constraints
==============================================================================
Measured only on instances where the constrained program is feasible, which
is a minority, and the count is reported so the selection bias is visible.

The measured answer on this family is surprising and is published as measured:
the price is ZERO. Where the declared floors can be met at all, they are met
by an alternative optimal schedule rather than by giving up objective. The
reason is visible in the structure: the per-contact objective is indifferent
between two placements of the same request, so the solver has freedom to
satisfy a floor for free, and the instances that cost something are the ones
where the floors are infeasible and no schedule exists. A non-zero price needs
a specific structure, and one is constructed below rather than claimed.

5b. A constructed instance where the fairness floor DOES cost objective
==============================================================================
Three 100 s slots on one antenna. SC-1 sees all three, SC-2 only the first
two. R1 is priority 1, needs one contact and is allowed three; R2 is
priority 2 and declares it needs two. The free optimum gives R1 all three
slots and starves R2 entirely; the constrained optimum gives R2 the two slots
it needs and leaves R1 one.

  objective_unconstrained    3.000000
  objective_constrained      2.000000
  objective_loss             1.000000
  objective_loss_fraction    0.333333
  jain_gain                  0.500000
  max_min_gain               1.000000
  starved_before             1.000000
  starved_after              0.000000
  seconds_short_before       200.000000
  seconds_short_after        0.000000

The constraint costs exactly one unit of objective, one third of the free
optimum, and buys a max-min share that rises from 0 to 1. That trade is the
number a planner needs and the one this product exists to print.

5. What the candidate grid costs
==============================================================================
The exact optimum is exact over the discretised candidate grid and not over
continuous placement. Refining the grid can only add candidates, so the
optimum is non-decreasing; the table shows by how much, on full-size
instances at a twelve-hour horizon.

  seed  grid 600 s     300 s     120 s      60 s  cands @60  gain 600->60
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
     3      3.5000    3.5000    3.5000    3.5000        463       +0.0000
     7     18.5000   18.5000   18.5000   18.5000        517       +0.0000
    11     11.2500   11.2500   11.2500   11.2500        244       +0.0000
    19      6.7500    6.7500    6.7500    6.7500        265       +0.0000
    23     14.2500   14.2500   14.2500   14.2500        316       +0.0000

grid refinements where the optimum fell : 0

A finer grid cannot lower the optimum here, because 60 divides 120, 300 and
600, so every coarse candidate is also a fine candidate. The measured gain is
zero on all five instances: at a twelve-hour horizon with these pass lengths
the 600 s grid already reaches the same objective as the 60 s grid, at a
fraction of the candidate count. That is a result about these instances and
not a general statement; a request whose duration is close to its window
length is exactly the case where a coarse grid loses a placement, and
contactsched.problem.build_candidates adds a window-end-aligned candidate
specifically so that the loss is not an artefact of where the grid happened
to land.

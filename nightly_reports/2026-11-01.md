### Structural change made tonight, and the reason

The 2026-10-31 run already identified "push each product as it completes" as the
fix and wrote it into its checkpoint. It then dispatched five agents anyway and
died before push 3. The written policy was not the binding constraint; the
five-way fan-out was.

So tonight changes the *shape* of the night, not just the push policy:

- **No five-way fan-out.** Products are built in sequence, smallest blast radius
  first, and each one is pushed before the next is started.
- **The first push after the stub is a finished product**, not a checkpoint.
  Checkpoints have survived three nights running; products have survived none.
- **One published product tonight is the success condition.** Five built and
  lost is the failure that has already happened twice. If the night ends with
  P081 alone on GitHub, that is strictly better than every run since 2026-10-29.

This is a deliberate deviation from the task prompt's batch-of-ten instruction
and from its five-concurrent-agent allowance. The prompt's own Rule Zero — "a
night that builds and cannot publish is worse than a night that does nothing" —
and its "push early and often, not once at the end" are the authority for it.

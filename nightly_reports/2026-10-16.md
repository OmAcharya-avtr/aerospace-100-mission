### Three AI products, three baseline-wins results, all kept

This is the outcome the mission's §11 rule exists to protect, and it landed in
all three AI products in the same wave.

- **P067 AcqSearch.** Over 840 paired episodes on 105 held-out scenarios, the
  **Bayesian-greedy baseline and the fixed spiral pattern win jointly** and are
  indistinguishable from each other (z = 0.40). Both beat the learned policy at
  more than six standard errors: learned − Bayesian-greedy **+0.3359 s ±
  0.0446, z = +7.53**; learned − fixed spiral **+0.3455 s ± 0.0531, z = +6.51**.
  The ablation shows performance degrading monotonically in the freedom the
  model is given to disagree with the baseline, and at k=1 it reproduces the
  baseline.
- **P068 DegradePredict.** The comparison has **two winners and they are
  different models**, which is the point of the product. On complete
  trajectories the learned forecaster wins decisively (CRPS 27.05 d against
  90.74 d for the best baseline). On the **56.75 % of the test set that is
  right-censored, the exponential baseline wins** (CRPS 346.37 d against
  435.56 d; pinball 166.72 d against 212.57 d), on 3 of 3 independent seed
  pairs. Reporting a single pooled winner would have been false.
- **P069 ScenarioCov.** The learned gap finder is **not separated** from greedy
  max-min (+0.004204, sd 0.015119, W/T/L 30/10/20 — the difference is 0.28 of
  its own standard deviation) and costs roughly 40× its wall clock. On the grid
  occupancy metric the learned model is **behind uniform random**
  (−0.001273, W/T/L 25/5/30). A deterministic coverage-greedy chooser beats all
  three on 60 of 60 seeds.

None of this was retuned away; each is pinned as a test assertion and stated in
the README and model card in the same terms.

## Failed tasks

None of the five products failed to deliver. Defects found *during* the builds
and reported rather than hidden are listed under Security and Blockers below;
the four worth naming here, because each would have shipped a wrong number:

1. **P068 generator event-flag inconsistency.** `event` was computed from the
   drawn forecast-origin age while `remaining_true_days` used the
   cadence-snapped origin, so the two fields disagreed for units whose
   follow-up fell between them. Every censored-coverage number in the product
   derives from those fields, so the headline result would have been silently
   wrong. Found by a test, fixed, pinned.
2. **P070 verdict instability at the dependency floor.** Hypothesis falsified a
   verdict-stability property at `P=100000.0, J=86413.50000000362, k=44, Q=1.0`
   — two summation orders of the latency equation differ by exactly one ulp at
   8.4e6 ms, wider than the 1e-9 ms absolute tolerance, flipping the verdict.
   **Found only because both ends of the declared range were actually run**;
   the ceiling passed it all build. The comparison band is now scale-aware.
3. **P067 README numbers were wrong until the final re-run.** Three stale
   figures were caught by re-executing every quoted command as the last step,
   including a wall clock quoted as 123.7 s in three documents when the
   committed output says 103.3 s, and a model-card compute breakdown presenting
   two inferred times as measurements.

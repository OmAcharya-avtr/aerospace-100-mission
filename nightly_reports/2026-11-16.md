### The detection-power bound, which is the actual product

`P = 1 − (1−p)^n`, inverted: after **n** agreeing runs, the largest
intermittency rate consistent with a clean result at 95 per cent is
`1 − 0.05^(1/n)`.

| agreeing runs | rate that could still have escaped |
|---|---|
| 2 | **0.776** |
| 10 | 0.259 |
| 44 | 0.066 |
| 131 | 0.023 |

**131 repeats** are needed to detect a 1-in-44 defect with 95 per cent
probability. Run-twice-and-diff detects it with probability **0.0444**, derived,
and **0.0514** measured over 4 400 processes — the exact interval containing
the derived value. At an injected rate of **1.0**, pairwise power is **0** at
every audit size: when every run manifests, every run agrees.

### Two defects the coordinator found that the agent reported as clean

**A wrong statistic, and worse than wrong.** The published Spearman correlation
was computed with `np.argsort(np.argsort(x))` — **ordinal** ranks, which break
ties by position. With widths `{48, 64, 96}` over nine cells the five 64s took
five distinct ranks instead of five midranks. I computed the correct value two
ways before saying anything — my own midrank implementation and
`scipy.stats.spearmanr`, both **0.5809475019311126** against the published
**0.5333333333333333**.

The agent then found something worse than I had: the old expression was **not
well defined.** Because its arrays came from a `groupby().median()`, the rows
arrived in alphabetical order, giving 0.5333; in execution order **the same
expression on the same data gives 0.8499999999999999.** I verified both to every
digit. A rank statistic that changes with the row order of a groupby is not a
statistic. The midrank form is invariant at 0.5809475019311126 either way. The
**finding was unchanged in direction and strengthened in magnitude** — the
randomisation decorrelation claim needed below 0.2 and misses by more than
before.

**This pattern is mission-wide.** `argsort(argsort(x))` is used as a rank in
**five** products — P021, P046, P052, P084 and P094 — and in three of them it is
labelled "Spearman rank correlation" with no note about ties. P084 documents its
tie-breaking honestly. Four are published. **Not modified. NEEDS THE OWNER.**

### Where the coordinator was wrong and the agent was right

I diagnosed failure 1 as an **overflow**: `[3.0, 2.0] × 1e308` sends both values
to `inf`, they tie, the stable tie-break flips a descending input. The
counterexample is real and I verified it. **But it cannot have been the draw
that failed**: the file's strategies cap values and factors at `1e6`, which I
then confirmed by reading them, so overflow is unreachable there. The agent
found the route that is reachable — an ordinary **rounding collision** at
`[-811260.5389416472, 1000000.0, 999999.9999999999] × 292915.2677047961`, where
two values one ulp apart have products closer than half an ulp and round
together, with nothing overflowing and every value of ordinary magnitude. I
reproduced it: ordering `[0, 2, 1]` becomes `[0, 1, 2]`.

My *class* was right and the fix I specified covers it, because a distinctness
guard tests the condition rather than the mechanism. My *mechanism* was wrong.
Recording it because last night's lesson was that a coordinator which re-uses an
agent's number as its own cross-check has stopped cross-checking; the converse
is that a coordinator which dresses a plausible guess as a finding has done the
same thing in the other direction. On failure 2 I did better: I gave the agent
the hypothesis I had ruled out — 400 bootstrap trials showing the point median
fell outside its interval in **0 of 400** for four stages, so coarse
quantisation was not the cause — and let it find the mechanism. It did: a
**one-ulp round trip**. `point/ns_per_op × ns_per_op` returns
`0.46874999999999994` against `0.46875`, short by exactly
`5.551115123125783e-17`, which is `ulp(0.46875)`. I verified the arithmetic and
swept a plausible calibration band: negative in **7.25 per cent** of values
against the agent's 8.75 per cent and my observed one in six.

And it found a second instance of its own accord: the **spread** was also
computed through calibration-dependent values, so its last bits moved by one ulp
between runs. **The document's own claim that the spread was exactly
reproducible was an overclaim.** Both are now computed calibration-free, the
published value is unchanged at 1114.8633602088898, and the pins are
strengthened from approximate to exact.

### New operational hazard found, and it is a silent one

**`device_commit_files` wrote a stale cached copy when the same `stagedPath` was
reused.** The second transfer of the night reused
`/mnt/user-data/outputs/inc.bundle`. The container's copy was the new 3,520,296-byte
bundle with sha256 `5656d571…`; the tool returned `{"written":[...]}` with no
error; the Mac received 3,264 bytes with sha256 `6eddc5e5…` — the *previous*
bundle. The push then reported `Everything up-to-date` and would have been read
as success.

A run that trusts the tool's `written` acknowledgement and does not compare
hashes will believe it published and will have published nothing. This is the v4
failure mode wearing a different coat. **Mitigation, now mandatory: use a unique
filename for every transfer (`wave2-<sha>.bundle`, `fix-<sha>.bundle`) and always
compare sha256 on the Mac against the container before merging.** Every transfer
after the first did both; all four matched byte-for-byte.

### Cross-checks, where disagreement is the finding

**P036 RtClock vs P031 HilForge — AGREE.** Unchanged from the previous session:
176 direct overruns with identical index lists, 307 cascade overruns, longest run
16, from independent implementations.

**P039 LatencyNet vs P033 EdgeInfer — DISAGREE, and it is reported, not fixed.**
P033's nominated comparable statistic was `p50_s`.

| quantity | P039 | P033 | relative difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| injected end-to-end mean | 270.000000 us | 270.000000 us | 0.000e+00 |
| injected end-to-end sd | 42.190046 us | 42.190046 us | 0.000e+00 |
| **p50** | **267.184676 us** (sampled) | **282.050500 us** (measured) | **−5.2706 %** |

Outside P039's declared ±3 % band. P039 did not change its implementation to
match. **The cause is now established and it is a specification defect, not a
product defect:** the two p50 figures are not the same quantity. P033's is a
measured wall-clock median on a contended core; P039's is the median of the
sampled cost model with no clock in it. A sum of right-skewed lognormals has its
median 1.4546 % below its mean, while P033's measured p50 sits above the injected
mean by its measurement overhead. Accounting for both leaves roughly 0.4 %
unexplained, which is the size of n=2000 sampling noise on a median here.

Confirming evidence from this session: regenerating P033's JSON moved its
measured p50 from 283.8625 to 282.0505 us and moved the disagreement from
−5.8753 % to −5.2706 % **while every deterministic figure stayed bit-identical.**
The disagreement tracks P033's volatile measurement, which is exactly what the
explanation predicts.

**Action for the spec, not for the products:** a cross-check that compares a
measured wall-clock statistic against a distributional one cannot converge on a
shared host whose timings move by factors of 2–13. If future cross-checks want
numeric agreement rather than a finding, the comparable statistic must be the
*sampled* median of the declared cost model, which both implementations can
produce bit-identically from the documented sampler recipe.

**P037 FrameSync vs P010 BERBench — AGREE.** BPSK/AWGN uncoded, Eb/N0 9.00 dB,
n = 8936 bits, 20,000 frames, seed 20261005: FER converted from P010's BER

## Honest results that a baseline won, kept as found

These are research evidence and were not removed, softened or retuned away.

- **P039 LatencyNet: the learned model loses.** Where stages are independent it is beaten by the analytic sum-of-stages baseline at p99 (0.05167 vs 0.03655, paired p=0.0009 *against* it). Worse for the product's premise, **the plain linear-regression baseline wins all four regime/target comparisons and the learned model loses to it in 12 of 12 capacity-sweep settings.** A covariance-aware analytic variant closes most of the correlated-stage gap on its own (0.15441 → 0.03317 at p99), so much of what the learned model "learns" is free by measuring a covariance. Published as "on this dataset a learned tail predictor is not justified."
- **P034 FaultInject: no measurable advantage over the non-learned ablation.** The learned prioritizer beats uniform random clearly (33.875 vs 16.583), but sits *inside* the interval of a kind-mean ablation that does no learning (33.875 in [32.792, 35.750]). Essentially all the gain is knowing which fault *kind* is dangerous. Coverage-greedy also shows no measurable advantage over random at finding severe faults (paired difference 0.125 [−0.292, 0.543], contains zero). The learned strategy reaches the *lowest* coverage of the four, published as a failure case.
- **P035 TelemetryOOL: the detector does not beat the mature alternatives, and the README says so.** The shipped Isolation Forest **loses on every scenario**, scoring below chance on decorrelation (AUC 0.4253), and was left in every table rather than deleted. The Gaussian mixture's margin over a classical Hotelling T²/Q monitor is inside Monte-Carlo error. CUSUM wins outright wherever the anomaly is univariate. The defensible claim is the operational OOL semantics and the matched-false-alarm-rate harness, not a better detector.
- **P040 BitFlipSim: the winner depends on protection granularity and no single method wins.** The learned predictor beats the exponent-bit heuristic by 12.2 % per protected byte on the idealised bit-granular cost model — but on the word-granular model that real ECC/TMR actually charges, the heuristic collapses to exactly the random-selection expectation (0.220135 at every budget, carrying no word-level information) and the plain magnitude baseline beats the learned model at most budgets. Also published: the heuristic costs zero injections and zero training and still reaches 87.5 % of the oracle.
- **P035 reports a published approximation failing its own stated band:** Siegmund's closed form deviates from the Brook-Evans chain by 6.17e-02 at δ=3 against a 2 % band stated *before* measurement. The band was not widened.
- **P037 reports one check as NOT VERIFIED rather than closing it.** The measured RS coding gain is 3.2941 dB (BER metric) / 5.8207 dB (FER metric), but CCSDS 130.1-G was unreachable from the container, so no published range is quoted and the comparison is left open with `"reference_range_status": "NOT_VERIFIED_NO_DOCUMENT_ACCESS"`. Quoting a range from memory would have been an invented citation. A reviewer with the document can close it.

## Carry integrity - a two-byte transcription corruption, caught and localised

The report and checkpoint were carried to the Mac as a gzipped tar, base64
encoded, appended through osascript in four 2500-character chunks. The first md5
comparison FAILED: the decoded archive hashed f21327844c075a000a4e748bc6bb7f3d
against the container 3f8c79465bc5bbc9aa3cf4f562b4a1f2, while the base64 text
matched the container in length, 9788 bytes, exactly.

Per-chunk md5 localised it to chunk 4 of 4. Prefix md5 bisection inside that
chunk localised it to a single substituted byte at offset 1820, an R written as
an h. The first patch attempt wrote the correct byte at the wrong offset, 1804,
which corrupted a second byte and was caught by the same bisection. Both were
then fixed and both hashes matched the container exactly before extraction, and
the two extracted files were md5-compared against the container individually.

Lesson, recorded here for the next session: VERIFY A HAND-CARRIED PAYLOAD WITH A
HASH, LOCALISE A MISMATCH BY PREFIX BISECTION, AND RE-BISECT AFTER PATCHING - a
byte written at the wrong offset leaves both the length and the symptom
unchanged, so a second failed comparison looks like the first.

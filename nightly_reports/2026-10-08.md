**P052 FalsifyLoop** — benchmark 8 instances × 5 strategies × 30 seeds, budget 100:
- Latin hypercube **never beats uniform random on any of the eight instances** (aggregate 0.5229 vs 0.6130).
- Cross-entropy **loses on all eight**, resolvably on six (aggregate 0.2955). On `rate-envelope` it found nothing in 30 runs of 100 simulations; best robustness across all 3000 simulations was +0.0213, never once negative.
- Simulated annealing loses resolvably on all four easy-to-moderate instances and wins resolvably on only the single hardest.
- The surrogate — the AI component — wins aggregate 0.8016 vs 0.6130 **but its advantage is confined to the hard instances**, with one point-estimate loss on `settling-band`, and on `overshoot-loose` all 30 of its violations came during its unlearned Latin-hypercube warm start.
- **14 of 32 per-instance comparisons are statistically undecided at 30 seeds**, and re-running at a different base seed flips the ledger. Both tables are published.
- 7.2 % of counterexamples (22 of 304) are discretisation artefacts that flip to satisfying at dt/4.
- The surrogate's uncertainty is **not calibrated**: 0.9356 coverage at 95 % nominal but 0.7631 at 68 % nominal.
- The percentile bootstrap band is degenerate — zero width — wherever all runs agree, in 11 of 40 cases, so it cannot be read as coverage at a curve's floor or ceiling.

**P053 TwinInvalidate**:
- **CUSUM beats the windowed GLR on all three change types** (62.3 vs 75.7 step; 232.5 vs 258.7 ramp; 211.0 vs 262.3 variance), contradicting the batch spec's own expectation that GLR would be hard to beat. Structural reason published.
- **The learned classifier loses to CUSUM, 2 of 3 in distribution and 3 of 4 out of it.** On a gain change of equal magnitude and opposite sign it detects 2 % of runs where every baseline detects 100 %. Not retuned. The README and MODEL_CARD recommend using the CUSUM instead of the model.
- The **correctly-specified** variance CUSUM is the *slowest* on the variance change (466.6 vs the mis-specified mean CUSUM's 211.0) — a measured counterexample to "use the right statistic".
- Noise-variance change is where every method does badly, 3.08–3.47× the step delay, with an exact reason.
- The classifier's confidence is quantised so coarsely it **cannot be operated at the baselines' false-alarm rate at all** (achievable ARL0s bracket the target 1.73× apart). Both brackets reported.
- Fixing an accidental seed overlap **moved the result against the learned model** (step win 4 % → 2 % loss; variance +84 % → +122 %) and the earlier, more favourable numbers were discarded.

**P054 RareVerify**:
- The learned surrogate **has nothing to win on the smooth analytic limit state** — its recovered tilt sits 5.890e-06 standard-normal units from the analytic one, i.e. it rediscovers the closed form at a deterministic 0.167 % variance penalty plus seconds of fit time against a closed form costing nothing. Structural, no retune attempted.
- It does win on the rough limit state (variance ratio 10.6563) but **falls 31 % short of the oracle tilt**.
- **26 of 40 swept importance-sampling tilts are worse than plain Monte Carlo.** Wrong-direction tilt: 1.0e+05× worse. At scale −1, every one of 40 replications returned exactly 0.
- **The build fell into its own trap and published it:** the first sweep labelled an over-tilted sampler "better" at VRF 1.836e+08 while its estimate was 2.07e5× too small, with a matching tiny standard error. Fixed by making a mean-squared-error reduction factor the verdict, and pinned by a regression test.
- Subset simulation's own standard error **understates its measured spread by a mean factor of 2.69**; intervals built on it are labelled LOWER BOUND.
- Wilson is not always narrower than Clopper-Pearson — the ordering reverses at exactly n = 46 — and no test asserts containment.
- The sample-efficiency curve is flat: 25 training evaluations suffice, so there is no trade-off to report, said plainly.

**P055 AssureGraph** — the headline number undercuts the product's own check, deliberately:
- **Staleness discrimination = 0.000.** Twenty meaning-inverting edits and twenty meaning-preserving edits are *both* reported stale, 20 of 20. Content-hash staleness detects that an artifact changed, never whether the change matters.
- A case can pass all six checks with every Solution citing an **empty file** whose digest was recorded from that empty file. The library prints that sentence itself with every COMPLETE verdict, not only in the README.

**P059 InvariantSet** — the numerical pathologies *are* the product:
- **A tolerance change alters the answer.** `redundancy_tol` ≤ 3e-3 converges in 5 iterations; 4e-3 and above **never converges**. The threshold was bisected to bracket the final per-iteration shrink margin 3.631987773647e-03 to ten figures. Mechanism stated: the tolerance must sit below the smallest shrink margin, which decays geometrically, so **no fixed default is safe**.
- A loose tolerance returns a **wrong, non-invariant** set: +9.2459 % area with the independent check failing at +4.158156e-02.
- **The strict invariance check reads False on correct maximal sets** (+2.220446e-16), because a maximal set has exactly zero margin and floating point lands on either side. Reported, not tuned away; the default tolerance stays 0.
- Two shipped systems **cannot converge** (per-iteration shrink ratio up to 1.000000000); four different caps return four different outer bounds, and the CLI exits 2 with "OUTER bound on S_inf, not S_inf".
- **Pontryagin difference is not the inverse of Minkowski sum**, tested as the identity that actually holds. The assumed equality is FALSE with a measured 8.000000 % deficit, and the reason it is widely believed was measured too: most random polytopes are box-dominated, so the random-pair deficit median is 0.0000 %.

## New environment defects found tonight — propagate

- **`numpy.polynomial.hermite.hermgauss(n)` returns NaN weights for n ≥ 400 on numpy 2.5.3** (finite at 200 and 300), silently. Use `scipy.special.roots_hermite`, finite to ≥ 3200 nodes. This *refines* the carried note, which said "above ~350 nodes".
- **`WhiteKernel` with a zero lower bound makes `GaussianProcessRegressor` emit negative posterior variances on sklearn 1.9.1**, corrupting any uncertainty output. Bound it to `1e-8·var(g)`.
- **`scipy.spatial.qhull` is deprecated** on the installed SciPy; import `QhullError` from `scipy.spatial`.
- **`np.linalg.det` emits divide-by-zero RuntimeWarnings** on badly scaled small matrices; an SVD rank test is quieter and faster (one suite went 83 s → 58 s).
- **Wall-clock figures in this container move 10–40 %**, not the 10–20 % the brief states. Quote a ratio or a measured range, never a single-run µs figure in prose.
- **`api.crossref.org` is blocked by the container's egress proxy** (`connect_rejected`); verify citations from publisher-hosted PDFs or DOI landing pages.
- **PyPI name traps:** `gsn` is *Global Sensor Networks* (EPFL), not Goal Structuring Notation, and `advocate` is an SSRF library, not NASA's AdvoCATE. There is no established GSN package on PyPI.
- **Mermaid reserves `graph` as a node id** in `flowchart` blocks, so a README architecture diagram with a module named `graph` fails to parse.
- Confirmed again: `n_jobs>1` cripples single-row forest inference — measured 7.9–8.9× slower tonight, worse than the 5.7× carried forward.
